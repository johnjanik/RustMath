## 2. Mathematics

### 2.1 Setting

Let `A` be the Jacobian of a curve of genus `g` over `F_q`, `q` odd (here `q = p`; after a base
change `q = p^k`). Let `T = T_2(A)`, a free `Z_2`-module of rank `2g`, and `F` the `q`-power
Frobenius endomorphism acting on it. Its characteristic polynomial on `T` is the Frobenius
polynomial `P` (Weil; J. Tate, *Endomorphisms of abelian varieties over finite fields*, Invent.
Math. 2 (1966), Thm. 1 and its proof, `l = 2 != p`). Write `alpha_1..alpha_2g` for its roots;
`|alpha_i| = sqrt q`.

`J = A[2](F_q-bar) = T / 2T`, with `F` acting `F_2`-linearly; its characteristic polynomial is
`Pbar = P mod 2`. As an `F_2[x]`-module (`x` acting as `F`),

    J = sum over irreducible f | Pbar of J_f,   J_f = sum_i F_2[x] / (f^lambda_{f,i}),

with `lambda_f` a partition of `e_f`, the multiplicity of `f` in `Pbar` (structure theorem over
the PID `F_2[x]`). The tuple `lambda = (lambda_f)_f` is the **type** of `J`. `f = x` never occurs
(`P(0) = q^g` is odd). For each `f` put `d_f = deg f`, `o_f` = the order of `x` modulo `f` (odd,
it divides `2^d_f - 1`), `f*` = the reciprocal `x^d_f f(1/x)` (monic, since `f(0) = 1`).
`S = lcm_f o_f` is the semisimple order, `e_max = max e_f`, `c(a) = ceil(log2 a)`, and
`N_max = S * 2^c(e_max)` (the bound `divides` of today's `TwoTorsionOrder::Unresolved`).

For `k >= 1`, `ker(F^k - 1 | J) = A(F_{q^k})[2]`: `F^k` fixes exactly the `F_{q^k}`-points. So
`r_k = dim ker(F^k - 1 | J)` is the 2-rank of `A(F_{q^k})`. Also

    #A(F_{q^k})   = det(1 - F^k | T) = prod (1 - alpha_i^k) = Res(P, x^k - 1),
    #A'_k(F_{q^k}) = det(1 + F^k | T) = prod (1 + alpha_i^k) = Res(P, x^k + 1),

where `A'_k` is the quadratic twist of `A` over `F_{q^k}` (Frobenius `-F^k`; same `T`).
`Res(P, h) = prod h(alpha_i)` for monic `P`, and the signs vanish because `deg P = 2g` is even.
Both are `P^(k)(1)` and `P^(k)(-1)`, `P^(k) = prod (T - alpha_i^k)` the Frobenius polynomial over
`F_{q^k}` (MAGMA `LPolynomial(C, k)` up to reversal). Neither is 0, since `|alpha_i^k| != 1`.

### 2.2 Lemma A (rank bound for any lift)

**Lemma A.** Let `g` in `Z[x]` with `Res(P, g) != 0`. Then
`dim_F2 ker(gbar(F) | J) <= v_2(Res(P, g))`.

*Proof.* `g(F)` is an endomorphism of `T` with `det g(F) = prod g(alpha_i) = Res(P, g) != 0`.
By the Smith normal form over the PID `Z_2`, `C = T / g(F) T` is a finite `Z_2`-module of order
`2^v`, `v = v_2(det g(F))`, and `C = sum_{i=1..d} Z/2^{a_i}` with every `a_i >= 1`. Then
`C / 2C = T / (g(F)T + 2T) = J / gbar(F) J` has dimension `d`, and
`d = dim J - rank gbar(F) = dim ker gbar(F)`. Finally `v = sum a_i >= d`. QED.

**Corollary.** For every `k >= 1`: `r_k <= v_2(#A(F_{q^k}))` (the rule of REQUESTS 1.2, which
is the case `k = 1`: the 2-rank of a finite abelian group is at most the 2-adic valuation of its
order) and `r_k <= v_2(#A'_k(F_{q^k}))` (the same rule for the quadratic twist, since
`x^k + 1 = x^k - 1` over `F_2`).

### 2.3 Kernel dimensions, the order, and which k

**Lemma B.** On `M = F_2[x] / (f^a)` (`f` irreducible, `f != x`), multiplication by `h` has a
kernel of dimension `d_f * min(a, v_f(h))`, where `v_f(h)` is the multiplicity of `f` in `h`.
*Proof.* `M` is uniserial with submodules `f^j M`, `dim f^j M = d_f (a - j)`. The kernel of `h`
is `f^{a - min(a, v_f(h))} M`, because `h = f^{v_f(h)} u` with `u` a unit of `M`. QED.

**Lemma C.** Write `k = 2^s k'` with `k'` odd. Over `F_2`, `x^k - 1 = (x^{k'} - 1)^{2^s}` and
`x^{k'} - 1` is squarefree (its derivative `k' x^{k'-1}` is prime to it). So
`v_f(x^k - 1) = 2^s` if `o_f | k'` and `0` otherwise (`f | x^{k'} - 1` iff `x^{k'} = 1` mod `f`).
Hence, for a type `lambda`,

    r_k(lambda) = sum over f with o_f | k' of d_f * sum_i min(2^s, lambda_{f,i}).

**Lemma D (order).** `F` has order `ord(lambda) = lcm_f o_f * 2^c(max lambda_f)` on a module of
type `lambda`. *Proof.* `x^n = 1` on `F_2[x]/(f^a)` iff `f^a | x^n - 1` iff (Lemma C) `o_f | n'`
and `2^{s(n)} >= a`; the least such `n` is `o_f 2^c(a)` (`o_f` is odd). A direct sum has the lcm
of the orders. QED. In particular every possible order divides `N_max`.

**Lemma E (which k).** Among the bounds `x^k - 1` and `x^k + 1` for all `k >= 1`, those with
`k | N_max` imply the others. *Proof.* Let `k = 2^s k'`, `s_max = c(e_max)`.

- If `s <= s_max`, put `kt = 2^s gcd(k', S)`, a divisor of `N_max`. The set
  `{f : o_f | k'}` equals `{f : o_f | gcd(k', S)}`, so `r_k(lambda) = r_kt(lambda)` for every
  type. `kt | k` gives `#A(F_{q^kt}) | #A(F_{q^k})` (a subgroup), and `k / kt = k' / gcd(k', S)`
  is odd, so `x^kt + 1` divides `x^k + 1` in `Z[x]` and `Res(P, x^kt + 1)` divides
  `Res(P, x^k + 1)`. The bound at `kt` is the stronger one.
- If `s > s_max`, every part of every `lambda_f` is at most `2^s`, so
  `r_k(lambda) = sum_{o_f | k'} d_f e_f` is the same for every type. That bound keeps all types or
  none, and the true type satisfies it (Lemma A), so it keeps all. QED.

So the bounds used are `r_k(lambda) <= v_2(Res(P, x^k - 1))` and
`r_k(lambda) <= v_2(Res(P, x^k + 1))` for each `k | N_max`. When `Pbar` is squarefree there is a
single type and no bound is computed.

### 2.4 Symplectic constraints

The Weil pairing `e: J x J -> mu_2 = F_2` of the principal polarization is nondegenerate and
alternating (D. Mumford, *Abelian varieties*, 1970, Sections 20 and 23; any polarization of odd
degree would do). The polarization is defined over `F_q`, so `e(Fx, Fy) = e(x, y)^q = e(x, y)`:
`F` lies in `Sp(J)`, and `e(Fx, y) = e(x, F^{-1} y)`.

**S0 (orthogonality).** `J_f` is orthogonal to `J_h` unless `h = f*`. *Proof.* For `x` in `J_f`
take `N` with `f(F)^N x = 0`. For `y` in `J_h`, `0 = e(f(F)^N x, y) = e(x, f(F^{-1})^N y)` and
`f(F^{-1}) = F^{-d_f} f*(F)`, which is invertible on `J_h` when `h != f*`. QED. So `J_f` is
nondegenerate when `f = f*`, and when `f != f*` the spaces `J_f`, `J_{f*}` are isotropic and in
perfect duality.

**S1.** `lambda_f = lambda_{f*}`. *Proof.* For `f != f*`, `y -> e(., y)` is an isomorphism of
`J_{f*}` onto `Hom(J_f, F_2)`, and it carries `F` to `phi -> phi o F^{-1}`, the transpose of
`F^{-1}` on `J_f`. A transpose has the type of the map, and `F^{-1}` acts on
`F_2[x]/(f^a)` as a cyclic operator with characteristic polynomial `(f*)^a`. So the `f*`-type of
`J_{f*}` is the `f`-type of `J_f`. For `f = f*` there is nothing to prove. QED.

**S2.** For `f = x + 1`, every odd part of `lambda_{x+1}` has even multiplicity. This is the
parity condition for unipotent elements of symplectic groups, which holds in characteristic 2 as
well (it is part of the classification of W. H. Hesselink, *Nilpotency in classical groups over a
field of characteristic 2*, Math. Z. 166 (1979) 165-181; see also M. W. Liebeck and G. M. Seitz,
*Unipotent and nilpotent classes in simple algebraic groups and Lie algebras*, AMS Surveys and
Monographs 180, 2012). The proof below is complete, so no citation carries weight.

*Proof.* By S0, `V = J_{x+1}` is nondegenerate and `u = F|V` is unipotent in `Sp(V)`. Let
`N = u + 1` (characteristic 2), `K_j = ker N^j`. From `e(ux, uy) = e(x, y)`:
`e(Nx, y) = e(ux, y) + e(ux, uy) = e(ux, Ny) = e(x, u^{-1} N y)`. So the adjoint of `N` is
`N* = cN` with `c = u^{-1} = 1 + N + N^2 + ...`, a unit commuting with `N`. For `j >= 1` define
`b_j(x, y) = e(x, N^{j-1} y)` on `K_j`.

1. Symmetric: `b_j(y, x) = e(N^{j-1} x, y) = e(x, c^{j-1} N^{j-1} y) = b_j(x, y)`, since
   `c^{j-1} = 1 + N(...)` and `N^j y = 0` (and `e` is symmetric in characteristic 2).
2. Alternating when `j` is odd, `j - 1 = 2i`:
   `b_j(x, x) = e(x, N^{2i} x) = e(c^i N^i x, N^i x) = e(N^i x, N^i x) + e(N^{i+1} z, N^i x)`
   for some `z`; the first term is 0 (`e` alternating) and the second is
   `e(z, c^{i+1} N^{2i+1} x) = 0`.
3. Radical: `x` in `K_j` is in the radical iff `c^{j-1} N^{j-1} x` lies in
   `K_j^perp = Im (N*)^j = Im N^j`, iff `N^{j-1} x` lies in `Im N^j`, iff `x` lies in
   `K_{j-1} + N V`. Intersected with `K_j` this is `K_{j-1} + N K_{j+1}` (modular law, and
   `K_j` meets `NV` in `N K_{j+1}`).
4. `dim K_j / (K_{j-1} + N K_{j+1})` is the number of Jordan blocks of size exactly `j`
   (`dim K_j - dim K_{j-1}` blocks of size `>= j`, of which `dim (K_{j-1} + N K_{j+1}) / K_{j-1}`
   have size `>= j + 1`).

So for odd `j` the blocks of size `j` are counted by the dimension of a space with a
nondegenerate alternating form: an even number. QED.

On the 54 rows S1 never applies (no `f != f*` with `e_f >= 2`) and S2 removes types (`3 1`, `7 1`,
`5 3`, `5 2 1`, ...) but never changes an order or a W type (prototype, Section 3.1). It is kept
because it is proved, cheap, and makes the reported lists of types at 1 shorter.

### 2.5 The Golay Lagrangian W

**Trusted hypothesis H_W.** `J` has an `F`-stable Lagrangian subspace `W` (dimension `g`,
isotropic) on which `F` has characteristic polynomial `Q` or `Q^rev`, with `Q Q^rev = Pbar`
(HJLPPZ; the owner's paper, Theorem 4.8 and Corollary 4.4; it rests on Gal = M23 and the branch
data `(2A, 23A, 23B)`, so it applies to X and to the six covers over L). The rule for `J` does not
use H_W; only the W columns do, and the JSON records H_W in every row.

**Lemma F.** Write `W_f = W ∩ J_f` (an `F`-stable subspace splits along the primary components),
`mu_f` its type, `m_f = mult_f(Q)`. Then `J_f / W_f` has type `mu_f` if `f = f*` and type
`mu_{f*}` (as an `f`-primary module) if `f != f*`. *Proof.* If `f = f*`, `J_f` is nondegenerate
and orthogonal to the other components (S0), and `W = W^perp` gives
`W_f = W_f^perp ∩ J_f`: `W_f` is Lagrangian in `J_f`. Then `x -> e(x, .)|W_f` maps `J_f` onto
`Hom(W_f, F_2)` with kernel `W_f`, and carries `F` to the transpose of `F^{-1}` on `W_f`, whose
`f*`-type, here the `f`-type, is `mu_f` (as in S1). If `f != f*`,
`W_f = W^perp ∩ J_f = Ann_{J_f}(W_{f*})`, and the perfect pairing `J_f x J_{f*}` gives
`J_f / W_f = Hom(W_{f*}, F_2)` with the transpose of `F^{-1}`, of `f`-type `mu_{f*}`. QED.

**Theorem (Green, Klein).** A finite module of type `lambda` over a discrete valuation ring with
finite residue field has a submodule of type `mu` with quotient of type `nu` iff the
Littlewood-Richardson coefficient `c^lambda_{mu,nu}` is nonzero. (J. A. Green, *The characters of
the finite general linear groups*, Trans. AMS 80 (1955); T. Klein, *The multiplication of
Schur-functions and extensions of p-modules*, J. London Math. Soc. 43 (1968) 280-284;
I. G. Macdonald, *Symmetric functions and Hall polynomials*, 2nd ed., Ch. II, Section 4, (4.3):
the Hall polynomial `g^lambda_{mu,nu}(t)` vanishes unless `c^lambda_{mu,nu} != 0`.) The
`f`-primary `F_2[x]`-modules are modules over the completion of `F_2[x]` at `f`, a DVR with
residue field `F_{2^d_f}`. Only the direction "submodule exists implies `c != 0`" is used.

**Consequence.** The W types are the tuples `(mu_f)` with `mu_f` a partition of `m_f` such that
some candidate type `lambda` of `J` has `c^{lambda_f}_{mu_f, mu_{f*}} != 0` for every `f`
(`mu_{f*} = mu_f` when `f = f*`). At the eigenvalue 1: `mu = mu_{x+1}` is a partition of
`m = mult_{x+1}(Q)` with `c^{lambda_{x+1}}_{mu,mu} != 0`. Two special cases agree with REQUESTS
1.2: `lambda_{x+1} = (2m)` forces `mu = (m)` (the coefficient of `s_(2m)` in `s_mu s_mu` is 1
only for a row), and `lambda_{x+1} = (1^{2m})` forces `mu = (1^m)`.

The direction used was checked directly for the case that occurs (`d_f = 1`, `|lambda| <= 8`):
for every `lambda` of 2, 4, 6, 8 the subspaces `U` of dimension `|lambda|/2` stable under a
nilpotent of type `lambda` over `F_2` were enumerated, and the pairs (type of `U`, type of the
quotient) that occur are exactly those with `c != 0` (Section 3.3).

### 2.6 The decision rule and its soundness

**Candidates.** `C` is the set of types `lambda = (lambda_f)` with `lambda_f` a partition of
`e_f` that satisfy S1, S2 and, for every `k | N_max`, both bounds of 2.3.

**Theorem (soundness).** If `P` is the Frobenius polynomial of a principally polarized abelian
variety over `F_q`, `q` odd, the type of `J` lies in `C`. *Proof.* Lemmas A and C give the
bounds; S1, S2 hold by 2.4. QED.

**Decisions.** The order of `F` on `J` is Decided when `ord(lambda)` (Lemma D) is the same for
every `lambda` in `C`; otherwise it is Unresolved with the candidate set
`{ord(lambda) : lambda in C}`. The type at 1 (`lambda_{x+1}`), the W types (2.5) and the class of
`F` on `W` are decided in the same way: one value over all of `C`, else the list. An empty `C`
means `P` is not such a polynomial (or the input is wrong): an error, never a decision.

**What is decided.** Everything above is a statement about `P`. It is a statement about the
curve's `Jac[2]` only when `P` is certified, that is on a Decided row (Lemma 9.1). The M23 layer
prints `Decided` on a Decided row and `conditional on the counts` otherwise, as today. The genus
and H_W are trusted inputs and are recorded.

**Reason.** For a Decided order the reason is either "P mod 2 squarefree" (one type) or a
minimal set of bounds that decides on its own, found by dropping bounds from the last (twisted
after untwisted, larger `k` later) while the order stays decided. Every eliminated type with a
different order is excluded by that set.

### 2.7 The rule of REQUESTS 1.2 as a special case

If `x + 1 | Pbar` then `r_1 >= 1` (the generalized eigenspace for 1 is nonzero, so it has a
fixed vector), so `v_2(#A(F_p)) = 1` forces `r_1 = 1`: `lambda_{x+1} = (2m)`, one block. The rule
at `k = 1` therefore contains the owner's rule. Run with the bound `x - 1` alone, the prototype
gives on all 29 rows of X the owner's `v`, the owner's order wherever he decides it, and his sets
of cycle types on 8 letters and on 15 points (`data/a8_frobenius_predictions.json`, Section 4.3).

A remark: when `(x + 1)^2 | Pbar`, `v_2(P(1)) = 1` iff `v_2(P(-1)) = 1`, because
`P(1) - P(-1) = 2 * (sum of the odd coefficients)` and that sum is even (it is `Pbar'(1) = 0`
mod 2), so `P(1) = P(-1)` mod 4. On the 54 rows the two valuations agree whenever one is 1.

### 2.8 Constraints considered and not used

- **Other lifts in Lemma A.** `x - u` for odd `u` in `[-63, 63]`, monic quadratic, cubic and
  quartic lifts of small height, the cyclotomic polynomials `Phi_n` for `n | N_max`, and the 0/1
  lift of each repeated factor. All are valid. On the 54 rows they decide no order beyond the
  family `x^k -+ 1`, and they shorten one list of types at 1 (L at (127, 74)). Not used: no gain,
  and the family `x^k -+ 1` has a plain meaning (point counts of the Jacobian and its twists).
- **Maximal orders.** If `Z_2[x]/(P_f)` (`P_f` the 2-adic factor of `P` for `f`) is a discrete
  valuation ring, then `T_f` is free of rank 1 over it and `J_f = F_2[x]/(f^{e_f})` is cyclic.
  Dedekind's criterion (H. Cohen, *A course in computational algebraic number theory*, GTM 138,
  Thm. 6.1.4 and its proof) tests this on `Pbar` and one integer polynomial; for `f = x + 1` the
  test is exactly `v_2(P(1)) = 1`. For any `f` its conclusion is also given by Lemma A: in a DVR
  the lift `t_f(F)` is a uniformizer, so `v_2(Res(P, t_f)) = d_f` and Lemma A gives one part.
  Subsumed; not used.
- **The complete answer from P.** The possible `J` are the `I / 2I` for the `Z_2[F]`-stable
  lattices `I` of `Q_2[x]/(P)` up to isomorphism (ideal classes of the over-orders), cut further
  by the polarization. That is a finite computation but a large one. Out of scope.
- **A[4] and the 2-Sylow subgroups** of `A(F_{q^k})` would decide more but need more than `P`.

### 2.9 Degenerate cases (mathematics)

- Genus 0: `P = 1`, `J = 0`, one candidate (the empty type), order 1, Decided ("P mod 2
  squarefree"). Genus 1: `Pbar = x^2 + x + 1` (order 3) or `(x + 1)^2` (types `2` and `1^2`;
  `1^2` is excluded iff one of the bounds at `k = 1` is 1). The hyperelliptic test covers 784
  curves of genus 1.
- `x + 1` does not divide `Pbar`: no eigenvalue 1, the type at 1 is "none", `m = 0` and the W type
  at 1 is empty.
- `q` even: `T_2` is not the right object (`l = p`); refused.
- `P` not the Frobenius polynomial of a principally polarized abelian variety (a synthetic
  `P`): the theorem does not apply; the result is a statement about a hypothetical `A` and may be
  wrong. The API says so; an empty `C` is an error.

### 3.5 Soundness on hyperelliptic Jacobians

For `y^2 = f(x)` with `f` squarefree of degree `2g + 1` or `2g + 2` over `F_p`, `J[2]` is the
space of even subsets of the `2g + 2` branch points modulo the whole set, and Frobenius permutes
the branch points with the cycle type of `f mod p` (plus a fixed point at infinity when
`deg f` is odd). So the exact Frobenius module is known.

- `proto/gp/hyper_truth.gp`: 3098 curves (`p` from 3 to 47, `g` from 1 to 4, random `f`, seed
  `20261120`): `P` by `hyperellcharpoly`, the cycle type, the order of `F` on `J[2]` and
  `dim ker (F - 1)^j` from the matrix of the permutation on `J[2]`, all in PARI.
- `proto/hyper_check.py` computes the same truth independently in Python; the two agree on all
  3098 curves.
- The rule (all `k | N_max`, untwisted and twisted, with S1, S2): the true type at 1 and the true
  order are always among the candidates, and each of the 1468 Decided orders equals the true one.
  `k = 1` alone decides 1344, all `k` without twists 1435. The curves decided only beyond `k = 1`:
  30 of genus 2, 33 of genus 3, 61 of genus 4. Without S1, S2 the decisions are the same.

The hyperelliptic `J[2]` is a permutation module, so this family does not exercise every type;
it is a test of soundness, not of strength.

### 5.2 New methods of FrobeniusPolynomial

```rust
impl FrobeniusPolynomial {
    /// P^(k)(T) = prod (T - alpha_i^k): the Frobenius polynomial over F_{q^k} (MAGMA
    /// `LPolynomial(C, k)`, reversed). Power sums s_1..s_{2gk} of the alpha_i by Newton's
    /// identities, then those of alpha_i^k (s_{jk}), then e_j with exact division.
    /// Its `p()` is q^k. Panics if k == 0 or k > MAX_BASE_CHANGE_DEGREE (4096).
    pub fn base_change(&self, k: u32) -> FrobeniusPolynomial;
    /// #Jac(F_{q^k}) = P^(k)(1) = Res(P, x^k - 1) (MAGMA `ClassNumber` of the degree-k constant
    /// field extension). Same panics.
    pub fn jacobian_order(&self, k: u32) -> Integer;
    /// The order of the quadratic twist of Jac over F_{q^k}: P^(k)(-1) = Res(P, x^k + 1).
    pub fn twisted_jacobian_order(&self, k: u32) -> Integer;
    /// The analysis of Jac[2] (design C57 2.6). Err: see TwoTorsionError.
    pub fn two_torsion_analysis(&self) -> Result<TwoTorsionAnalysis, TwoTorsionError>;
    /// Unchanged signature; now `two_torsion_analysis().order()`, and on
    /// Err(TooManyCandidates) the bound of P mod 2 alone (as today). Same panics as today.
    pub fn two_torsion_frobenius_order(&self) -> TwoTorsionOrder;
}
```

The doc of `p()` changes from "the prime p" to "the size q of the base field (a prime for the
curves here; q^k after `base_change(k)`)". `from_coefficients` already accepts any `q >= 2`.

### 5.3 Types

```rust
/// The order of Frobenius on Jac[2] as far as P decides it. A statement about P: it is the
/// order on the curve's Jac[2] only when P is certified (unchanged doc).
pub enum TwoTorsionOrder {
    /// P mod 2 squarefree: one type; the lcm of the o_f (unchanged).
    DeterminedByP(u64),
    /// P mod 2 has a repeated factor and every candidate type has this order. `bounds` is a
    /// minimal decisive set (design 2.6).
    DeterminedByTwoRank { order: u64, bounds: Vec<RankBound> },
    /// Several orders remain. `semisimple_order` = S and `divides` = N_max as today;
    /// `candidates` = the possible orders, sorted (each a multiple of S dividing N_max).
    Unresolved { semisimple_order: u64, divides: u64, candidates: Vec<u64> },
}

/// One bound of Lemma A: dim ker(F^k - 1 | Jac[2]) <= v2, v2 = v_2(#Jac(F_{q^k})) (twisted =
/// false) or v_2 of the order of the quadratic twist over F_{q^k} (twisted = true).
#[derive(Clone, Copy, Debug, PartialEq, Eq, PartialOrd, Ord, Hash)]
pub struct RankBound { pub k: u64, pub twisted: bool, pub v2: u32 }
impl RankBound {
    /// "v2 #Jac(F_q^5) = 4", "v2 #Jac'(F_q^4) = 6" (F_q for k = 1).
    pub fn label(&self) -> String;
}

/// An irreducible factor f of P mod 2: ascending bits, multiplicity e_f, the order o_f of x
/// modulo f, and the index of f* in the list.
#[non_exhaustive]
pub struct PrimaryFactor { pub f: Vec<i64>, pub multiplicity: u32, pub order_of_x: u64, pub reciprocal: usize }

/// A type of Jac[2]: one partition (non-increasing) per factor, aligned with
/// TwoTorsionAnalysis::factors.
#[derive(Clone, Debug, PartialEq, Eq, PartialOrd, Ord, Hash)]
pub struct ModuleType(pub Vec<Vec<u32>>);

#[non_exhaustive]
pub struct TwoTorsionAnalysis {
    pub q: Integer,
    pub factors: Vec<PrimaryFactor>,   // the order of factor_mod(2)
    pub semisimple_order: u64,         // S
    pub max_order: u64,                // N_max
    pub bounds: Vec<RankBound>,        // every bound, k | N_max ascending, untwisted first;
                                       // empty when P mod 2 is squarefree
    pub candidates: Vec<ModuleType>,   // C of 2.6, sorted, never empty
    pub symplectic_removed: usize,     // types removed by S1, S2 before the bounds
}

impl TwoTorsionAnalysis {
    pub fn order_of(&self, t: &ModuleType) -> u64;             // Lemma D; panics on u64 overflow
    pub fn kernel_dimension(&self, t: &ModuleType, k: u64) -> usize;  // r_k (Lemma C)
    pub fn orders(&self) -> Vec<u64>;                          // sorted, distinct
    pub fn order(&self) -> TwoTorsionOrder;
    /// The types of Jac[2] at the eigenvalue 1 (lambda_{x+1}), distinct, sorted in reverse
    /// lexicographic order (8 before 6 2); None when x + 1 does not divide P mod 2.
    pub fn eigenvalue_one_types(&self) -> Option<Vec<Vec<u32>>>;
    /// The types of an F-stable Lagrangian on which F has characteristic polynomial w
    /// (ascending bits, degree g, w w^rev = P mod 2), by Lemma F and the LR criterion (design
    /// 2.5). Each entry lists, per factor f of w, its partition, and the indices of the
    /// candidates it is compatible with. Err(NotAReciprocalHalf) if w w^rev != P mod 2;
    /// Err(TooLarge) if some e_f > MAX_LR_SIZE (16).
    pub fn lagrangian_types(&self, w: &[i64]) -> Result<Vec<LagrangianType>, TwoTorsionError>;
}

#[non_exhaustive]
pub struct LagrangianType {
    pub parts: Vec<(Vec<i64>, Vec<u32>)>,   // (f | w, mu_f), the order of factor_mod
    pub compatible: Vec<usize>,             // indices into TwoTorsionAnalysis::candidates
}
impl LagrangianType {
    pub fn eigenvalue_one(&self) -> Vec<u32>;  // mu_{x+1}, empty if x + 1 does not divide w
    pub fn order(&self) -> u64;                // order of F on W (Lemma D on W)
    pub fn fixed_dimension(&self) -> usize;    // number of parts of mu_{x+1}
}

pub enum TwoTorsionError {
    EvenCharacteristic,                    // q even
    TooManyCandidates { count: u128 },     // the product of the partition counts > 10^6
    TooLarge(String),                      // N_max > MAX_BASE_CHANGE_DEGREE, or e_f > MAX_LR_SIZE
    NotAReciprocalHalf,                    // lagrangian_types: w w^rev != P mod 2
    Inconsistent,                          // no candidate survives: P is not the Frobenius
                                           // polynomial of a principally polarized abelian variety
}
```

### 5.4 Statuses, panics, limits

- Statuses: `TwoTorsionOrder` as above. The M23 layer maps `DeterminedByP` and
  `DeterminedByTwoRank` to `Decided` on a Decided row and to `conditional on the counts`
  otherwise, and `Unresolved` to `Unresolved: a or b` (Section 7).
- Panics (unchanged, documented as today): an irreducible factor of `P mod 2` of degree above 62
  (`order_of_x_mod2`), an order or `N_max` that does not fit in a `u64`
  (`ORDER_OVERFLOW`, message "does not fit in a u64"); new: `base_change` with `k = 0` or above
  `MAX_BASE_CHANGE_DEGREE`.
- Limits: candidates at most `10^6` (`TooManyCandidates`; genus 4 has at most 22); `N_max <= 4096`
  for the bounds (genus 4 with a repeated factor has `N_max <= 30`, by enumerating every palindromic
  `Pbar` of degree 8); `e_f <= 16` for
  `lagrangian_types`. `two_torsion_frobenius_order` never returns an error: on
  `TooManyCandidates` or `TooLarge` it returns `Unresolved` with the candidates
  `{S 2^t : 0 <= t <= c(e_max)}`, a true statement.
- Hypotheses (doc of `two_torsion_analysis`): `P` is the Frobenius polynomial of a principally
  polarized abelian variety over `F_q`, `q` odd (a Jacobian). For any other monic `P` with the
  functional equation the result is about a hypothetical variety.

## 10. Cost

- The analysis per row: `factor_mod(2)` (degree 8), the orders of `x` (degree <= 8), one
  `base_change` per `k | N_max` (on these rows `N_max <= 15`, at most 6 divisors, so at most 12
  bounds; power sums up to index `2gk <= 120`, integers of a few hundred bits), at most 22
  candidate types, and at most 25 Schur products of degree 8. Expected well under 10 ms in release; to be measured. The
  prototype does all 54 rows and 8 variants in 1.2 s of Python.
- The example: unchanged (the plane counts dominate; F-9 measured about 18 min for X to 131 and
  15 min for L to 127 on 4 threads under load). Writing the JSON after each row costs
  milliseconds.
- Build: one new crate in the graph (`rustmath-symmetricfunctions`, 7360 lines). The worktree's
  `target/` is empty, so the first build compiles everything (GMP through `rug`): several minutes
  with `-j 2`, a few GB of disk.

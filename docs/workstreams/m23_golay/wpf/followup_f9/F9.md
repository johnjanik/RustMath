## 2. The criterion and its proof

### 2.1 Setting

`F` is a bihomogeneous form of bidegree `(a, b)`, `a = dx >= 1`, `b = dy >= 1`, over `k = F_p`,
p odd. In the code `F = sum F[i][j] X^i Y^j` is the affine chart (`FormModP::coeffs`). Write
`c_i(Y)` for the coefficient of `X^i` (a binary form of degree b after homogenizing) and `r_j(X)`
for the coefficient of `Y^j`. The X-fibre over a closed point `v0` of the Y-line, of degree `d`
(`v0 ∈ F_(p^d)`, minimal polynomial g over `F_p`, or `v0 = infinity` with `d = 1`), is the binary
form `F(X, v0) = sum c_i(v0) X^i` of degree a over `F_(p^d)`. "Irreducible of full degree" means
`c_a(v0) != 0` and `F(X, v0)` irreducible in `F_(p^d)[X]`. Y-fibres are the same with X and Y
exchanged (`FormModP::transpose`).

Standard facts used.
- (F1) A factor of a bihomogeneous form is bihomogeneous. Proof: grade `k[X0, X1, Y0, Y1]` by
  `Z^2`, order bidegrees lexicographically; if `F = G H`, the products of the top components and of
  the bottom components of G and H are nonzero (a domain) and are the top and bottom components of
  F, which has one bidegree, so G and H have one bidegree each.
- (F2) The gcd of polynomials over `F_p` is the gcd over any extension (Euclid's algorithm runs in
  `F_p`).
- (F3) `F_(p^n)/F_p` is Galois with cyclic group generated by `sigma: x -> x^p`; `F_p` is perfect
  (R. Lidl, H. Niederreiter, *Finite fields*, Chapter 2).
- (F4) `f` of degree n over `F_q` is irreducible iff it is squarefree and its distinct-degree
  factorization has one block of degree n (J. von zur Gathen, J. Gerhard, *Modern computer
  algebra*, Algorithm 14.3; `residue.rs::factor_degrees` already implements it over `F_p[t]/(g)`).

### 2.2 Step 1: no factor in one variable (lines of the rulings)

Check: `gcd_i c_i(Y) = 1` and some `c_i` has degree b (else `Y0` divides every `c_i`: the line
`Y = infinity`); `gcd_j r_j(X) = 1` and some `r_j` has degree a (else the line `X = infinity`). A
failure exhibits a factor of F in one variable alone, so F is reducible (or not of the stated
bidegree). This is refused, not Unresolved: `Err(DegenerateModel(FibreComponent { place }))`, the
error `singular_locus` already gives for a horizontal line (`singular.rs:325`) and
`plane_point_count(.., FibresInY)` gives for a vertical one (`fibres.rs:109`). Place strings:
`"y-place g(y) = [..] (ascending)"`, `"y = infinity"`, `"x-place g(x) = [..] (ascending)"`,
`"x = infinity"`, with g the monic gcd. A bidegree with a zero entry is `Err(Shape(..))`
("bidegree (2, 0): both degrees must be positive"), the case (v).

Lemma 9.1's "F mod p has bidegree (a, b)" is the affine statement that `T0` and `V0` do not divide
`F mod p`, that is, no component at infinity. Step 1 checks it.

### 2.3 Step 2: an irreducible fibre gives irreducibility over `F_p`

**Claim.** If step 1 passes and the X-fibre over a closed point `v0` of degree d is irreducible of
full degree over `F_(p^d)`, then F is irreducible over `F_(p^d)`, hence over `F_p`. The same holds
for Y-fibres.

Proof. Suppose `F = G H` over `F_(p^d)` with G, H not constant. By (F1) they are bihomogeneous, of
bidegrees `(a1, b1)` and `(a2, b2)`, `a1 + a2 = a`. Restricted to the line `Y = v0` this gives
`F(X, v0) = G(X, v0) H(X, v0)`, binary forms of degrees `a1` and `a2`, both nonzero because their
product is. `F(X, v0)` is irreducible of degree a, so `a1 = 0` or `a2 = 0`, say `a1 = 0`. Then G is
a nonconstant form in Y alone dividing every `c_i`, so `gcd_i c_i != 1` over `F_(p^d)`, hence over
`F_p` by (F2) (or `Y0 | G`, the line at infinity). Step 1 excludes both. A factorization over `F_p`
is one over `F_(p^d)`, so F is irreducible over `F_p` too. QED.

This is the paper's argument ("a fibre F(T, v0) that is irreducible of degree 4"), with the
content condition made explicit; the owner's `curve.py` (`singular_locus`, the irreducibility
block) checks the same content and uses fibres over `F_(p^k)`, k = 1, 3, 5, 7.

### 2.4 Step 3: from `F_p` to `F_p-bar`

**Claim A.** Let F be irreducible over `F_p`. Over `F_p-bar`, `F = c G_0 G_1 ... G_(m-1)` with
`G_i = sigma^i(G_0)` pairwise non-associate and absolutely irreducible, `G_0` defined over
`F_(p^m)`, all of bidegree `(a/m, b/m)`. So `m | gcd(a, b)`.

Proof. The factorization over `F_p-bar` is defined over a finite field `F_(p^n)`. `sigma` permutes
the absolutely irreducible factors up to scalars. The product over one orbit, suitably scaled, is
`sigma`-stable, hence (F3) defined over `F_p`, and divides F; F is irreducible, so there is one
orbit. `sigma(F) = F`, so each factor appears with the same multiplicity e, and `F = c' H^e` with H
the orbit product, defined over `F_p`; irreducibility gives `e = 1`. The orbit of `G_0` has m
elements and its stabilizer is generated by `sigma^m`, so `sigma^m(G_0) = lambda G_0`; by Hilbert's
Theorem 90 `G_0` can be rescaled to be fixed by `sigma^m`, that is, defined over `F_(p^m)`. `sigma` preserves
bidegrees, so all `G_i` have the same bidegree `(a', b')` and `m a' = a`, `m b' = b`. QED.

**Claim B.** If moreover a fibre over a point of degree d is irreducible (step 2), then
`gcd(m, d) = 1`.

Proof. Over `F_(p^d)` the Galois group is generated by `tau = sigma^d`, which acts on the indices
`Z/m` by `+d`. Its orbits are `gcd(m, d)` in number, and the product over each is defined over
`F_(p^d)`. Step 2 says F is irreducible over `F_(p^d)`, so there is one orbit. QED.

So `m` divides `n_d`, the largest divisor of `n = gcd(a, b)` prime to d. **If `n_d = 1`, F is
absolutely irreducible.** This covers `gcd(a, b) = 1` (all M23 forms: (4, 23)) and, for example,
`gcd(a, b) = 2` with an irreducible fibre over a point of even degree.

**Claim C (smooth point).** If F is irreducible over `F_p` and `V(F)` has a smooth
`F_p`-rational point P, then `m = 1`.

Proof. P lies on some `G_j`. For a form H over `F_(p^m)` and P with coordinates in `F_p`,
`sigma(H)(P) = sigma(H(P))`, so `G_(j+1)(P) = sigma(G_j(P)) = 0`: P lies on every `G_i`. If
`m >= 2`, `F = G_0 R` with `G_0(P) = R(P) = 0`, so `grad F(P) = R(P) grad G_0(P) + G_0(P) grad R(P)
= 0` and P is singular. QED.

A smooth point is found by an exhaustive pass: for every `y0 ∈ F_p` the roots `x0 ∈ F_p` of
`F(X, y0)` (linear factors from `fp_factor`, sorted ascending) and the points with a coordinate at
infinity (the charts `reverse_x`, `reverse_y`), testing `(F_X, F_Y) != (0, 0)` in the chart. The
pass is complete over `P^1 x P^1 (F_p)`, so "no smooth point" is a fact, not a bounded search.

### 2.5 The other hypotheses of Lemma 9.1

- **Primitive F.** At p this is `F mod p != 0`: a unit content does not change `V(F)` over
  `Z_(p)`, and `FormModP::new` refuses the zero form (`ZeroForm`). Where the integral form is
  known it is checked literally: `BihomogeneousForm::reduce_primitive(p)` returns
  `Err(CountError::NotPrimitive { content })` unless the content is 1, and the M23 driver uses it.
  For a `FormModP` built directly the rest is the caller's responsibility, documented on
  `FormModP` and `certify`: it is the reduction of a primitive form of the same bidegree whose zero
  set over the generic fibre is birational to the curve of genus `genus`.
- **The L covers.** `calF` has coefficients in `Z[s]`; PARI gives `[O_L : Z[s]] = 1`
  (`f9/gp/lfield.out.txt`), so `(p, s - s0)` is a prime of `O_L` with residue field `F_p`, and
  HJLPPZ state in the header of `polynomial_calF.m` that the coefficients of calF generate the unit
  ideal of `O_L` (primitivity). Lemma 9.1 is stated over Q, but its proof uses only that the base
  is a discrete valuation ring with perfect residue field (with the uniformizer in place of p), so
  it applies at these primes, ramified ones included. This goes into the doc of `calf_mod` and the
  header of `certify.rs`.
- **Every delta invariant computed.** Unchanged: an unclassified point is `Unresolved`.
- **The genus.** Still a trusted input, recorded in the certificate.

### 2.6 Remark: with a correct genus the check never fails on a certifiable model

If the genus input g is the true genus and `F mod p` is reduced, the delta condition already forces
absolute irreducibility. This is step 5 of the WP-F design's Theorem 4.5: the normalization
`Y` of `V(F)` over `Z_(p)` has `f_* O_Y = Z_(p)` (Y is normal and the generic fibre is
geometrically integral), so by Zariski's connectedness theorem (R. Hartshorne, *Algebraic
geometry*, III.11.3; EGA III 4.3) `Y_p` is geometrically connected; the delta condition makes
`Y_p ⊗ F_p-bar` normal, hence smooth, hence irreducible; and the components of `Y_p` correspond to
those of `F mod p` (the map is an isomorphism at their generic points, where the local ring is a
discrete valuation ring with uniformizer p because `F mod p` is reduced). Reducedness holds
whenever `singular_locus` succeeds: a repeated factor involving X makes `D = 0`; one in Y alone
makes a fibre vanish.

Consequences. (a) On the M23 rows, where the genus 4 is right, the check can only fail if the
criterion is too weak, never because the reduction is reducible; the prototype shows it is strong
enough at every row. (b) The regression curves (i), (ii), (iii) are inconsistent inputs: no curve
over Q of genus 1 reduces to them along these models. The check turns such inputs (a wrong genus,
a form that is not the reduction of the curve) into a refusal instead of a wrong Decided. (c) The
certificate now matches Lemma 9.1 verbatim, whose proof does not need Zariski's theorem.

### 2.7 The search, its bounds, and when it says NotShown

```
absolute_irreducibility(f):
  bidegree (a, b); a = 0 or b = 0 -> Err(Shape)
  step 1 (line components)          -> Err(DegenerateModel(FibreComponent { place }))
  n = gcd(a, b); first = None
  for d in 1..=MAX_BASE_DEGREE (4):
    settles = (n_d == 1)            // n without the primes dividing d
    if first.is_some() and not settles: continue
    for ruling in [FibresInX, FibresInY]:
      for v0 in the first MAX_FIBRES_PER_DEGREE (64) closed points of degree d of the base line:
        if the fibre over v0 is irreducible of full degree over F_(p^d):
          if settles: return Absolute { fibre, Bidegree { gcd: n } }
          if first is None:
            if smooth_rational_point(f) = Some(P): return Absolute { fibre, SmoothRationalPoint(P) }
            first = Some(fibre)
          break                      // next d
  return NotShown(first.map_or(NoIrreducibleFibre, |w| NoSmoothRationalPoint { w, gcd: n }))
```

Order of the closed points: for `d = 1`, `0, 1, ..., p - 1`, then infinity; for `d >= 2`, the
monic irreducible g of degree d in the order of the code `sum g_i p^i` (irreducibility of g by
`fp_factor::factor_with_multiplicity`). The fibre over a point of degree d is evaluated in
`PolyField::new(p, g)` (`residue.rs:201`) with `eval_fp_poly` and tested by `factor_degrees`
(F4); over infinity, the reversed columns at 0.

NotShown happens:
- when F is reducible over `F_p` without a line component (curve (i)): no fibre can be
  irreducible, since a factorization `F = G H` with both factors of positive X-degree restricts
  to every fibre (the converse of step 2);
- when F is irreducible over `F_p` but not absolutely (curve (ii)): with `gcd(a, b) > 1`, every
  `F_p`-point is singular (Claim C) and every fibre over a point of degree divisible by m splits,
  so the search ends with `NoSmoothRationalPoint`;
- when F is absolutely irreducible but the monodromy of both rulings has no full cycle (for
  example a quartic fibration with group A4 or V4: no fibre over any finite field is irreducible),
  or the full cycles are rare enough to miss in 64 points per degree, or `gcd(a, b) > 1` with no
  smooth rational point and no fibre over a degree that settles n. None of these occur in any
  case of Section 3.

It never claims "reducible" except through step 1, which exhibits the factor. A search that finds
nothing returns NotShown (Unresolved), as the project rule requires.

### 2.8 Cost

Step 1: two gcd chains over `F_p` (dx + 1 polynomials of degree dy, dy + 1 of degree dx). Step 2:
at most `2 * 4 * 64 = 512` fibre tests, each a distinct-degree factorization of a polynomial of
degree dx (X-fibres) or dy (Y-fibres) over `F_(p^d)`: `O(n^3 d^2 log p^d)` operations for degree n.
Step 3: when `gcd(a, b) > 1`, one pass over `p + 1` fibres (root finding of degree dx) plus the
charts at infinity. The search stops at the first witness. Measured (debug build, prototype,
shared 4-core machine): M23 forms at `p >= 7`, 0.5 to 1.7 ms per form (the first `F_p`-rational
X-fibre, at most 17 tried); at p = 5, 18 to 20 ms (12 rational fibres fail, then a fibre over a
point of degree 2); 90 calls (G, G7, G13 at every prime 5..131) 155 ms in all; small (4,2) curves
0.2 to 2.6 ms; the regression curves (i) 324 ms and (ii) 230 ms (they exhaust the search). Release
was not measured; it is negligible beside the plane count (seconds to minutes per prime). The
worst case is dominated by Y-fibre tests when dy is large: at `dy = 4096` (the bound of
`FormModP`) a single test is slow, but it is reached only after every X-fibre test failed, and
`singular_locus` is already far from interactive there (`singular.rs:418`).

pub const MAX_BASE_DEGREE: usize = 4;
pub const MAX_FIBRES_PER_DEGREE: usize = 64;

/// A closed point of the base line of a fibration.
#[derive(Clone, Debug, PartialEq, Eq)]
pub enum BasePoint {
    Rational(u64),
    Infinity,
    /// The root of g, monic irreducible over F_p of degree >= 2 (ascending).
    Closed(Vec<u64>),
}

/// A fibre irreducible of full degree over the residue field of its base point.
#[derive(Clone, Debug, PartialEq, Eq)]
pub struct IrreducibleFibre {
    /// FibresInX: over a point of the Y-line, degree dx in X; FibresInY: the other ruling.
    pub fibration: Fibration,
    pub base: BasePoint,
}
impl IrreducibleFibre { pub fn residue_degree(&self) -> usize; }

#[derive(Clone, Debug, PartialEq, Eq)]
pub enum AbsoluteWitness {
    /// Every prime factor of gcd = gcd(dx, dy) divides the residue degree of the fibre
    /// (Claims A and B); in particular gcd = 1.
    Bidegree { gcd: usize },
    /// A smooth F_p-rational point (Claim C); None is infinity.
    SmoothRationalPoint { x: Option<u64>, y: Option<u64> },
}

#[derive(Clone, Debug, PartialEq, Eq)]
pub struct IrreducibilityEvidence {
    pub fibre: IrreducibleFibre,
    pub absolute: AbsoluteWitness,
}

#[derive(Clone, Debug, PartialEq, Eq)]
pub enum IrreducibilityGap {
    /// No fibre over a closed point of degree <= MAX_BASE_DEGREE (at most MAX_FIBRES_PER_DEGREE
    /// per ruling and degree) is irreducible of full degree.
    NoIrreducibleFibre,
    /// Irreducible over F_p (the fibre), gcd(dx, dy) = gcd > 1, no smooth F_p-point, and no
    /// irreducible fibre over a point whose degree settles gcd.
    NoSmoothRationalPoint { fibre: IrreducibleFibre, gcd: usize },
}

#[derive(Clone, Debug, PartialEq, Eq)]
pub enum Irreducibility {
    Absolute(IrreducibilityEvidence),
    NotShown(IrreducibilityGap),
}

/// Errors: Shape (a zero entry in the bidegree), DegenerateModel(FibreComponent) (a line of a

## 11. Open points for the reviewer

- Line components are refusals (`Err`) rather than `Unresolved`: consistent with the existing
  horizontal case and with "model degenerate at this prime", and model selection then tries the
  next model. The alternative is an `Unresolved::LineComponent`.
- The bounds 4 and 64: chosen so that the regression curves stay below a third of a second in
  debug while the M23 forms need at most 17 rational fibres or 4 points of degree 2. They are
  public constants; a curve that needs more gets `NotShown`, never a wrong answer.
- Removing `CountOptions::branch_rule` changes the API; the alternative (keep the option and allow
  Decided with assumed-unibranch counts) breaks the WP-F guarantee, so it is not proposed.

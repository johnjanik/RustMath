## Scope and overview

A reflection is a diagonalisable linear transformation of finite order whose space of fixed
points is a hyperplane. A reflection group is a finite-dimensional linear group over a field
F generated by a finite number of reflections.

There are no restrictions on the field F and no requirement for a reflection to have order
two. However, if F is a real field every reflection does have order two, and there is a much
richer theory — in particular, every Coxeter group is a real reflection group (Chapter 98).
The classification of finite real reflection groups is given in Section 95.6; the standard
references are **[Bou68]** (Chapters 4–6) and **[Hum90]**.

The chapter also covers finite complex reflection groups, including the full Shephard–Todd
classification **[ST54]**. There is a finite root system for every finite complex reflection
group, but no known analogue of simple roots; the theory of complex root data (generalising
root data to the complex setting) is used instead, following **[LT09]**. A diagram notation
extending the standard Coxeter–Dynkin conventions was introduced by Cohen **[Coh76]** and
is used in Magma with a minor variation (Cohen's EN₄ is called O₄ in Magma).

The chapter covers: construction of individual pseudo-reflections, transvections, and
reflections; construction of pseudo-reflection groups from root/coroot data; construction of
real reflection groups from Coxeter-theoretic data; construction of all finite complex
reflection groups (imprimitive family G(m,p,n) and 34 primitive groups); operations and
structural invariants of reflection groups; root/coroot/reflection access; weight lattices;
and conversion to related structures (Coxeter groups, Lie algebras, groups of Lie type).

Useful book references: **[LT09]**, **[Bro10]**, **[Kan01]** for complex reflection groups;
**[Bou68]** and **[Hum90]** for real reflection groups.

## 99.2 Construction of Pseudo-reflections

Let V be a vector space of dimension n over a field F. A pseudo-reflection in Magma (following
**[Bou68]**) is a linear transformation of V whose space of fixed points is a subspace of
dimension n − 1, i.e. a hyperplane. If r is a pseudo-reflection then dim(im(1 − r)) = 1; a
basis element of im(1 − r) is called a root of r. The coroot is the unique element φ ∈ V*
such that vr = v − φ(v)a; the determinant of r is 1 − φ(a). Special cases:
- φ(a) = 1: non-invertible (projection onto H);
- a ∈ H (i.e. φ(a) = 0): transvection;
- φ(a) ≠ 0, 1: reflection (finite order considered here).

In Magma both V and V* are identified with the space F^n of row vectors of length n; the
coroot b is represented as a row vector and the matrix of r is I − b^tr · a.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `PseudoReflection(a, b)` | Returns the matrix of the pseudo-reflection with root `a` and coroot `b`. | Direct formula I − b^tr · a. |
| `Transvection(a, b)` | Returns the matrix of the transvection with root `a` and coroot `b`. Input is checked to ensure `a` and `b` define a transvection. | As above; validity check. |
| `Reflection(a, b)` | Returns the matrix of the reflection with root `a` and coroot `b`. Input is checked to ensure `a` and `b` define a reflection. | As above; validity check. |
| `IsPseudoReflection(r)` | Returns `true` if `r` is the matrix of a pseudo-reflection; also returns a root and coroot. | Definition check via rank of 1 − r. |
| `IsTransvection(r)` | Returns `true` if `r` is the matrix of a transvection; also returns a root and coroot. | As above. |
| `IsReflection(r)` | Returns `true` if `r` is the matrix of a reflection; also returns a root and coroot. | As above. |
| `IsReflectionGroup(G)` | Returns `true` if G is a group generated by reflections. Note: not every generator need be a reflection. | Checks generators and closure. |

*Worked examples: H99E1 (creating a pseudo-reflection in GF(5)³ and verifying it is a transvection). H99E2 (matrix group of order 24 over a cyclotomic field generated by a reflection and a non-reflection; finding reflection generators via `IsReflectionGroup`). H99E3 (transvection classes in GL(3,25) and their normal closure SL(3,25)).*

### 99.2.1 Pseudo-reflections Preserving Reflexive Forms

Let J be the matrix of a non-degenerate reflexive bilinear or sesquilinear form β on V over
F, equipped with an automorphism σ with σ² = 1 (identity for symmetric/alternating forms,
complex conjugation for hermitian forms). If a is a root of a pseudo-reflection r that
preserves β, the coroot of r is ασ(a)J^tr for some α ∈ F, and the matrix of r is
I − αJ^tr σ(a)^tr a.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `SymplecticTransvection(a, alpha)` | The symplectic transvection with root `a` and multiplier α, with respect to the alternating form attached to the parent of `a`. Matrix: I − αJa^tr a. Error if form is not alternating. | Definition via alternating form; requires dim V even. |
| `UnitaryTransvection(a, alpha)` | The unitary transvection with root `a` and multiplier α, with respect to the hermitian form attached to the parent of `a`. Matrix: I − αJ̄a^tr a. Requires: `a` isotropic (aJā^tr = 0) and trace of α is 0 (α + ᾱ = 0). Error if form not hermitian, `a` not isotropic, or trace(α) ≠ 0. | Definition via hermitian form. |
| `UnitaryReflection(a, zeta)` | The unitary reflection with root `a` and determinant ζ (a root of unity), preserving the hermitian form of the ambient space of `a`; sends a ↦ ζa. Coroot: a∨ = ᾱa where α = (1 − ζ)/aJā^tr. | Definition via hermitian form; requires `a` non-isotropic. |
| `OrthogonalReflection(a)` | The reflection determined by a non-singular vector `a` of a quadratic space with quadratic form Q and polar form β. Formula: vr = v − Q(a)⁻¹β(v,a)a. In characteristic 2 this is a transvection; otherwise a reflection. Coroot: a∨ = Q(a)⁻¹a. | Standard orthogonal reflection formula from quadratic form theory. |

*Worked example: H99E4 (unitary transvection and unitary reflection of order 4 in a 4-dimensional hermitian space over the cyclotomic field K = Q(i)).*

## 99.3 Construction of Reflection Groups

A pseudo-reflection group in Magma is a group generated by a finite set of invertible
pseudo-reflections. The generators are specified via a collection of roots and coroots, called
basic roots and basic coroots of W. In the general complex case there are no distinguished
generating reflections analogous to simple roots of Weyl groups; the basic roots here must
be carefully distinguished from the simple (fundamental) roots of real reflection groups.
See Section 99.4 for real reflection groups and Section 99.5 for finite complex reflection groups.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `PseudoReflectionGroup(A, B)` | The pseudo-reflection group with basic roots given by the rows of matrix A and corresponding basic coroots by the rows of B. Returns a matrix group whose generators are the pseudo-reflections with the given root/coroot pairs. | Construction from root/coroot data via I − b^tr a for each pair. |

*Worked example: H99E5 (direct construction of the Shephard–Todd group G(14,1,2) with user-supplied roots and coroots over Q(ζ₇); order 392).*

## 99.4 Construction of Real Reflection Groups

Over the real field the only root of unity is −1, so every pseudo-reflection is a reflection.
A reflection group is called real if it is defined over the reals with linearly independent
simple roots and coroots. Real reflection groups are the reflection representations of Coxeter
groups (Chapter 98). Magma allows the base field to be the integer ring (Chapter 18), the
rational field (Chapter 20), number fields (Chapter 34), or cyclotomic fields (Chapter 36);
the real field (Chapter 25) is not allowed (lacks infinite precision). The classification of
finite real reflection groups is given in Section 95.6.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `ReflectionGroup(M)` | The reflection group with Coxeter matrix `M` (see Chapter 95). | From Coxeter matrix. |
| `ReflectionGroup(G)` | The reflection group with Coxeter graph `G` (see Chapter 95). | From Coxeter graph. |
| `ReflectionGroup(C)` | The reflection group with Cartan matrix `C` (see Chapter 95). | From Cartan matrix. |
| `ReflectionGroup(D)` | The reflection group with Dynkin digraph `D` (see Chapter 95). | From Dynkin digraph. |
| `ReflectionGroup(N)` | The finite or affine reflection group with Cartan name given by the string `N` (see Section 95.6). | Name lookup in classification table. |
| `IrreducibleReflectionGroup(X, n)` | The finite or affine irreducible reflection group with Cartan name Xn (see Section 95.6). | Name lookup in classification table. |
| `ReflectionGroup(R)` | The finite reflection group with root system or root datum `R` (see Chapters 96 and 97). | Constructed from the root datum. |
| `ReflectionGroup(W)` *(Coxeter group, fp or mat)* | A reflection group W′ isomorphic to the Coxeter group `W`, together with the isomorphism W → W′. Parameters: `A` (matrix of simple roots), `B` (matrix of simple coroots), `C` (Cartan matrix). Any two of A, B, C determine the third; if C is not given, the standard matrix from Section 95.4 is used. Constraints: A, B have same dimensions, defined over Q, a number field or cyclotomic field with real entries; C = AB^tr must be a Cartan matrix for W. Second form (permutation Coxeter group): no optional parameters needed since the root system is already determined. | Reflection representation of a Coxeter group. |

*Worked examples: H99E6 (B3 from symmetric Cartan matrix, order 48 over Q(√2)). H99E7 (B3 from root datum, order 48 over Z). H99E8 (B3 from fp Coxeter group via `CoxeterGroup(GrpFPCox,·)`).*

## 99.5 Construction of Finite Complex Reflection Groups

A finite complex reflection group has a finite root system but no known analogue of simple
roots. However, the concept of root datum generalises to the complex case. Let D be the ring
of integers of a number field F with well-defined complex conjugation, µ(D) the group of
roots of unity in D, and V = F ⊗_D L. A complex root datum is a 4-tuple (L, L*, Φ, ρ)
where L and L* are free D-modules of rank n in duality, Φ ⊂ L is finite, and
ρ: Φ → L*, satisfying (for all a ∈ Φ): closure under µ(D)-scaling; ρ(λa) = λρ(a);
f(a) = 1 − ⟨a, ρ(a)⟩ ∈ µ(D) \ {1}; and the reflections r_a and r*_a permute Φ and Φ*
respectively with f constant on conjugacy classes.

The Weyl group W of the root datum is generated by the reflections {r_a | a ∈ Φ}. The
basic root and coroot matrices A (rows = roots) and B (rows = coroots) give the complex
Cartan matrix C = AB^tr. Diagrams are named following Cohen **[Coh76]** (with Magma's
variation: Cohen's EN₄ → O₄). The original numbering G1–G37 for primitive groups
is due to Shephard and Todd **[ST54]**.

| `FundamentalWeights(W)` / `FundamentalCoweights(W)` | The fundamental (co)weights of the real reflection group W as rows of a matrix. Requires integral roots and coroots. Parameter: `Basis`. | Dual basis to simple (co)roots **[Bou68]**. |
| `IsDominant(R, v)` | Returns `true` if and only if v is a dominant weight for the root datum R, i.e. a nonnegative integral linear combination of the fundamental weights. Parameter: `Basis`. | Inner product check against simple coroots. |
| `DominantWeight(W, v)` | The unique dominant weight in the same W-orbit as v, where W is a real reflection group and v is a weight (vector or sequence). Also returns a Coxeter group element taking v to the dominant weight. Parameter: `Basis`. | Orbit computation using reflections **[Hum90]**. |
| `WeightOrbit(W, v)` | The W-orbit of v as an indexed set, where W is a real reflection group and v is a weight. The first element is always dominant. Also returns a sequence of Coxeter group elements taking the dominant weight to each orbit element. Parameter: `Basis`. | Orbit computation using reflections **[Hum90]**. |

*Worked examples: H99E26 (weight lattice and fundamental weights of E6). H99E27 (`DominantWeight` and `WeightOrbit` for B3).*

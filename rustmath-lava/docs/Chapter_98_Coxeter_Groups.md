## Scope and overview

This chapter describes Magma functions for computing with Coxeter groups. A **Coxeter system** is a group G with finite generating set S = {s₁, …, sₙ} defined by the relations sᵢ² = 1 and the braid relations sᵢsⱼsᵢ⋯ = sⱼsᵢsⱼ⋯ (each side of length mᵢⱼ ≥ 2). The convention mᵢⱼ = 0 is used in Magma to signify that no braid relation is imposed (where the mathematical convention uses mᵢⱼ = ∞). The **rank** of the system is n = |S|. A system is **reducible** if there is a proper subset I ⊆ {1,…,n} such that mᵢⱼ = 2 whenever i ∈ I and j ∉ I; in that case G is the direct product of the subgroups WI and WIᶜ. Two Coxeter groups are **Coxeter isomorphic** if there is a group isomorphism taking Coxeter generators to Coxeter generators (i.e., the same up to renumbering). See [Bou68] for the foundational theory.

Magma supports three representations:

1. **GrpFPCox** — finitely presented Coxeter group (standard generators and relations); elements are automatically reduced to normal form (see §98.1.1). Applies to finite and infinite groups.
2. **GrpPermCox** — permutation group acting on the roots of the root system; only finite groups. These groups carry an underlying root system or root datum, enabling root-level computations.
3. **GrpMat** — reflection group (matrix group generated by reflections); described in Chapter 99.

If no category is specified, `CoxeterGroup` returns GrpPermCox for finite groups and GrpFPCox for infinite groups. The code for permutation Coxeter groups was originally modelled on the Chevie package of GAP [GHL+96] by Geck, Lübeck, Michel, and Pfeiffer.

### 98.1.1 The Normal Form for Words

Every element w of a Coxeter group W can be written w = r₁r₂⋯rₗ with each rᵢ ∈ S. A **reduced expression** is such a word with l minimal; l is the **length** of w. The **normal form** of w is the lexicographically smallest reduced expression, where the ordering on S is inherited from the generator numbering. Efficient algorithms for computing this normal form were developed and implemented by R. B. Howlett, based on the concept of a **minimal root** [Bri98, BH93]. Elements of GrpFPCox are automatically put into normal form, so equality testing reduces to syntactic comparison.

## 98.3 Converting Between Types of Coxeter Group

Conversion between GrpFPCox, GrpPermCox, GrpMat, GrpFP, and GrpPerm. When converting from a finitely presented Coxeter group (which lacks a built-in reflection representation), the optional parameters `A` (simple roots), `B` (simple coroots), and `C` (Cartan matrix) may be used to specify a reflection representation; any two determine the third, and defaults use the identity for A and the standard Cartan matrix.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `CoxeterGroup(GrpFPCox, W)` | The finitely presented Coxeter group W′ isomorphic to the permutation Coxeter group W, together with the isomorphism W → W′. | — |

## 98.7 Roots, Coroots and Reflections

These functions give access to the root system (or datum) of a **permutation** Coxeter group; they do not apply to finitely presented Coxeter groups.

Roots are stored as an indexed set {@ α₁, …, αN, αN₊₁, …, α₂N @} where α₁, …, αN are positive roots (ordered compatible with height) and αN₊ᵢ = −αᵢ; simple roots are α₁, …, αₙ.

Many functions accept an optional `Basis` parameter:
- `"Standard"` (default): standard basis for the (co)root space.
- `"Root"`: basis of simple (co)roots.
- `"Weight"`: basis of fundamental (co)weights.

## 98.8 Reflections

An element of a Coxeter group is a **reflection** if it is conjugate to one of the Coxeter generators. In a permutation Coxeter group, the root α acts on the root space via the reflection sα; the coroot α* acts on the coroot space via the coreflection s*α.

| `SimpleReflectionPermutations(W)` | The sequence of simple reflections (generators) of the permutation Coxeter group W. | — |

## 98.9 Reflection Subgroups

A **reflection subgroup** of a Coxeter group is a subgroup generated by reflections; all such subgroups are themselves Coxeter groups. The most important class are the **standard parabolic subgroups** WJ generated by a subset J ⊆ {1,…,Rank(W)} of simple roots. A **parabolic subgroup** is a conjugate of a standard parabolic subgroup. In a reflection subgroup, elements are given as permutations of the roots of the overgroup.

Most functions in this section are currently only implemented for permutation Coxeter groups with a root datum.

## 98.13 W-graphs

Given a Coxeter system (W, S), a **W-graph** is a directed or undirected graph whose vertices carry labels (subsets of S called descent sets) and whose edges carry scalar weights. The W-graph determines a representation of the Hecke algebra H = H⟨q⟩: for s ∈ S and a basis element v, the action of Tₛ is:

- v · Tₛ = (−q⁻¹) · v   if s is in the descent set of v,
- v · Tₛ = q · v + Σ(m · u)   if s is not in the descent set of v,

where the sum is over edges with terminal vertex v such that s is in the descent set of the initial vertex u, and m is the edge weight.

The underlying Coxeter group computation uses the elementary root action table (`ReflectionTable`): for a finitely presented Coxeter group W with N elementary roots (numbered 1 to N) and r simple reflections (numbered 1 to r), `ReflectionTable(W)[i,j]` equals k if the i-th simple reflection takes the j-th elementary root to the k-th (k = 0 if non-elementary, k = −i if negative). Mij-sequences encode the Coxeter matrix as the on-or-below-diagonal entries.

The W-graph poset theory ([Nguyen, PhD thesis, University of Sydney, 2010]) underlies the algorithmic construction: if P is a W-graph poset (a set corresponding to a basis of the Specht-module-generalisation GSM(P)), the W-graph is uniquely determined by the function (s,v) ↦ sv and the descent/ascent sets.

# Chapter 56 — Chain Complexes

**Handbook part:** VIII — Modules
**Handbook pages:** 1443–1456 (PDF pages 1574–1591)

---

## Scope and overview

Chapter 56 documents Magma's support for chain complexes of modules — the type `ModCpx` — and the associated type of chain maps. Chain complexes are fundamental objects in homological algebra: conceptually an infinite sequence of modules, indexed by integers, with maps between successive modules such that the composition of any two successive maps is zero. In practice Magma requires all but finitely many of the modules and maps to be zero.

The chapter covers two types of modules over which complexes are supported:

1. **Modules over a basic algebra** `A` (see Chapter 85).
2. **Modules over a multivariate polynomial ring over a field** (see Chapter 109 and `FreeResolution`).

Most functions in the chapter work for either type; exceptions are noted in the text. The chapter is organised into two main parts: §56.1 covers complexes of modules (creation, subcomplexes, quotients, access, elementary operations, extensions, and predicates), and §56.2 covers chain maps (creation, access, operations, predicates, and maps on homology).

The homomorphism from M_n to M_{n-1} is the nth **boundary map**. The **homology in degree n** is the quotient of the kernel of the nth boundary map by the image of the (n+1)th boundary map. A complex is **exact** if the image of each map equals the kernel of the next.

## 56.1 Complexes of Modules

A complex of modules over a basic algebra A is written as an infinite sequence with indexing decreasing to the right:

```
... -> M_n -> M_{n-1} -> M_{n-2} -> ...
```

where the map f_n has domain M_n and codomain M_{n-1}.

### 56.1.1 Creation

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `Complex(L, d)` | Given a list `L` of maps between successive A-modules, creates the corresponding complex. The last term (lowest degree) has degree `d`. Returns an error if the maps do not actually form a complex. | — |
| `Complex(f, d)` | Given a single map `f` between A-modules M and N, forms the two-term complex whose only boundary map is `f`. The term N is in degree `d`. | — |
| `ZeroComplex(A, m, n)` | Given a basic algebra `A` and integers `m > n`, creates a complex of modules over `A` starting at degree `m` and ending at degree `n`, with all modules and maps zero. | — |
| `Dual(C)` / `Dual(C, n)` | Returns the dual of the complex `C` over a basic algebra `A` as a complex over the opposite algebra of `A`. If an integer `n` is supplied, the last term of the dual complex is in degree `n`; otherwise it is in degree 0. | — |

### 56.1.2 Subcomplexes and Quotient Complexes

A **subcomplex** S of a complex C is a complex whose terms are submodules of the terms of C and whose maps are the restrictions of the maps of C to the terms of S.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `sub< C \| Q >` / `sub< C \| L >` | Given a complex `C` and a sequence `Q` of submodules of the terms of `C` (or a list `L` of sequences of elements of the successive terms), returns the smallest subcomplex whose terms contain the given modules (or the submodules they generate). Also returns the chain map giving the inclusion of the subcomplex into `C`. | — |
| `RandomSubcomplex(C, Q)` | Given a chain complex `C` over a basic algebra in degrees `a` to `a-t+1` and a sequence `Q = [q_1, ..., q_t]` of natural numbers, creates the minimal chain complex whose term in degree `a-i+1` is a submodule generated by `q_i` random elements of the corresponding term of `C`. Also returns the inclusion chain map. | — |
| `quo< C \| D >` / `quo< C \| S >` / `quo< C \| L >` | Given a complex `C` and a subcomplex `D`, returns the quotient complex `C/D` together with the natural quotient chain map. Can also be given a sequence `S` of submodules or a list `L` of sequences of elements, from which the subcomplex is computed. | — |

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `Degrees(C)` | Returns the first and last degrees of the defined terms of the complex `C`. | — |
| `Algebra(C)` | Given a complex `C` over a basic algebra `A`, returns the algebra `A`. | — |
| `BoundaryMap(C, n)` | Returns the boundary map of the complex `C` from the term of degree `n` to the term of degree `n-1`. | — |
| `BoundaryMaps(C)` | Returns the list of all boundary maps of the complex `C`. | — |
| `DimensionsOfHomology(C)` | Returns the list of dimensions of the homology groups of `C`. | — |
| `DimensionOfHomology(C, n)` | Returns the dimension of the homology group of `C` in degree `n`. | — |
| `DimensionsOfTerms(C)` | Returns the list of dimensions of the terms of `C`. | — |
| `Term(C, n)` | Returns the module in `C` in degree `n`. | — |
| `Terms(C)` | Returns the sequence of all terms of `C`. | — |

### 56.1.4 Elementary Operations

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `DirectSum(C, D)` | Returns the direct sum of complexes `C` and `D`. | — |
| `Homology(C)` / `HomologyOfChainComplex(C)` | Returns the sequence of homology groups of `C` as a sequence of modules. | — |
| `Homology(C, n)` | Returns the homology group of `C` in degree `n` as an A-module. | — |
| `Prune(C)` | Returns the complex consisting of the terms of `C` with the right end term (term of lowest degree) removed. | — |
| `Prune(C, n)` | Returns the complex with `n` terms removed from the right end. | — |
| `Preprune(C)` | Returns the complex consisting of the terms of `C` with the left end term (term of highest degree) removed. | — |
| `Preprune(C, n)` | Returns the complex with `n` terms removed from the left end. | — |
| `Shift(C, n)` | Given `C` in degrees `r` to `s`, returns the complex in shifted degrees `r+n` to `s+n`. The integer `n` may be positive or negative. All maps in the complex are multiplied by the scalar `(-1)^n`. | — |
| `ShiftToDegreeZero(C)` | Returns the shift of `C` so that the last term (lowest degree) is in degree 0. | — |
| `Splice(C, D)` | Given two complexes `C` and `D` over the same basic algebra, such that the end term of `C` coincides with the initial term of `D`, forms the complex corresponding to the concatenation of `C` and `D` as sequences of maps. Checks that the resulting sequence forms a complex. Degrees of `C` are adjusted to fit `D`. | — |
| `Splice(C, D, f)` | Forms the splice of complex `C` with complex `D` along the map `f` from the last term of `C` to the first term of `D`. The degree of the last term of the splice equals the degree of the last term of `D`. | — |

*Worked examples: H56E1 (constructing a quiver algebra, projective and injective resolutions, splicing them into a single complex via a two-term complex).*

### 56.1.5 Extensions

The following are elementary operations for extending complexes.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `LeftExactExtension(C)` | Returns the complex of length one greater, obtained by adjoining the inclusion map from the kernel of the boundary map in highest degree to the term of highest degree in `C`. | — |
| `RightExactExtension(C)` | Returns the complex of length one greater, obtained by adjoining the quotient map to the cokernel of the boundary map of lowest degree from the term of lowest degree in `C`. | — |
| `ExactExtension(C)` | Returns both the left and right exact extensions of `C`. | — |
| `LeftZeroExtension(C)` / `LeftZeroExtension(C, n)` | Returns the complex of length one greater (or `n` greater), obtained by adjoining the zero map from the zero module to the term of highest degree in `C`. | — |
| `RightZeroExtension(C)` / `RightZeroExtension(C, n)` | Returns the complex of length one greater (or `n` greater), obtained by adjoining the zero map to the zero module from the term of lowest degree in `C`. | — |
| `ZeroExtension(C)` | Returns both the left and right zero extensions of `C`. | — |
| `EqualizeDegrees(C, D)` / `EqualizeDegrees(Q)` | Given complexes `C` and `D` over the same algebra (or a sequence `Q` of complexes), returns the complexes obtained by taking zero extensions as necessary so that all complexes have the same degree range. | — |
| `EqualizeDegrees(C, D, n)` | Returns `C` and `D` with zero extensions sufficient so that `C` and the shift of `D` by degree `n` have the same degrees. | — |

### 56.1.6 Predicates

The following functions return a Boolean value.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `IsExact(C)` | Returns `true` if and only if `C` is an exact sequence, i.e. the image of each map equals the kernel of the succeeding map (with obvious exceptions at the first and last maps). If the complex has only two terms this is trivially true. | — |
| `IsShortExactSequence(C)` | Returns `true` if `C` consists of a short exact sequence together with other terms that are zero. Also returns the degrees of the non-zero terms. | — |
| `IsExact(C, n)` | Returns `true` if and only if `C` is exact in degree `n`, i.e. the image of the map into degree `n` equals the kernel of the succeeding map. | — |
| `IsZeroComplex(C)` | Returns `true` if and only if `C` is composed entirely of zero modules and maps. | — |
| `IsZeroMap(C, n)` | Returns `true` if and only if the boundary map in degree `n` of `C` is the zero map. | — |
| `IsZeroTerm(C, n)` | Returns `true` if and only if the term in degree `n` of `C` is the zero object. | — |

## 56.2 Chain Maps

A **chain map** from complex C to complex D is a sequence of homomorphisms between the terms of C and D such that the maps commute with the boundary maps on both complexes. A chain map has **degree zero** if the map on C_n lands in D_n; more generally the **degree** n expresses how much the degree indices are shifted (the map on C_i lands in D_{i+n}).

Chain maps need not be defined in every degree, but must be defined wherever both the domain term and the corresponding codomain term are defined. For a chain map of degree n from C (degrees a to b) to D (degrees u to v), the map is **proper** when u ≥ a+n and v ≥ b+n. Kernel and cokernel are only defined for proper chain maps.

*Worked examples: H56E2 (basic algebra of Z/3 × S_3, projective resolution, pruning, zero extensions, random subcomplex, homology of subcomplex and cokernel); H56E3 (basic algebra with four non-idempotent generators, random complex, random subcomplex, long exact sequence on homology).*

### 56.2.1 Creation

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `ChainMap(Q, C, D, n)` | Returns the chain map given by the sequence of maps `Q`. Each element of `Q` is a map from a term of complex `C` to a corresponding term of complex `D`. The integer `n` is the degree of the chain map: the term in degree `i` of `C` is mapped to the term in degree `n+i` of `D`. A map must be defined for every degree `i` in `C` such that the term in degree `n+i` exists in `D`. | — |
| `ZeroChainMap(C, D)` | Given chain complexes `C` and `D`, constructs the chain map from `C` to `D` all of whose component maps are zero. | — |

### 56.2.2 Access Functions

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `Degree(f)` | Returns the degree of the chain map `f`. The map `f : C → D` has degree `n` if it takes the term in degree `i` of `C` to the term in degree `i+n` of `D`. | — |
| `ModuleMap(f, n)` | Given a chain map `f : C → D` and integer `n`, returns the map from the term in degree `n` of `C` to the term in degree `n + Degree(f)` of `D`. | — |
| `Kernel(f)` | Returns the kernel complex of `f` and the inclusion of the kernel into the domain of `f`. Only defined for proper chain maps. If `f` is not defined on a term, that term is treated as though `f` is the zero map there. | — |
| `Cokernel(f)` | Returns the cokernel complex of `f` and the projection from the codomain of `f` onto the cokernel. Only defined for proper chain maps. If `f` is not defined on a term, that term is treated as though `f` is the zero map there. | — |
| `Image(f)` | Returns the image complex of `f`, the inclusion of the image in the codomain of `f`, and the projection of the domain of `f` onto the image. Only defined for proper chain maps. | — |

### 56.2.3 Elementary Operations

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `f + g` | The sum of two chain maps with the same domain and codomain. | — |
| `a * g` | The product of the chain map `g` by a scalar `a` in the base ring of the algebra. | — |
| `f * g` | The composition of the two chain maps `f` and `g`. | — |

### 56.2.4 Predicates

The following functions return a Boolean value.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `IsSurjective(f)` | Returns `true` if the chain map `f` is a surjection in every degree, `false` otherwise. | — |
| `IsInjective(f)` | Returns `true` if the chain map `f` is an injection in every degree, `false` otherwise. | — |
| `IsZero(f)` | Returns `true` if the chain map `f` is zero in every degree, `false` otherwise. | — |
| `IsIsomorphism(f)` | Returns `true` if the chain map `f` is an isomorphism of chain complexes, `false` otherwise. | — |
| `IsShortExactSequence(f, g)` | Returns `true` if the sequence `0 → Domain(f) → Domain(g) → Codomain(g) → 0`, where the internal maps are `f` and `g`, is exact. | — |
| `IsChainMap(L, C, D, n)` | Returns `true` if the list of maps `L` from the terms of complex `C` to the terms of complex `D` forms a chain map of degree `n`, i.e. it has the right length and all diagrams commute. | — |
| `IsChainMap(f)` | Returns `true` if the supposed chain map `f` is genuinely a chain map (all diagrams commute). | — |
| `IsProperChainMap(f)` | Returns `true` if the chain map `f` is a proper chain map, a necessary condition for taking the kernel and cokernel. | — |
| `HasDefinedModuleMap(C, n)` | Returns `true` if the module map in degree `n` of the chain map `C` is defined. | — |

### 56.2.5 Maps on Homology

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `InducedMapOnHomology(f, n)` | Returns the homomorphism induced on homology by the chain map `f` in degree `n`. | — |
| `ConnectingHomomorphism(f, g, n)` | Returns the connecting homomorphism in degree `n` of the short exact sequence of chain complexes given by the chain maps `f` and `g`. | — |
| `LongExactSequenceOnHomology(f, g)` | Returns the long exact sequence on homology for the exact sequence of complexes given by the chain maps `f` and `g`, returned as a chain complex. The homology group in degree `i` for the cokernel of complex `C` appears in degree `3i` of the resulting complex. | — |

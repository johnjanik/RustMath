# Chapter 135 — Supersingular Divisors on Modular Curves

**Handbook part:** XVII — Modular Arithmetic Geometry
**Handbook pages:** 4499–4511 (PDF pages 4631–4645)

---

## Scope and overview

This chapter documents how to compute with the Hecke module `D(N, p)` of divisors on the
supersingular points on `X₀(N)` in characteristic `p`. For a prime `p` and an integer `N` not
divisible by `p`, `D(N, p)` is the free abelian group generated by isomorphism classes of pairs
`(E, C)`, where `E` is a supersingular elliptic curve over **F**_{p²} and `C` is a cyclic
subgroup of `E` of order `N` ("an elliptic curve enhanced with level `N` structure"). The group
carries a natural action of Hecke operators `Tₙ`.

The module of supersingular points is a special case of the Brandt module construction
(Chapter 134), via the equivalence between: pairs `(E, C)`; isomorphism classes of left ideals of
an Eichler order of level `N` in the quaternion algebra over **Q** ramified at `p` and `∞`; and
supersingular points on `X₀(N)/`**F**_p over `\overline{F}_p`. These supersingular points
correspond to singularities of the special fiber of a minimal model of `X₀(Np)` at `p`, so the
system of Hecke operators on the supersingular divisor group gives a representation of the
`p`-new subspace of modular forms of level `Np`. Accordingly the *level* of the supersingular
module is `Np`, and `N` is called the *auxiliary level*.

A primary motivation: `D(N, p)` is isomorphic as a module over the Hecke algebra to a subspace of
the modular forms for `Γ₀(N)` of weight 2 and level `Np` (precisely, `D(N, p) ⊗ `**C** is
isomorphic to the `p`-new subspace of `M₂(Γ₀(Np))`). For `N = 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 13`, Magma computes
`D(N, p)` using the highly-efficient method of graphs (Mestre–Oesterlé), which for small `q`
produces very sparse matrices representing the Hecke operators `T_q`; otherwise it falls back to
Brandt modules, which work in general but are slower. The module also relates to formulas of
Gross, Kudla, Merel and others, and (via Stein) to orders of component groups of modular abelian
varieties.

**Methods and limitations.** Two computational approaches are used: the method of graphs of
Mestre–Oesterlé (only when `N = 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 13`, and much faster when applicable) and Brandt
modules in general. The modular-curve approach computes correspondences on `X₀(N)` via
pre-computed models for the covering maps `X₀(Nℓ) → X₀(N)`; these give the Hecke operators `T_ℓ`
as adjacency matrices of the graphs of `ℓ`-isogenies. For the Brandt-module alternative see
Chapter 134 and Pizer **[Piz80]** and Kohel **[Koh01]**. Current package limitations: when using
Brandt modules, no facility is provided for describing the divisors in terms of supersingular
elliptic curves, and it is only possible to work with the module of supersingular divisors over
the integers.

**Categories.** Modules of supersingular points belong to the category `ModSS`; their elements
belong to `ModSSElt`.

**Verbose output.** Set the verbosity with `SetVerbose("SupersingularModule", n)`, where `n` is
0 (silent), 1 (verbose), or 2 (very verbose).

## 135.2 Creation Functions

### 135.2.1 Ambient Spaces

An ambient supersingular divisors module is specified by an integer `N` (the level) and a prime
`p` (the characteristic).

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `SupersingularModule(p, N : parameters)` | The module `M` of supersingular points on `X₀(N)` over `\overline{F}_p`; equivalently the free abelian group on the supersingular elliptic curves in characteristic `p` enhanced with level `N` structure. Requires `N` and `p` coprime. Parameter `Brandt` (`BoolElt`, default `false`): forces computation via quaternion arithmetic (Brandt modules) even when the graph method would apply (and be faster). | Mestre–Oesterlé method of graphs when `N ∈ {1,2,3,5,7,13}` **[Mes86]**; otherwise Brandt modules **[Piz80, Koh01]**. `Brandt := true` forces the Brandt-module algorithm. |
| `SupersingularModule(p)` | The Hecke module `M` of divisors of degree 0 on the supersingular points on `X₀(1)` over `\overline{F}_p`; equivalently the free abelian group on the supersingular `j`-invariants in characteristic `p`. | Method of graphs (`N = 1`) **[Mes86]**. |

*Worked example:* H135E1 (`SupersingularModule(11)`, `(11,3)`, `(3,11)`, `(97)`, and `(97 : Brandt := true)` — dimensions and the effect of the `Brandt` flag).

### 135.2.2 Elements

| Intrinsic | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| `M . i` | The `i`-th basis element of the module `M`. |
| `M ! x` | The coercion of `x` into the module `M`. |

*Worked example:* H135E2 (creating `SupersingularModule(11,3)`, extracting basis elements `X.1`, printing, `Eltseq`, coercion `X![1,0,0,0]`; note Brandt-computed modules — e.g. `(3,11)`, since `X₀(11)` has positive genus — print elements in a less informative way).

### 135.2.3 Subspaces

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `CuspidalSubspace(M)` | The cuspidal submodule `X` of `M`: the submodule of divisors of degree 0 on the supersingular points (the subspace of elements whose coefficients sum to 0). "Cuspidal" because `X ⊗ `**Q** is isomorphic as a Hecke module to the space `S₂(Γ₀(Np); `**Q**`)`^{p-new} of `p`-new cuspforms with rational Fourier coefficients. | Degree-zero condition (sum of coefficients `= 0`). |
| `EisensteinSubspace(M)` | The Eisenstein submodule of `M`: the orthogonal complement of the cuspidal subspace of `M` with respect to the monodromy pairing. | Orthogonal complement under the monodromy pairing. |
| `OrthogonalComplement(M)` | The orthogonal complement of the module `M` in the ambient space with respect to the monodromy pairing. | Monodromy-pairing orthogonal complement. |
| `Kernel(I, M)` | The kernel of `I` on the module `M`: the subspace obtained by intersecting the kernels of the operators `f_n(T_{p_n})`, where `I` is a sequence `[⟨p₁, f₁(x)⟩, …, ⟨p_n, f_n(x)⟩]` of pairs (prime number, polynomial). | Intersection of Hecke-operator polynomial kernels. |
| `Decomposition(M, n)` | Decomposition of the module `M` with respect to the Hecke operators `T₁, T₂, …, Tₙ`. | Simultaneous Hecke-operator decomposition (a few operators usually suffice for a complete decomposition). |

*Worked example:* H135E3 (cuspidal and Eisenstein bases for `p = 11`, `N = 1`; `OrthogonalComplement` showing `S = OrthogonalComplement(E)`; recovering the cuspidal subspace as `Kernel([<2, x+2>], M)` since `T₂` acts as `−2` on it; `Decomposition(M,5)`).

## 135.4 Properties

| Intrinsic | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| `AuxiliaryLevel(M)` | The (auxiliary) level of the module `M`; for `SupersingularModule(N, p)` this is, by definition, `N`. |
| `BaseRing(M)` | The base ring of `M` (currently always **Z**). |
| `Degree(P)` | The sum of the coefficients of the module element `P`, written with respect to the basis of the ambient space of the parent of `M`. |
| `Dimension(M)` | The dimension of the module `M`. |
| `Eltseq(P)` | A sequence of integers defining the module element `P`. |
| `Level(M)` | The level of the module `M`; for `SupersingularModule(N, p)` this is, by definition, `Np`. |
| `ModularEquation(M)` | The equation of `X₀(N)` used when applying the Mestre method to compute with the supersingular module `M`. |
| `Prime(M)` | The prime of the module `M`; for `SupersingularModule(N, p)` this is, by definition, `p`. |

*Worked example:* H135E4 (`SupersingularModule(3,11)`: `AuxiliaryLevel` `= 11`, `BaseRing` `= `Integer Ring, `Degree`/`Eltseq` of `M.1+7*M.2`, `Dimension`, `Level` `= 33`, `Prime` `= 3`; `ModularEquation(SupersingularModule(11,3))` returns `x*y + 8`).

## 135.7 Arithmetic

The standard arithmetic operations `+` and `−` and left scalar multiplication are defined on
elements of supersingular modules; two submodules of an ambient supersingular module can also be
added and intersected.

| Intrinsic | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| `P + Q` / `P - Q` / `a * P` | Sum, difference, and left scalar multiple of module elements. |
| `M1 + M2` | The submodule generated by all sums of elements in the supersingular modules `M₁` and `M₂`. |
| `M1 meet M2` | The intersection of the supersingular modules `M₁` and `M₂`. |

*Worked example:* H135E7 (element arithmetic on `SupersingularModule(11)`; submodule arithmetic `V := E + S`; index of `E + S` in `M` (= 5, related to Eisenstein/cuspform congruences) via `RSpace(M)/RSpace(V)`; and `E meet S` being the zero module).

## 135.8 Operators

| Intrinsic | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| `HeckeOperator(M, n)` | A matrix representing the `n`-th Hecke operator `Tₙ` with respect to `Basis(M)` for the supersingular module `M`. |
| `AtkinLehnerOperator(M, q)` | A matrix representing the Atkin–Lehner involution `W_q` on the supersingular module `M`. The number `q` must equal either `Prime(M)` or `AuxiliaryLevel(M)`. |

*Worked example:* H135E8 (for `p = 11`, `N = 3`, `T₂` and `W₃` have the same characteristic polynomial on the cuspidal supersingular subspace as on `S₂(Γ₀(33))`; and the `p = 3`, `N = 11` cuspidal subspace matches the 3-new cuspforms — comparing `HeckeOperator`/`AtkinLehnerOperator` values).

## 135.9 The Monodromy Pairing

The monodromy pairing is a nondegenerate, Hecke-equivariant, integer-valued pairing on the module
of supersingular points (not perfect in general). It has a simple description in terms of the
enhanced supersingular elliptic curves: if `E` and `F` are two supersingular elliptic curves in
characteristic `p` with level `N` structure, then `E` and `F` pair to 0 unless `E ≅ F`, in which
case they pair to half the number of automorphisms of `E`.

| Intrinsic | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| `MonodromyPairing(P, Q)` | The monodromy pairing of elements `P` and `Q` of a supersingular module. |
| `MonodromyWeights(M)` | The diagonal entries that define the monodromy pairing on the ambient space. |

*Worked example:* H135E9 (`p = 3`, `N = 11`: `MonodromyWeights(SupersingularModule(11))` returns `[2, 3]`; pairing a cuspidal element with an Eisenstein element gives 0, while a cuspidal element with itself gives 5).

**Handbook part:** XIX — Geometry

## Scope and overview

This chapter documents a package of tools to build and analyse finite-dimensional,
finitely-generated, **rational** polyhedra. In general a polyhedron is the Minkowski sum of a
rational polytope (the compact, convex hull of finitely many rational points) and a cone (the
convex hull of finitely many rational half-lines emanating from the origin).

The package works only with *rational* polyhedra: their vertices lie in some finite-dimensional
rational vector space `L = Q^n` with the supporting hyperplanes represented in its dual (loosely,
all faces have rational gradients). There is a natural lattice in this setup: the integral
sublattice `Z^n` of `L`. This is a lattice only in the sense of being a **Z**-module; it does not
come with a preferred definite quadratic form, so lengths of vectors are not defined. (Volumes are
nonetheless defined relative to the integral lattice: the lattice-normalised volume is
`Vol(P) = n! × vol(P)`, with `vol(P)` the Euclidean volume.) A point of `L` is *integral* if it
lies in the integral sublattice, but vertices of polyhedra need not be integral. These ambient
spaces `L` are referred to as *lattices* (toric lattices).

The chapter is organised so that functions applying to compact polyhedra (polytopes) are collected
together. Section 143.2 lists ways of constructing polytopes, cones and polyhedra, including
Minkowski sum and other arithmetic and set-theoretic operations. Section 143.3 presents the basic
combinatorics common to all polyhedra (vertices, inequalities, facets, faces). Section 143.4 is
dedicated to polytopes; most of these functions rely on compactness and include enumeration of
integral points (point counting via the methods of Barvinok and Pommersheim **[BP99]** as described
in **[DLHTY04]**), Ehrhart theory, automorphism groups and triangulation. Section 143.5 details
functions that apply to cones and polyhedra more generally, including computing the integral
generators of `C ∩ Z^n` as a semigroup. Section 143.6 outlines the functions operating on the
ambient toric lattices (type `TorLat`), which are normally a book-keeping device but can be used to
change the underlying notion of which points count as integral.

The principal Magma types are `TorPol` (polytopes and polyhedra), `TorCon` (cones), `TorLat`
(toric lattices) and `TorLatElt` (toric lattice elements).

## 143.2 Polytopes, Cones and Polyhedra

A *(rational) polytope* in a lattice `L = Q^n` is the convex hull of finitely many points in `L`
(not necessarily integral). A *cone* `C` in a lattice `L` is the convex hull of finitely many rays
(or zero); a *ray* is a half-line from the origin, treated synonymously with its first integral
lattice point outside the origin (so an intrinsic returning a ray actually returns a primitive
integral point). Rays that are the intersection of a linear hyperplane with `C` are the *extreme
rays* (corner edges) of `C`. A cone is *regular* if generated as a semigroup in `Z^n` by its
extreme rays. A *(rational) polyhedron* is the Minkowski sum of a polytope and a cone — the cone is
the unique *tail cone*; the terms *compact part* and *infinite part* denote the defining polytope
and cone.

### 143.2.1 Polytopes

| Intrinsic | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| `Polytope(Q)` | The polytope defined as the convex hull of the sequence of points `Q`, specified as sequences of integers or rational numbers (of fixed length) or as points of a common lattice. |
| `RandomPolytope(L,n,k)` / `RandomPolytope(d,n,k)` | A polytope in the toric lattice `L` (or a toric lattice of dimension `d`) generated by `n` random integral points with coefficients of modulus at most the non-negative integer `k`. |
| `Polar(P)` | The polar dual polyhedron `P★ = { u ∈ L^∨ : u·v ≥ −1 ∀ v ∈ P }`. |
| `CrossPolytope(L)` / `CrossPolytope(d)` | The maximum-dimensional cross-polytope in the lattice `L`, or of dimension `d` for an integer `d`. |
| `StandardSimplex(L)` / `StandardSimplex(d)` | The simplex given by the standard basis and the origin of the lattice `L`, or the `d`-dimensional simplex given by the standard basis and the origin. |
| `CyclicPolytope(L,n)` / `CyclicPolytope(d,n)` | The cyclic polytope generated by `n` points in the lattice `L`, or by `n` points in `d`-dimensional space for an integer `d`. |

*Worked examples:* H143E1 (building a polytope as a convex hull, recovering `Vertices`, `Facets`,
`Points`, `Volume`, `Polar`, `EhrhartCoefficients`, `EhrhartSeries`, then mapping it through a
lattice map and triangulating the image).

### 143.2.2 Cones

Cones in lattices are of type `TorCon`.

| Intrinsic | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| `Cone(A)` | The cone in a lattice generated by a sequence `A` of its elements (or simply of sequences of integer or rational coefficients). |
| `Cone(v)` | The cone in a lattice generated by a single element `v`; namely the single ray `Q⁺v`. |
| `ConeWithInequalities(B)` | The cone in a lattice `L` defined by the set `B` of elements of the dual lattice `L^∨` (or sequences of integer/rational coefficients): the intersection of half-spaces `v·u ≥ 0`, namely `⋂_{v∈B} { u ∈ L : v·u ≥ 0 }`. |
| `FullCone(L)` / `FullCone(n)` | The cone which is the entire lattice `L`, or the lattice of dimension `n` for a positive integer `n`. |
| `PositiveQuadrant(L)` / `PositiveQuadrant(n)` | The cone in lattice `L` generated by the standard basis vectors of `L`, or that of the lattice of dimension `n`. |
| `ZeroCone(L)` / `ZeroCone(n)` | The cone consisting of only the origin of the lattice `L`, or of the lattice of dimension `n`. |
| `Dual(C)` | The dual cone `C^∨ = { u ∈ L^∨ : v·u ≥ 0 ∀ v ∈ C }`, where `C` lies in the lattice `L`. |
| `NormalisedCone(P)` | A cone `C` such that the polyhedron `P` is the intersection of `C` with a hyperplane at height one, together with the embedding of the ambient lattice of `P` into the ambient lattice of `C`. |
| `ConeInSublattice(C)` | The cone of maximal dimension given by the intersection of `C` with its linear span. Also gives the embedding of the sublattice in the ambient toric lattice. |
| `ConeQuotientByLinearSubspace(C)` | The strictly convex cone given by the quotient of `C` by its maximal linear subspace. Also gives the quotient map. |

*Worked example:* H143E2 (`Cone`, `Rays`, Minkowski sum `P + C` of a `StandardSimplex` and a cone,
and `Facets` of the resulting polyhedron).

### 143.2.3 Polyhedra

Polytopes and polyhedra in lattices are of type `TorPol`.

| Intrinsic | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| `Polyhedron(C,H,h)` | The polyhedron constructed as the slice of the cone `C` by the hyperplane determined by the primitive dual vector `H` at height given by the integer or rational number `h`. |
| `Polyhedron(C)` | The polyhedron arising as the intersection of the cone `C` with the hyperplane at height one. Parameter `level` (`RngIntElt`, default `1`) changes the height. |
| `HalfspaceToPolyhedron(v,h)` / `HalfspaceToPolyhedron(Q,h)` | The halfspace `{ u : v·u ≥ h }` as a polyhedron, where `v` is a point of a toric lattice (or a sequence of its integral/rational coefficients) and `h ∈ Q`. |
| `HyperplaneToPolyhedron(v,h)` / `HyperplaneToPolyhedron(Q,h)` | The hyperplane `{ u : v·u = h }` as a polyhedron, where `v` is a point of a toric lattice (or a sequence of coefficients) and `h ∈ Q`. |
| `Polyhedron(C,f,v)` | The polyhedron arising as the preimage of the cone `C` under the affine map `f + v`. |
| `EmptyPolyhedron(L)` | The empty polyhedron in the toric lattice `L`. |
| `ConeToPolyhedron(C)` | The cone `C` as a polyhedron. |
| `PolyhedronInSublattice(P)` | The polyhedron of maximal dimension given by the intersection of the toric lattice containing `P` with an affine sublattice of dimension equal to the dimension of `P`. Also gives the affine embedding as a lattice embedding and translation. |
| `FixedSubspaceToPolyhedron(G)` / `FixedSubspaceToPolyhedron(L,G)` | The subspace (as a polyhedron) fixed by the action of the matrix group `G` on the toric lattice `L`. `G` should be a subgroup of either `GL(n, Z)` or `SL(n, Z)`, where `n = dim L`. |

*Worked example:* H143E3 (slicing `PositiveQuadrant(3)` with various dual vectors to obtain compact
and non-compact polyhedra; `IsPolytope`, changing the slice level).

### 143.2.4 Arithmetic Operations on Polyhedra

| Intrinsic | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| `C eq D` | `true` iff the cones `C` and `D` are equal (lie in the same toric lattice and have identical supports). |
| `P eq Q` | `true` iff the polyhedra `P` and `Q` are equal (same toric lattice, identical supports). |
| `C meet D` | The intersection of the cones `C` and `D`; they must lie in the same toric lattice. |
| `P meet Q` | The intersection of the polyhedra `P` and `Q`; they must lie in the same toric lattice. |
| `P subset Q` | `true` iff the support of polyhedron `P` is contained in that of `Q`; both must lie in the same toric lattice. |
| `C + D` / `P + Q` | The Minkowski sum of the cones `C` and `D`, or of the polyhedra `P` and `Q`. |
| `P + C` / `C + P` | The polyhedron constructed as the Minkowski sum of the polytope `P` and the cone `C`. |
| `P * Q` | The convex hull of the Cartesian product of the polyhedra `P` and `Q`. |
| `k * P` | The dilation of the polyhedron `P` by the rational number `k`. |
| `-P` | The negation of the polyhedron `P`. |

## 143.3 Basic Combinatorics of Polytopes and Polyhedra

Cones, polytopes and polyhedra have similar combinatorial features: they are composed of faces of
various dimensions that meet in faces of lower dimension. A *face* of a cone `C` is the intersection
of `C` with an affine hyperplane whose defining equation is non-negative on `C`; a *facet* is a
codimension-1 face. Definitions for polytopes and polyhedra are similar.

### 143.3.1 Vertices and Inequalities

| Intrinsic | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| `Vertices(P)` | A sequence containing the vertices of the polyhedron `P`. |
| `NumberOfVertices(P)` | The number of vertices of the polyhedron `P`. |
| `Rays(C)` | The sequence of generators of the rays of the cone `C` (returned as primitive lattice points). If `C` is strictly convex, this is the same as the minimal **R**-generators. |
| `Ray(C,i)` | The `i`-th ray of the cone `C` (in the order returned by `Rays(C)`). |
| `LinearSpanEquations(C)` / `LinearSpanEquations(Q)` | A sequence of equations defining the minimal linear subspace containing the cone `C` (or the sequence of toric lattice points `Q`). |
| `LinearSpanGenerators(C)` / `LinearSpanGenerators(Q)` | A sequence of generators of the minimal linear subspace containing the cone `C` (or the sequence of toric lattice points `Q`). |
| `LinearSubspaceGenerators(C)` | A basis of the maximal linear subspace contained in the cone `C`. |
| `Inequalities(C)` / `Inequalities(P)` / `MinimalInequalities(C)` | If the cone `C` or polyhedron `P` lies in a toric lattice `L`, a finite sequence of vectors in the dual lattice `L̄` defining the supporting hyperplanes of `C` or `P`. The list is forced minimal for `MinimalInequalities`. If the argument is a polyhedron, an integer `k` is returned as a second value, so the first `k` inequalities correspond to the facets of `P` while the remaining ones cut out the subspace containing `P`. |

*Worked example:* H143E4 (`Inequalities` of a polytope, second return value counting facet
inequalities; reconstructing the polytope from its halfspaces via `&meet`/`HalfspaceToPolyhedron`;
a non-full-dimensional polytope where some inequalities cut out the affine span).

### 143.3.2 Facets and Faces

| Intrinsic | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| `#fVector(C)` / `fVector(P)` | The `f`-vector of the polyhedron `P` or cone `C`. |
| `#hVector(C)` / `#hVector(P)` | The `h`-vector of the polyhedron `P` or cone `C`. |
| `Facets(C)` / `Facets(P)` | A sequence containing all facets of the toric cone `C` or polyhedron `P`. |
| `FacetIndices(P)` | A sequence of sets describing the facets of the polytope `P`. The `j`-th set gives the indices of the vertices of `P` which define the `j`-th facet. |
| `NumberOfFacets(P)` | The number of facets of the polyhedron `P`. |
| `Faces(C)` / `Faces(P)` / `Faces(C,i)` / `Faces(P,i)` | A sequence containing all face cones of the toric cone `C` or polyhedron `P`, or only those of dimension `i` if an integer `i` is also specified. |
| `FaceIndices(P,i)` | A sequence of sets describing the `i`-dimensional faces of the polytope `P`. The `j`-th set gives the indices of the vertices of `P` which define the `j`-th `i`-dimensional face. |
| `Edges(P)` | A sequence containing all the edges of the polyhedron `P`. |
| `EdgeIndices(P)` | A sequence of sets describing the edges of the polyhedron `P`. The `j`-th set gives the indices of the vertices of `P` which define the `j`-th edge. |
| `Graph(P)` | The graph of the face lattice of the polyhedron `P`. The vertices of the graph are labelled by the dimension of the corresponding face. |
| `FaceSupportedBy(C,H)` | The face of the toric cone `C` supported by the toric lattice element `H` in the dual lattice to the one containing `C` (so `H` is a linear form on the ambient lattice of `C`). |
| `IsSupportingHyperplane(v,h,P)` | `true` iff the hyperplane `v·u = h` is a supporting hyperplane of the polyhedron `P`, where `v` is a lattice point of the dual ambient lattice of `P` and `h` is rational. If so, also gives the sign `τ ∈ {−1,0,+1}` such that `Sign(v·u − h)` is either `0` or `τ` for all `u ∈ P` (`τ = 0` if `P` is contained in the hyperplane). |
| `SupportingCone(P,v)` | The cone `C` such that `C + v` is a supporting cone of the polyhedron `P`, where `v` is a vertex of `P`. |

## 143.4 The Combinatorics of Polytopes

### 143.4.1 Points in Polytopes

Given a polytope, one might want to know how many integral points it contains and to list them.
These are computed by different algorithms. For point counting Magma uses the methods of Barvinok
and Pommersheim **[BP99]**, as described in **[DLHTY04]**. The number of points in integral
dilations of a polytope are the coefficients of a generating function, the Ehrhart series.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `Points(P)` / `InteriorPoints(P)` / `BoundaryPoints(P)` | The integral toric lattice points, the strictly interior lattice points, or the boundary lattice points of the polytope `P`. | Lattice-point enumeration. |
| `NumberOfPoints(P)` | The number of integral toric lattice points of the polytope `P`. | Barvinok–Pommersheim point counting **[BP99, DLHTY04]**. |

### 143.4.2 Ehrhart Theory of Polytopes

| Intrinsic | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| `EhrhartSeries(P)` | The rational generating function of the Ehrhart series for the polytope `P`. |
| `EhrhartPolynomial(P)` | A sequence representing the Ehrhart (quasi-)polynomial for `P`: a sequence of polynomials `[p_0,…,p_{r−1}]` of length `r`, the quasi-period, so that the number of lattice points of the dilation `nP` is `p_s(k)` where `n = kr + s` is the Euclidean division of `n` by `r` (`s` is the least residue of `n` mod `r`). Since Magma indexes sequences from 1, `p_i = EhrhartPolynomial(P)[i+1]`. |
| `EhrhartCoefficients(P,l)` | The first `l + 1` coefficients of the Ehrhart series for `P` (starting with `0P` up to and including `lP`). |
| `EhrhartCoefficient(P,k)` | The number of lattice points in the (non-negative, integral) dilation `kP` of the polytope `P`. |

### 143.4.3 Automorphisms of a Polytope

| Intrinsic | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| `AutomorphismGroup(P)` | The subgroup of `GL(n, Z)` (acting on the ambient lattice) which leaves the polyhedron `P` unchanged. |

*Worked example:* H143E5 (`AutomorphismGroup` of `CrossPolytope(2)`, returned as a `MatrixGroup(2,
Integer Ring)`).

### 143.4.4 Operations on Polytopes

| Intrinsic | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| `Triangulation(P)` | A sequence of polytopes that make a triangulation of the polytope `P`. |
| `TriangulationOfBoundary(P)` | A sequence of polytopes that make a triangulation of the boundary of the polytope `P`. |

### 143.5.1 Generators of Cones

| Intrinsic | Description |

### 143.5.2 Properties of Polyhedra

| Intrinsic | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| `CompactPart(P)` | The polytope defined as the convex hull of the vertices of the polyhedron `P`. This is the smallest polytope which can occur as a factor in an expression of `P` as the Minkowski sum of a polytope and a cone. |
| `IntegralPart(P)` | The polyhedron defined as the convex hull of the integral points contained in the polyhedron `P`. |
| `InfinitePart(P)` | The tail cone of the polyhedron `P`. |
| `IsEmpty(P)` | `true` iff the polyhedron `P` is empty. |
| `IsMaximalDimension(C)` / `IsMaximalDimension(P)` | `true` iff the cone `C` or polyhedron `P` has dimension equal to that of its ambient lattice. |
| `IsStrictlyConvex(C)` | `true` iff the cone `C` is strictly convex; i.e. there exists a hyperplane `H` such that `C` lies on one side of `H` and meets `H` in the single point 0. |
| `IsSimplicial(C)` / `IsSimplicial(P)` | `true` iff the cone `C` or polyhedron `P` is simplicial. |
| `IsSimplex(P)` | `true` iff the polyhedron `P` is a simplex. |
| `IsSimple(P)` | `true` iff the polyhedron `P` is simple. |
| `IsAffineLinear(P)` | `true` iff the polyhedron `P` is an affine linear space. |
| `IsZero(C)` | `true` iff the cone `C` is supported at the origin of its ambient toric lattice. |

*Worked examples:* H143E7 (`Polar` of a polytope, `HasIntegralPoint`, `IntegralPart`,

### 143.5.3 Attributes of Polyhedra

| Intrinsic | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| `Dimension(C)` / `Dimension(P)` | The dimension of the toric cone `C` or polyhedron `P`. |
| `Index(C)` | The index of the sublattice generated by the minimal **R**-generators of the cone `C` in its linear span. |
| `IsShellable(P)` | `true` iff the integral polytope `P` is shellable; i.e. if there exists a polytope `S` such that each lattice point `u ∈ P` lies on the boundary of `v + i*(S − v)` for some `0 ≤ i ≤ k`, where `v` is the barycentre of the vertices of `P`. If `true`, also returns `S`, `v`, and `k`. |

### 143.5.4 Combinatorics of Polyhedral Complexes

| Intrinsic | Description |
|-----------|-------------|

| `ChangeAmbient(C,L)` / `ChangeAmbient(P,L)` | Make a cone or polyhedron identical to `C` or `P` but lying in the toric lattice `L`; the identification of the existing ambient toric lattice with `L` is by identification of the two standard bases and requires the dimensions of the two spaces to be equal. |

## 143.6 Toric Lattices

A *toric lattice* is a finite-dimensional rational vector space with a distinguished free
**Z**-module `L` that spans it: the pair `L ⊗ Q ⊃ L` where `L ≅ Z^n`. The covering vector space
allows fluent working with non-integral points of `L`. In Magma these two spaces (`L` and `L_Q`,
and their duals) are bound together in a single object. Toric lattices are of type `TorLat` and
their elements are of type `TorLatElt`.

### 143.6.1 Toric Lattices

| Intrinsic | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| `ToricLattice(n)` | Create an `n`-dimensional toric lattice `Q^n ⊃ Z^n`. The integer `n` must be non-negative. |
| `ScalarLattice()` | The unique one-dimensional toric lattice of scalars `Q ⊃ Z`. |
| `Dual(L)` | The dual lattice of the toric lattice `L`. |

*Worked examples:* H143E9 (summing a properly typed sequence of toric lattice points with `&+`;
`IsIntegral`); H143E10 (`Dual` of a toric lattice, default printing `(Z^2)^*`, double-dual
returns `L`).

### 143.6.2 Points of Toric Lattices

Points of `L` are interpreted as points of `L ⊗ Q`; integral coefficients are recognised.

| Intrinsic | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| `L + M` / `DirectSum(L,M)` / `DirectSum(Q)` | The direct sum of the toric lattices `L` and `M`. Also returns the embedding of `L`, the embedding of `M`, the projection onto `L`, and the projection onto `M`. The argument may also be a sequence `Q` of toric lattices, giving three return values: the direct sum, a sequence of inclusion maps, and a sequence of projection maps. |
| `L ^ n` | The direct sum of the toric lattice `L` with itself `n` times. Also returns a sequence of injections and a sequence of projections. Identical to `DirectSum([L,L,...,L])` with the sequence of length `n`. |
| `Dimension(L)` | The dimension of the toric lattice `L`. |
| `L ! [a,b,...]` / `LatticeVector(L,Q)` / `LatticeVector(Q)` | The point `(a,b,…)` as an element of the toric lattice `L`, where `Q = [a,b,…]` is a sequence of rational numbers or integers. (If `L` is omitted, it will be created.) |
| `L . i` / `Basis(L,i)` | The `i`-th standard basis element of the toric lattice `L`. |
| `Basis(L)` | The standard basis elements of the toric lattice `L` as a sequence of points of `L`. |
| `Form(L,Q)` | The point `(a,b,…)` as an element of the dual of the toric lattice `L`, where `Q = [a,b,…]`. |
| `Zero(L)` | The zero vector in the toric lattice `L`. |
| `P + Q` / `P - Q` / `n * P` / `P / n` | The sum and difference of toric lattice points `P` and `Q` (or points of the dual), the rational multiple `nP`, and the quotient `P/n`, where `n ∈ Q`. |
| `P eq Q` | `true` iff the toric lattice points `P` and `Q` are the same point of the same lattice. |
| `AreProportional(P,Q)` | `true` iff the toric lattice points `P` and `Q` are rational multiples of one another; the factor `Q/P` is returned in that case. |
| `P / Q` | The rational factor `P/Q` in the case that the toric lattice points `P` and `Q` are rational multiples of one another. |
| `v in L` | `true` iff the toric lattice point `v` lies in the toric lattice `L`. |
| `IsZero(v)` | `true` iff the toric lattice point `v` is the zero vector. |
| `IsIntegral(v)` | `true` iff the coefficients of the toric lattice point `v` are integral. |
| `IsPrimitive(v)` | `true` iff the toric lattice point `v` is a primitive integral vector; i.e. `v` is not divisible as an integral vector in its ambient toric lattice. |
| `PrimitiveLatticeVector(v)` | The first toric lattice point on the ray spanned by `v`. |

*Worked examples:* H143E11 (building points in a toric lattice and arithmetic: `Parent`, sum,
scalar multiple, equality); H143E12 (primitivity in a sublattice vs the larger lattice; `Sublattice`,
pulling back via `@@ emb`, and the difference from coercion `K ! v`).

### 143.6.3 Operations on Toric Lattices

| Intrinsic | Description |

| `Quotient(C)` / `Quotient(Q)` / `Quotient(v)` | A toric lattice isomorphic to the quotient of `L` by the linear span of the cone `C`, or by the elements `v ∈ L` comprising the sequence `Q`, or by the single primitive vector `v ∈ L`. The projection map of `L` to the quotient is also returned. |
| `AddVectorToLattice(v)` / `AddVectorToLattice(Q)` | A toric lattice `L₁` isomorphic to the toric lattice generated by `L` together with the vector `v ∈ L` (or by a sequence of vectors of `L`). The inclusion map of `L` in `L₁` is also returned. |

| `IsSuperlattice(L)` | `true` iff the toric lattice `L` was constructed as a superlattice of another toric lattice (by the addition of a rational vector). |
| `IsDirectSum(L)` | `true` iff the toric lattice `L` was constructed as the direct sum of other toric lattices. |
| `IsQuotient(L)` | `true` iff the toric lattice `L` was constructed as the quotient of another toric lattice. |
| `Sublattice(L)` | If the toric lattice `L` was constructed as the extension of a toric lattice `K` by the addition of a vector, return `K`. |
| `Superlattice(L)` | If the toric lattice `L` was constructed as a sublattice of a toric lattice `K`, return `K`. |
| `Summands(L)` | If the toric lattice `L` was constructed as a direct sum, return a sequence of the toric lattice summands used (and parallel sequences of their embedding maps in `L` and the projections from `L` to them). |

*Worked example:* H143E13 (`Sublattice`, `Quotient` and `AddVectorToLattice` of a toric lattice,
recovering the basis images under the associated maps).

### 143.6.4 Maps of Toric Lattices

Maps between toric lattices are of type `TorLatMap`, constructed with `hom< L -> K | M >` where `M`
is the matrix of the linear map with respect to the standard bases of `L` and `K`. The evaluation
operations `@` and `@@` apply; image and preimage intrinsics exist too. Basic arithmetic of maps
(sum, difference, scalar multiple, pointwise) is available.

| Intrinsic | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| `ZeroMap(L,K)` | The zero map between toric lattices `L` and `K`. |
| `IdentityMap(L)` | The identity map on the toric lattice `L`. |
| `hom< L -> K \| M >` / `LatticeMap(L,K,M)` | The map between toric lattices `L` and `K` determined by the matrix `M` (with respect to the standard bases of `L` and `K`). |
| `LatticeMap(L,Q)` | The toric lattice map from `L` to the toric lattice `M` determined by the sequence `Q` of toric lattice points, where `M` is the toric lattice containing the points of `Q`. |
| `DefiningMatrix(f)` | The defining matrix of the lattice map `f`. |
| `Image(f,C)` / `Image(f,P)` / `Image(f,v)` | The image of the cone `C`, polyhedron `P` or lattice element `v` under the toric lattice map `f`; the type of the object is preserved. |
| `Preimage(f,C)` / `Preimage(f,P)` / `Preimage(f,v)` | The preimage of the cone `C`, polyhedron `P` or lattice element `v` by the toric lattice map `f`; the type of the object is preserved. |
| `KernelEmbedding(f)` / `KernelEmbedding(v)` | The inclusion of the kernel of the toric lattice map `f`, regarded as a distinct toric lattice, into the domain of `f`; or the same for the dual subspace annihilated by lattice element `v`. |
| `KernelBasis(f)` / `KernelBasis(v)` | A basis for the kernel of the toric lattice map `f`, or the dual of the sublattice annihilated by the lattice element `v`. |
| `ImageBasis(f)` | A basis for the image of the toric lattice map `f`. |
| `IsCokernelTorsionFree(f)` | `true` iff the cokernel of the toric lattice map `f` is torsion free. |

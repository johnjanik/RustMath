## Scope and overview

This chapter presents the Magma tools for partitions, words and Young tableaux. The three
topics are closely connected through the theory of the **plactic monoid** and **Knuth
equivalence**.

1. **Partitions.** A partition of a positive integer `n` is a weakly decreasing sequence
   `[n₁, n₂, …, n_k]` of positive integers (the *parts*) summing to `n`. Magma's partition
   functions operate only on small positive integers: enumeration, counting, restricted
   partitions, weight and lexicographic indexing.

2. **Words.** Words are elements of an *ordered monoid* — a free monoid whose generators carry
   an explicit ordering. The two built-in flavours are the monoid of words over the positive
   integers (natural order) and finitely generated monoids with named, ordered generators. The
   ordering matters because it defines **Knuth equivalence** (via the relations `yxz ∼ yzx` for
   `x < y ≤ z` and `xzy ∼ zxy` for `x ≤ y < z`). The quotient of an ordered monoid by Knuth
   equivalence is the **plactic monoid**, whose elements are canonical class representatives —
   in fact the *row word* of the corresponding tableau. The plactic monoid is isomorphic to the
   monoid of tableaux over the same generators.

3. **Young tableaux.** The tableaux module is loosely based on the computer-algebra system
   SYMMETRICA of Kerber and Kohnert **[KKL92]**; the theoretical reference is Fulton's *Young
   Tableaux* **[Ful97]**. A Young (Ferrers) diagram is a left-justified array of cells whose row
   lengths form a partition (the *shape*); a Young tableau is a numbering with weakly increasing
   rows and strictly increasing columns. Tableaux form a monoid under a product realised either
   by Schensted **row insertion** ("bumping") or by Schützenberger's **jeu de taquin**
   ("sliding"). Tableaux can be standard or skew, and the chapter covers creation, enumeration,
   random generation, structural access, operations, the **Robinson–Schensted–Knuth (RSK)
   correspondence**, and counting (including hook lengths and Kostka numbers).

## 145.2 Partitions

A *partition* of a positive integer `n` is a decreasing sequence `[n₁, n₂, …, n_k]` of positive
integers such that `Σ nᵢ = n`. The `nᵢ` are called the *parts*. All partition functions in
Magma operate only on small, positive integers.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `NumberOfPartitions(n)` | The total number of partitions of a positive integer `n`. | — |
| `Partitions(n)` | The sequence of all partitions of a positive integer `n`. | — |
| `Partitions(n, k)` | The sequence of all partitions of `n` into `k` parts. | — |
| `RestrictedPartitions(n, M)` | The sequence of all partitions of `n` whose parts are restricted to elements of the set `M` of positive integers. | — |
| `RestrictedPartitions(n, k, M)` | The sequence of all partitions of `n` into `k` parts, with parts restricted to elements of the set `M`. | — |
| `IsPartition(S)` | True if the sequence `S` consists of weakly decreasing positive integers; trailing zeros are allowed and the empty sequence is accepted (as a partition of zero). | — |
| `RandomPartition(n)` | A weakly decreasing sequence of positive integers which is a random partition of `n`. | — |
| `Weight(P)` | For a partition `P` (sequence of positive integers): the sum of its parts. | — |
| `IndexOfPartition(P)` | The lexicographical order of partition `P` among partitions of the same weight. Ordering: `P₁ > P₂` iff `P₁` is greater in the first part in which they differ; the first index is zero. | Lexicographic ranking. |

*Worked examples:* H145E1 (partitions of `n` ↔ conjugacy classes of `Sym(n)`, via `PartitionToElt`); H145E2 (coin-change counts via `RestrictedPartitions`, checked against a generating function).

## 145.3 Words

Words are elements of an *ordered monoid* — a free monoid whose generators have an explicit
ordering. The most important example is the monoid of words generated by the positive integers,
though Magma also allows finitely generated ordered monoids. The ordering is essential to the
definition of **Knuth equivalence**, given by the two relations

```
yxz ∼ yzx      x < y ≤ z
xzy ∼ zxy      x ≤ y < z
```

The *plactic* monoid is the quotient of an ordered monoid by Knuth equivalence; it is isomorphic
to the monoid of tableaux over the same generators. Elements of the plactic monoid are
represented by a canonical representative — the *row word* of the corresponding tableau.

### 145.3.1 Ordered Monoids

An ordered monoid is a free monoid with an ordered basis. Magma has two types: the monoid of
words over the positive integers (retaining their natural ordering), and finitely generated
monoids whose generators may be assigned names.

| Intrinsic | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| `OrderedMonoid(n)` | The ordered monoid with `n` ordered generators. |
| `OrderedIntegerMonoid()` | The ordered monoid of words over the positive integers. |
| `Id(O)` / `O ! 1` | The identity element (null word) of ordered monoid `O`. |
| `O . i` | The `i`-th generator of `O`. |
| `O ! [w₁, …, w_n]` | The word `w₁ ⋯ w_n` from a sequence of elements of `O`. |
| `O ! [i₁, …, i_n]` | For `O` over the positive integers: the word `i₁ ⋯ i_n` from a sequence of integers. |
| `w1 eq w2` | True if words `w1`, `w2` from the same ordered monoid are equal. |
| `w1 * w2` | Product (concatenation) of two words from the same ordered monoid. |
| `IsKnuthEquivalent(w1, w2)` | True if `w1` and `w2` (from the same monoid) can be transformed into one another by elementary Knuth transformations. |
| `w[i]` | The `i`-th generator in the product expressing word `w`. |
| `ElementToSequence(w)` / `Eltseq(w)` | For a word `w` over the positive integers: `w` as a sequence of integers. |
| `Length(w)` / `#w` | The length of word `w`. |
| `Content(w)` | A sequence of non-negative integers whose `i`-th entry is the number of occurrences of the `i`-th generator in `w`. |
| `IsReverseLatticeWord(w)` | True if `w` is a reverse lattice (Yamanouchi) word: for every `n > 0`, the content of the last `n` letters of `w` is a partition. |
| `MaximalIncreasingSequence(w)` | A weakly increasing subsequence of `w` of maximal length (not necessarily unique). |
| `MaximalIncreasingSequences(w, k)` | A sequence of `k` distinct increasing subsequences of `w` using the maximal number of entries of `w`; empty sequences are returned once all entries are used (not necessarily unique). |

*Worked examples:* H145E3 (creating words over the positive integers; `Eltseq`, indexing, product); H145E4 (finitely generated ordered monoid with named generators); H145E5 (`MaximalIncreasingSequence`/`MaximalIncreasingSequences`).

### 145.3.2 Plactic Monoids

The *plactic monoid* of an ordered monoid is the quotient defined by Knuth equivalence. Its
elements are equivalence classes of words, represented by a canonical representative.

| Intrinsic | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| `PlacticMonoid(O)` | The plactic monoid obtained by factoring ordered monoid `O` by Knuth equivalence. |
| `PlacticIntegerMonoid()` | The plactic monoid obtained by factoring the ordered monoid over the positive integers by Knuth equivalence. |
| `OrderedMonoid(P)` | For a plactic monoid `P`: the ordered monoid on which `P` is based. |
| `Id(P)` / `P ! 1` | The identity element (null word) of plactic monoid `P`. |
| `P . i` | The `i`-th generator of `P`. |
| `P ! [u₁, …, u_n]` | The product `u₁ ⋯ u_n` of a sequence of elements of `P`. |
| `P ! [i₁, …, i_n]` | For `P` over the positive integers: the element corresponding to the Knuth equivalence class of `i₁ ⋯ i_n`. |
| `P ! w` | For a word `w` from the ordered monoid `P` is based on: the element of `P` corresponding to the Knuth equivalence class of `w`. |
| `P ! [w₁, …, w_n]` | For a sequence of elements of the underlying ordered monoid: the element of `P` for the class of `w₁ ⋯ w_n`. |
| `P ! t` | For a plactic monoid `P` and a tableau `t` over the same ordered monoid: the element of `P` uniquely associated to `t`. |
| `u1 eq u2` | True if elements `u1`, `u2` of the same plactic monoid are equal. |
| `u1 * u2` | Product of `u1`, `u2` (inherited from word concatenation in the ordered monoid). |
| `Length(u)` / `#u` | The length of plactic-monoid element `u` (invariant under Knuth equivalence). |
| `Content(u)` | The content of `u` (sequence of generator-occurrence counts; invariant under Knuth equivalence). |

*Worked examples:* H145E6 (ordered vs plactic monoid over the integers; Knuth-equivalent and non-equivalent words); H145E7 (Length/Content invariance under Knuth equivalence).

## 145.4 Tableaux

The tableaux module is loosely based on SYMMETRICA (Kerber and Kohnert) **[KKL92]**; the main
theoretical reference is Fulton's *Young Tableaux* **[Ful97]**.

A Young (Ferrers) diagram is a collection of cells in left-justified rows with weakly decreasing
row lengths; the row lengths form a partition (the *shape*) of `n`, the number of cells. A
numbering assigns a label (a generator of some ordered monoid) to each cell. A Young *tableau*
is a numbering with weakly increasing rows and strictly increasing columns; tableaux form a
monoid under a product defined either by Schensted **row insertion** ("bumping") or by
Schützenberger's **jeu de taquin** ("sliding"). Flipping a diagram over its main diagonal gives
the conjugate diagram (the `ConjugatePartition` of the shape). A skew (inner) shape is obtained
by deleting a smaller Young diagram from inside a larger one; a skew tableau is a numbering on a
skew diagram obeying the same restrictions.

### 145.4.1 Tableau Monoids

For an ordered monoid `O`, the generators of `O` serve as labels for a family of tableaux `M`,
the *tableau monoid*. Its only defining characteristic is the ordered monoid specifying its
labels. The tableau monoid and the plactic monoid over the same ordered monoid are isomorphic.

| Intrinsic | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| `TableauMonoid(O)` / `TableauMonoid(P)` | Given an ordered monoid `O` or a plactic monoid `P` (with `O` the ordered monoid `P` is based on): the tableau monoid of tableaux whose labels are the generators of `O`. |
| `TableauIntegerMonoid()` | The tableau monoid over the positive integers. |
| `OrderedMonoid(M)` | For a tableau monoid `M`: the ordered monoid from which `M` gets its labels. |

*Worked examples:* H145E8 (tableau monoid over the positive integers; image of a word as a tableau); H145E9 (tableau monoid over a finite label set).

### 145.4.2 Creation of Tableaux

Tableaux are created from the words of some ordered monoid. Over the positive integers the rows
can be input directly as sequences; words can specify rows exactly, or an entire word can be
mapped to a tableau by row insertion.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `Tableau(Q)` | For a sequence `Q` of sequences of non-negative integers: the tableau with the elements of `Q` as its rows (zeroes indicate skew entries). Must have weakly increasing rows and strictly increasing columns. | Direct construction. |
| `Tableau(Q)` | For a sequence `Q` of words from some ordered monoid: the (non-skew) tableau with the words of `Q` as its rows. Must have weakly increasing rows and strictly increasing columns. | Direct construction. |
| `Tableau(S, Q)` | For a partition `S` and a sequence `Q` of sequences of positive integers: a skew tableau with inner shape `S` and the non-skew part of each row given by the entries of `Q`. Must satisfy the tableau row/column conditions. | Direct construction. |
| `Tableau(S, Q)` | For a partition `S` and a sequence `Q` of words from some ordered monoid: a skew tableau with inner shape `S` and rows given by the words of `Q`. | Direct construction. |
| `WordToTableau(w)` / `WordToTableau(u)` | For a word `w` from an ordered monoid (or an element `u` of a plactic monoid): the tableau obtained by row-inserting the entries of `w` (from the left) into the empty tableau. For `u`, any word of its Knuth class is used (well defined). | Schensted **row insertion** ("bumping"); a surjective homomorphism from the ordered monoid to the tableau monoid and an isomorphism from the plactic to the tableau monoid. |
| `M ! w` | For a tableau monoid `M` and a word `w` from its ordered monoid: the tableau corresponding to `w` through row insertion. | Row insertion. |
| `M ! u` | For a tableau monoid `M` and an element `u` of the associated plactic monoid: the tableau corresponding to `u` through row insertion. | Row insertion. |
| `M ! [i₁, …, i_n]` | For `M` over the positive integers: the tableau corresponding to the word `i₁ * ⋯ * i_n`. | Row insertion. |
| `M ! Q` (sequences) | For `M` over the positive integers and a sequence `Q` of sequences of non-negative integers: the tableau with the elements of `Q` as its rows (zeroes indicate skew entries). Must satisfy the row/column conditions. | Direct construction. |
| `M ! Q` (words) | For a tableau monoid `M` and a sequence `Q` of words from its ordered monoid: the (non-skew) tableau with the words of `Q` as rows. | Direct construction. |

*Worked examples:* H145E10 (build a tableau from explicit rows, from a single word via `WordToTableau`, and a skew tableau via zero entries); H145E11 (skew tableau in a tableau monoid with finitely many labels); H145E12 (natural maps between the ordered, plactic and tableau monoids; Knuth equivalence ⇒ equality of images).

### 145.4.3 Enumeration of Tableaux

Multiple tableaux may be created corresponding to specified parameters. In each pair of
signatures, if the tableau monoid `M` is omitted the monoid over the positive integers is used.

| Intrinsic | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| `StandardTableaux(P)` / `StandardTableaux(M, P)` | The set of all standard tableaux from tableau monoid `M` of shape `P` (a partition). If `M` is given it must contain more than `Weight(P)` labels. |
| `StandardTableauxOfWeight(n)` / `StandardTableauxOfWeight(M, n)` | The set of all standard tableaux from `M` of weight `n` (positive integer). If `M` is given it must contain at least `n` labels. |
| `TableauxOfShape(S, m)` / `TableauxOfShape(M, S, m)` | The set of all tableaux of shape `S` (a partition) using only the first `m` labels of `M`. If `M` is given it must contain at least `m` labels. |
| `TableauxOnShapeWithContent(S, C)` / `TableauxOnShapeWithContent(M, S, C)` | The set of all tableaux from `M` having shape `S` (a partition) and content `C` (non-negative integers; see §145.4.6). If `C` prescribes fewer cells than fill `S`, the tableau within the given shape is returned. If `M` is given it must have at least as many labels as `C` has entries. |
| `TableauxWithContent(C)` / `TableauxWithContent(M, C)` | The set of all tableaux from `M` with content `C` (non-negative integers; see §145.4.6). If `M` is given it must have at least as many labels as `C` has entries. |

*Worked examples:* H145E13 (`StandardTableaux` of shape `[3,2]`, count checked against `NumberOfStandardTableaux`); H145E14 (`TableauxOnShapeWithContent`).

### 145.4.4 Random Tableaux

The functions in this section are based on ideas from **[GNW84]**.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `RandomHookWalk(P, i, j)` / `RandomHookWalk(t, i, j)` | For a Young diagram given by partition `P` or tableau `t`, and `(i, j)` on the shape: perform a random hook walk starting at cell `(i, j)`, repeatedly moving to a random cell of the current cell's hook until an outside corner is reached; return the coordinates of that final cell. The *hook* of a cell is the cells to its right and below. | Random **hook walk** **[GNW84]** (basic tool for random tableau creation). |
| `RandomTableau(S)` / `RandomTableau(M, S)` | A random standard tableau from `M` of shape `S` (a partition). If `M` is given it must contain more than `Weight(S)` labels. | Hook-walk construction **[GNW84]**. |
| `RandomTableau(n)` / `RandomTableau(M, n)` | A (not completely) random standard tableau from `M` of weight `n`. The probability of a given shape `S` is `N_S / n!` (`N_S` = number of standard tableaux of shape `S`), so more populous shapes occur more often. If `M` is given it must contain at least `n` labels. | Hook-walk construction **[GNW84]**. |

*Worked example:* H145E15 (numerical test that random shape-`[4,3,2]` tableaux are nearly uniform; average percentage difference ≈ 0.6%).

### 145.4.7 Operations

Several operations manipulate tableaux and let them interact.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `t1 eq t2` | True if tableaux `t1`, `t2` are equal. | — |
| `t1 * t2` | The product tableau of `t1` and `t2`, definable by either row insertion or jeu de taquin. | Row insertion / jeu de taquin (equivalent). |
| `DiagonalSum(t1, t2)` | Build a rectangle of empty cells with as many columns as `t1` and as many rows as `t2`; attach `t1` below it and `t2` to its right, giving a skew tableau. | Diagonal placement. |
| `Conjugate(t)` | For a tableau `t` with strictly increasing rows: the tableau on the conjugate Young diagram obtained by transposing rows and columns. | Transposition. |
| `JeuDeTaquin(∼t, i, j)` / `JeuDeTaquin(t, i, j)` | For a skew tableau `t` and an *inside corner* `(i, j)` of its skew shape: remove the skewed `(i, j)` cell using *jeu de taquin*. | Schützenberger **jeu de taquin** ("sliding"). |
| `JeuDeTaquin(∼t)` / `JeuDeTaquin(t)` / `Rectify(∼t)` / `Rectify(t)` | Remove all skewed cells successively using jeu de taquin (rectification). | Jeu de taquin (rectification). |
| `InverseJeuDeTaquin(∼t, i, j)` / `InverseJeuDeTaquin(t, i, j)` | For a tableau `t` and coordinates `(i, j)` on its outside resulting in a valid shape: add a new skew cell at `(i, j)` and perform jeu de taquin in reverse, producing a skew tableau of skew degree one higher than `t`. | Reverse jeu de taquin. |
| `RowInsert(∼t, w)` / `RowInsert(t, w)` / `RowInsert(∼t, u)` / `RowInsert(t, u)` | Insert a word `w` (from the ordered monoid `t` is associated to) or a plactic-monoid element `u` into `t` by Schensted row insertion. For `u`, any word of its Knuth class is used (well defined). | Schensted **row insertion**. |
| `RowInsert(∼t, x)` / `RowInsert(t, x)` | For a tableau `t` over the positive integers and a positive integer `x`: insert `x` into `t` by Schensted row insertion. | Schensted row insertion. |
| `InverseRowInsert(∼t, i, j)` / `InverseRowInsert(t, i, j)` | For `(i, j)` an outside corner of `t`: apply row insertion in reverse to remove that entry. The functional form returns both the new tableau and the removed entry (as a word of an ordered monoid). | Reverse row insertion. |

*Worked examples:* H145E19 (order of jeu de taquin slides is irrelevant to the rectified result); H145E20 (multiplying tableaux via row insertion vs `DiagonalSum`+`Rectify`; both equal `t1*t2`).

### 145.4.8 The Robinson-Schensted-Knuth Correspondence

The plactic-to-tableaux map is an isomorphism, but many words map to the same tableau. A
bijective map exists between the ordered monoid and *pairs* of tableaux of the same shape, the
second standard — the **Robinson correspondence**. Dropping the standardness restriction extends
it to a bijection with all matrices of non-negative integers — the **Robinson–Schensted–Knuth
(RSK)** correspondence — which can also be made bijective with all lexicographically ordered
pairs of words of equal length.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `LexicographicalOrdering(∼w1, ∼w2)` / `LexicographicalOrdering(w1, w2)` | Place two words `w1`, `w2` of equal length in increasing lexicographical order (functional version returns two new words). The pairs `(w1[i], w2[i])` are ordered primarily by `w2[i] < w2[j]`, and on ties by `w1[i] < w1[j]`. | Lexicographic sort of pairs. |
| `IsLexicographicallyOrdered(w1, w2)` | True if the pairs `(w1[i], w2[i])` are ordered as in `LexicographicalOrdering`. | — |
| `RSKCorrespondence(w)` | For a word `w`: the two tableaux `t1`, `t2` of the RSK bijection. `t1` is labelled by the entries of `w` (built by row insertion, as in `WordToTableau`); `t2` (the insertion/recording tableau) is standard over the positive integers — when `w[k]` adds a cell to `t1`, the same cell is added to `t2` labelled `k`. | **RSK** via row insertion with simultaneous recording tableau **[Ful97]**. |
| `InverseRSKCorrespondenceSingleWord(t1, t2)` | For tableaux `t1`, `t2` of the same shape with `t2` over the positive integers: the preimage of the single-word RSK map, returned as one word of the ordered monoid of `t1`. | Inverse RSK (single word). |
| `RSKCorrespondence(w1, w2)` | For lexicographically ordered words `w1`, `w2` of equal length with `w2` over the positive integers: the two tableaux `t1` (labelled by `w1`) and `t2` (labelled by `w2`), where the cell added to `t1` by `w1[k]` is added to `t2` labelled `w2[k]`. | RSK (double word). |
| `InverseRSKCorrespondenceDoubleWord(t1, t2)` | For tableaux `t1`, `t2` of the same shape with `t2` over the positive integers: the preimage of the double-word RSK map, returned as words of the ordered monoids of `t1` and `t2`. | Inverse RSK (double word). |
| `RSKCorrespondence(M)` | For a matrix `M` of non-negative integers: the two tableaux `t1`, `t2` over the positive integers. For the `(i, j)`-th entry `m_{ij}`, append `i` to a word `w1` `m_{ij}` times and `j` to `w2` `m_{ij}` times, then apply the word-pair RSK. | RSK (matrix). |
| `InverseRSKCorrespondenceMatrix(t1, t2)` | For tableaux `t1`, `t2` of the same shape, both over the positive integers: the preimage of the matrix RSK map, a matrix of non-negative integers. | Inverse RSK (matrix). |

*Worked examples:* H145E21 (two words with the same tableau-monoid image but distinct RSK insertion tableaux; inverse single-word check); H145E22 (double-word RSK on words from a finitely generated and the integer monoid); H145E23 (matrix RSK; recovering `(i,j)` multiplicities); H145E24 (permutation `p` ↔ pair `(T1, T2)` iff `p⁻¹` ↔ `(T2, T1)`).

### 145.4.9 Counting Tableaux

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `NumberOfStandardTableaux(P)` | For a partition `P`: the number of standard tableaux of shape `P` (equal to the number of Knuth-equivalent words for each such standard tableau). | Hook-length formula (cf. **[Ful97]**). |
| `NumberOfStandardTableauxOnWeight(n)` | For a positive integer `n`: the number of standard tableaux of weight `n`. | Sum over shapes of weight `n`. |
| `NumberOfTableauxOnAlphabet(P, m)` | For a partition `P`: the number of tableaux of shape `P` with entries from `[1, …, m]`. | — |
| `KostkaNumber(S, C)` | For a partition `S` and a sequence of non-negative integers `C`: the number of tableaux of shape `S` and content `C` (the Kostka number). If `C` prescribes fewer cells than fill `S`, the count within the given shape is returned. | Kostka number. |

*Worked example:* H145E25 (count of words mapping to a fixed standard tableau equals `NumberOfStandardTableaux` of its shape).

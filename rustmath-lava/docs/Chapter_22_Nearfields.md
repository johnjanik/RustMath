## Scope and overview

Nearfields are algebraic structures that satisfy all the axioms of a field except for the
commutativity of multiplication and the left distributive law. The subject was initiated by
L. E. Dickson in 1905 **[Dic05a, Dic05b]**, who introduced the first example (a 9-element
system) and subsequently an infinite series of examples obtained by twisting the multiplication
of a Galois field. The term "nearfield" was introduced by Zassenhaus **[Zas35]**, who also showed
that the only finite nearfields are those due to Dickson together with exactly seven irregular
exceptions.

Magma implements only right-distributive nearfields: axioms require `(N, +)` to be an abelian
group, `(N×, ◦)` to be a group, `a ◦ 0 = 0 ◦ a = 0`, and the right distributive law
`(a + b) ◦ c = a ◦ c + b ◦ c` for all elements. The **prime field** P(N) is the
sub-nearfield generated by 1; for finite nearfields it is a Galois field F_p and the
characteristic of N is p. The **kernel** K(N) is the set of elements x satisfying the left
distributive law `x(y + z) = xy + xz`; it is a subfield of N. The **centre** Z(N) ⊆ K(N) is
the set of multiplicatively central elements.

Nearfields connect three areas of mathematics:

- **Group theory:** the finite sharply doubly transitive permutation groups are in one-to-one
  correspondence with the finite nearfields via the affine group of transformations `v ↦ va + b`.
- **Geometry:** nearfields coordinatise a class of translation planes **[Hal59, Lün69]** and are
  the starting point for the Hughes plane construction **[Dem71, Hug57]**.
- **Linear representations:** every sharply transitive collineation group of projective space
  over a finite field is a quotient of the unit group of a nearfield **[EK63]**.

Magma provides types `Nfd` (virtual), `NfdDck` (Dickson/regular), and `NfdZss` (Zassenhaus/
irregular), together with construction, arithmetic, group, automorphism, and plane functions.

## 22.2 Nearfield Properties

Defines the axioms of a right-distributive nearfield and the key substructures: sub-nearfields,
prime field P(N), kernel K(N), and centre Z(N). Includes the identity
`Z(N) = ∩{ K(N)x | x ∈ N×}`. For Dickson nearfields, Z(N) = K(N).

### 22.2.1 Sharply Doubly Transitive Groups

Describes the bijection between finite nearfields and finite sharply doubly transitive
permutation groups. If G acts sharply doubly transitively on Ω then the fixed-point-free
elements together with the identity form an elementary abelian normal p-subgroup M of order
p^n, and Ω can be given nearfield structure so that G is the affine group of Ω. The minimal
faithful permutation degree equals p^n (the order of M). No intrinsics in this subsection;
the material motivates `UnitGroup` and `AffineGroup` in §22.6.

## 22.3 Constructing Nearfields

Two construction families cover all finite nearfields: the regular (Dickson) nearfields and
the seven irregular (Zassenhaus) nearfields.

### 22.3.1 Dickson Nearfields

A **Dickson triple** is a triple `(p, h, v)` with p prime and positive integers h, v such that
every prime factor of v divides `q − 1` (where `q = p^h`) and `q ≢ 3 (mod 4)` whenever
`4 | v`. The pair `(q, v)` is called a **Dickson pair**. For each Dickson pair there exist
`φ(v)/g` isomorphism classes of Dickson nearfields, where φ is Euler's totient and g is the
multiplicative order of p modulo v **[Lün71]**. A variant parameter s (coprime to v) selects
among these isomorphism classes. Addition in the Dickson nearfield is that of the underlying
Galois field F_{q^v}; multiplication is twisted by `w ◦ u = w^{ρ(u)} u` where ρ maps each
unit u to a power of the Frobenius automorphism.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `DicksonPairs(p, hlo, hhi, vlo, vhi)` | List of Dickson pairs `(q, v)` for prime p, with h in `[hlo, hhi]` and v in `[vlo, vhi]`. | Exhaustive check of the Dickson pair condition. |
| `DicksonPairs(p, h1, v1)` | List of Dickson pairs `(p^h, v)` for prime p, with h ≤ h1 and v ≤ v1. | Exhaustive check of the Dickson pair condition. |
| `DicksonTriples(p, hb, vb)` | List of Dickson triples `(p, h, v)` for prime p, with h ≤ hb and v ≤ vb. | Exhaustive check of the Dickson triple condition. |
| `NumberOfVariants(q, v)` | Number of non-isomorphic nearfields with Dickson pair `(q, v)`. | Formula φ(v)/g where g = ord_v(p) **[Lün71]**. |
| `NumberOfVariants(N)` | Number of variants of the Dickson nearfield N (i.e. non-isomorphic nearfields sharing N's Dickson pair). | As above applied to the pair of N. |
| `VariantRepresentatives(q, v)` | Representatives for the variant parameter s of nearfields with Dickson pair `(q, v)`. | Enumerates coset representatives coprime to v. |
| `DicksonNearfield(q, v : Variant := 1, LargeMatrices := false)` | Create a Dickson nearfield from the Dickson pair `(q, v)`. `Variant` selects the isomorphism class via an integer s coprime to v (converted internally to an exponent e coprime to `q^v − 1` with `s ≡ e (mod v)`). `LargeMatrices` controls whether the unit group is represented over the kernel (default, smaller matrices) or over the prime field (larger matrices). | Twisted-multiplication construction **[Dic05a, Dic05b]**; isomorphism classification **[Lün71]**. |

*Worked examples: H22E1 (listing Dickson pairs and triples for p = 5), H22E2 (variants for pair (625, 4)), H22E3 (constructing and comparing the two non-isomorphic nearfields with Dickson pair (625, 4)).*

### 22.3.2 Zassenhaus Nearfields

In addition to the regular nearfields, Zassenhaus **[Zas35]** showed there are exactly seven
irregular finite nearfields (not Dickson nearfields); existence and uniqueness proofs are
most reliably given in the PhD thesis of Dancs-Groves **[Gro74]**. These seven nearfields have
orders 5², 11², 7², 23², 11², 29², 59². Their multiplicative groups are not metacyclic
(whereas the multiplicative group of every Dickson nearfield is metacyclic), so no Zassenhaus
nearfield can appear as a sub-nearfield of a Dickson nearfield.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `ZassenhausNearfield(n)` | Creates the nth Zassenhaus nearfield, n ∈ {1, …, 7}. Orders are 25, 121, 49, 529, 121, 841, 3481. | Explicit construction from the seven irregular nearfields **[Zas35, Gro74]**. |

*Worked example: H22E4 (constructing all seven Zassenhaus nearfields and displaying their orders).*

## 22.4 Operations on Elements

### 22.4.1 Nearfield Arithmetic

Addition, subtraction, and negation are inherited from the underlying Galois field. Multiplication
is the defining nearfield operation (non-commutative; left distributive law may fail).

## 22.6 The Group of Units

The multiplicative group N× of a nearfield N of kernel K = F_q acts on the non-zero
vectors of the vector space N over K by right multiplication: each u ∈ N× gives a
K-linear, fixed-point-free transformation x ↦ xu. For the Dickson nearfield D(p, h, v, ζ)
with m = (q^v − 1)/v, the unit group U has generators a and b satisfying
`a^m = 1`, `b^v = a^t`, `b^{−1}ab = a^q` where q = p^h and t = m/(q − 1). The Sylow
2-subgroup of U is a generalised quaternion group if and only if `v ≡ 2 (mod 4)` and
`q ≡ 3 (mod 4)` **[EK64]**. The unit group representation defaults to matrices over the
kernel; setting `LargeMatrices := true` at construction time uses the prime field instead.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `UnitGroup(N)` | The unit group N× of the nearfield N, as a matrix group acting on N viewed as a vector space over its kernel (or prime field if `LargeMatrices` was set). | Fixed-point-free linear action; presentation from **[EK64]**. |
| `UnitGroup(GrpPerm, N)` | The unit group of N as a permutation group. | As above, permutation representation. |
| `UnitGroup(GrpPC, N)` | The unit group of N as a polycyclic group. | As above, PC representation. |
| `Order(x)` | The multiplicative order of the unit x in its nearfield. | — |
| `AffineGroup(N)` | The sharply two-transitive affine group associated with N (the group of transformations `v ↦ va + b`, a ∈ N×, b ∈ N), returned as a matrix group. All sharply two-transitive groups arise this way. | Fixed-point-free linear action of N× on N; affine extension. |
| `AffineGroup(GrpPerm, N)` | The affine group of N as a permutation group. | As above. |
| `AffineGroup(GrpPC, N)` | The affine group of N as a polycyclic group. | As above. |
| `ExtendedUnitGroup(D)` | The extended unit group of a Dickson nearfield D: the image of D× in the semidirect product Gal(K/F_p) × K×, where multiplication is `(γ₁a₁)(γ₂a₂) = γ₁γ₂ · a₁^{γ₂} a₂`. | Embedding via the Frobenius-index map ρ **[EK64]**. |

*Worked example: H22E7 (constructing the unit group of a sub-nearfield of a Dickson nearfield of order 3^{39}, demonstrating abelianness of the sub-nearfield's unit group).*

## 22.8 Nearfield Planes

Every finite nearfield N is **planar**: the map `x ↦ −xa + xb` is a bijection on N whenever
a ≠ b. From any finite nearfield N one constructs an affine plane with point set N × N and
lines `y = xm + b` (for each m, b ∈ N) and `x = c` (for each c ∈ N). The corresponding
projective plane P is obtained by adjoining a line at infinity. Points of P are labelled by
triples `[w, x, y]` of Galois field elements; lines by triples `[a, b, c]`; incidence is
`wa + xb + yc = 0`.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `ProjectivePlane(N : Check := false)` | The finite projective plane coordinatised by the nearfield N. Points are represented as triples of Galois field elements. `Check` (default false) enables consistency checking. | Nearfield coordinatisation; point and line labelling as described above. |

*Worked example: H22E8 (projective plane of order 9 from the Dickson nearfield of pair (3, 2); automorphism group of order 311040 and its composition factors).*

### 22.8.1 Hughes Planes

In 1957 Hughes **[Hug57]** discovered projective planes constructed from Dickson nearfields of
rank 2 over their kernel. These planes are neither translation planes nor their duals, so they
cannot be obtained by the coordinatisation method above. In 1960 Rosati **[Ros60]** generalised
the construction to include the Zassenhaus nearfields (see also **[Dem68, §5.4]** and
**[Dem71]**). The term "Hughes plane" is used in Magma to include both the original Hughes
planes and the generalised Hughes planes.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `HughesPlane(N : Check := false)` | The Hughes plane based on the nearfield N. `Check` (default false) enables consistency checking. | Construction of Hughes **[Hug57]** as generalised by Rosati **[Ros60]**; see also **[Dem68, Dem71]**. |

*Worked example: H22E9 (four projective planes of order 49: the Desarguesian plane PG(2,49), a Dickson nearfield plane, a Zassenhaus nearfield plane, and a Hughes plane; distinguished by the factored orders of their collineation groups).*

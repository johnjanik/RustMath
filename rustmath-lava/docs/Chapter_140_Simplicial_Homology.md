**Handbook part:** XVIII — Topology
**Handbook pages:** 4693–4709 (PDF pages 4824–4845)

## Scope and overview

This chapter presents Magma's category of finite **abstract simplicial complexes** and the
computation of their (simplicial) homology. An abstract simplicial complex `K` is defined as a
subset of the power set of some vertex set `V` with the property that if `S ∈ K` and `T ⊆ S`
then `T ∈ K` (closure under subsets). Complexes carry the category name `SmpCpx`; constructors
and internal functions guarantee the closure-under-subsets invariant is maintained.

Complexes may be defined over any `SetEnum`, but many construction methods operate only over
`SetEnum[RngIntElt]` — a complex built on subsets of the integers `1, 2, 3, …` is called
**normalized** in the handbook. `Normalization` relabels the vertices to `1, 2, 3, …`, which is
a prerequisite for several operations (topological sum, join, cone, suspension, homology).

The module supports: construction from face lists and from graphs (flag/clique complexes);
modification and recombination (subdivision, skeleton, prune, glue, add-simplex); standard
constructors of well-known topological objects (spheres, tori, Klein bottle, lens spaces,
projective plane); and a homology engine. The homology code computes **reduced** homology of a
complex with coefficients in an arbitrary ring; to obtain non-reduced homology one adds a single
free rank to dimension 0. From the chain complex it derives homology groups, Betti numbers,
torsion coefficients, the Euler characteristic, boundary matrices and explicit homology
generators.

For background the chapter refers to Hatcher **[Hat02]** and Armstrong **[Arm83]**.

A note on **degree vs dimension**: a face is said to have *degree* `n` if it has `n` elements as
a set, and *dimension* `n` if it has `n + 1` elements; so degree = dimension + 1.

## 140.2 Simplicial Complexes

Supports creation of simplicial complexes from lists of faces, from a few preprogrammed complex
types, and several standard techniques for modifying and recombining complexes.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `SimplicialComplex(f)` | Constructs an abstract simplicial complex with the faces in the list `f`. The argument should be a sequence of sets; there is no requirement on the element type, but several constructions require integer entries (see `Normalization`). | Subset closure of the given faces. |
| `SimplicialComplex(G)` | Constructs a 1-dimensional simplicial complex isomorphic to the graph `G`. | One edge/vertex per graph edge/vertex. |
| `FlagComplex(G)` / `CliqueComplex(G)` | Constructs a simplicial complex with an `n`-simplex for each `n`-clique in the graph `G`. | Clique enumeration of `G`. |
| `Dimension(X)` | Returns the dimension of the highest-dimensional face of the simplicial complex `X`. (A face with `n` elements has dimension `n − 1`.) | Maximum over face cardinalities. |
| `Faces(X, d)` | Returns the faces of degree `d` (number of elements `d`, one more than the dimension). The order returned for each degree is also the correspondence between faces and the basis of the corresponding module in the chain complex built from the complex. Returns an empty sequence if there are no faces of the requested degree. | Enumeration of degree-`d` subsets. |
| `Facets(X)` | Returns the facets of `X` — the faces that are not subsets of any other face. This is what the normal printing mode of a complex outputs. | Maximal-face selection. |

*Worked examples: H140E1 (listing facets vs all faces via `:Maximal`); H140E2 (`Dimension`); H140E3 (`Faces` of given degree, empty result for absent degree); H140E4 (`Facets` vs default printing).*

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `Normalization(X)` / `Normalization(~X, s)` | Relabels the vertices (elements of the face sets) of `X` using `1, 2, 3, …`, ensuring the complex is built on subsets of the integers — a prerequisite for several functions. The functional first signature copies `X` and returns a new complex; the procedural `~` signature modifies `X` in place. | Vertex renumbering. |
| `Shift(X, n)` / `Shift(~X, n)` | Shifts all integer vertices of the normalized complex `X` by an offset `n`. Functional `vs` procedural (`~`) signatures as above. | Adds `n` to every vertex label. |
| `Boundary(X)` | Returns the simplicial complex generated by all simplices lying in the boundary of `X`. Useful for building a complex representing the `n`-sphere (e.g. the boundary of an `(n+1)`-simplex). | Boundary-face generation. |
| `S + T` | Returns the topological sum (disjoint union) of two simplicial complexes. Requires both complexes to be normalized (see `Normalization`). | Relabelled disjoint union. |
| `S eq T` | Compares two simplicial complexes. Does **not** attempt to find an isomorphism; ensures the vertices lie in the same universe and then does a set comparison on the set of faces. (Shifting/relabelling breaks equality.) | Universe + face-set comparison. |
| `Product(S, T)` | Returns the cartesian product of two simplicial complexes. Works on any complexes; the new vertices are pairs of vertices from the component complexes. | Cartesian product of vertex sets / faces. |
| `Join(S, T)` / `S * T` | Constructs the join of two complexes: the complex generated by faces of the form `{x₁,…,xᵣ, y₁,…,yₛ}` for `{x₁,…,xᵣ} ∈ S` and `{y₁,…,yₛ} ∈ T`. Requires both complexes to be normalized. | Face-wise join. |

*Worked examples: H140E5 (`Boundary` of a 2-simplex, `Normalization` and `Shift` on string-labelled complex); H140E6 (`Boundary` of a 4-simplex / sphere); H140E7 (`S + T` disjoint union of two circles); H140E8 (`eq` semantics under shifting and isomorphic-but-differently-labelled circles); H140E9 (`Product` of two circles giving a torus); H140E10 (`Join` of two edges giving a 3-simplex).*

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `AddSimplex(X, s)` / `AddSimplex(~X, s)` | Adds either a single simplex `s` (a set) or a sequence of simplices to a simplicial complex. The functional versions return a new complex with the added simplices; the procedural (`~`) versions change `X` in place. | Insertion with subset closure. |
| `Prune(X, f)` / `Prune(~X, f)` | Removes a face `f` and all faces containing `f` from `X`. If `f` is not a face, `X` is returned unchanged. Functional `vs` procedural (`~`) signatures as above. | Star removal of `f`. |
| `Glue(X, e)` / `Glue(~X, e)` | In the complex `X`, identifies all vertices paired in the sequence `e` (a sequence of pairs of elements of the face sets). The identification eliminates the first component of each pair, replacing it with the second wherever it occurs in `X`. Functional `vs` procedural (`~`) signatures as above. | Vertex identification / quotient. |
| `BarycentricSubdivision(X)` | Constructs the barycentric subdivision of `X`: abstractly, the complex whose faces are chains `X₁ ⊂ … ⊂ Xₙ` of faces of `X`. The new complex has more faces but the same homotopy type as the old. | Order complex of the face poset. |
| `Skeleton(X, q)` | Returns the `q`-skeleton of `X`: the complex consisting of all faces of `X` of dimension at most `q`. | Truncation to dimension `q`. |
| `UnderlyingGraph(X)` | Constructs a graph isomorphic to the 1-skeleton of `X`. | Vertices and edges of the 1-skeleton. |
| `Cone(X)` | Constructs the cone over `X`: generated by all faces of `X` with an additional vertex included in each face. Any cone is acyclic (all homology groups vanish). Requires a normalized complex. | Adjoin apex vertex to every face. |
| `Suspension(X)` | Constructs the suspension (double cone) over the normalized complex `X`. The suspension shifts all homology groups up by one dimension. | Double-cone construction. |

*Worked examples: H140E11 (connected sum of two tori via `Product`, `Prune`, `+` and `Glue`); H140E12 (`Cone` and `Suspension` of a circle).*

### 140.2.1 Standard Topological Objects

Preprogrammed constructors for well-known topological spaces, returned as abstract simplicial
complexes.

| Intrinsic | Description |
|-----------|-------------|
| `Simplex(n)` | Returns the `n`-dimensional simplex as a simplicial complex. |
| `Sphere(n)` | Returns a simplicial complex triangulating the `n`-sphere. |
| `KleinBottle()` | Returns a triangulation of the Klein bottle as an abstract simplicial complex. |
| `Torus()` | Returns a triangulation of a torus as an abstract simplicial complex. |
| `Cylinder()` | Returns a triangulation of a cylinder as an abstract simplicial complex. |
| `MoebiusStrip()` | Returns a triangulation of the Möbius strip as an abstract simplicial complex. |
| `LensSpace(p)` | Constructs the lens space `L(p, 1)`, which has a 1-dimensional homology generator of order `p`. |
| `SimplicialProjectivePlane()` | Constructs a triangulation of the projective plane. |

*Usage of `LensSpace` and `SimplicialProjectivePlane` is demonstrated in Section 140.3 (homology computation).*

## 140.3 Homology Computation

The code computes exclusively **reduced** homology of the given simplicial complexes. To obtain
the non-reduced homology, add a single free rank to dimension 0 (generated by any single point of
the complex). The resulting homology modules are stored in falling dimension, always including
the dimension `−1` (vanishing) homology module at the very end.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `Homology(X)` / `Homology(~X)` / `Homology(X, A)` / `Homology(~X, A)` | Calculates the reduced homology of `X` with coefficients in the ring `A`. If no ring is given, defaults to integer coefficients. The procedural (`~`) form caches the results of the calculation in the complex object. Returns the homology modules (in falling dimension) and the connecting maps from the chain modules. | Reduced homology of the simplicial chain complex over `A`. |
| `HomologyGroup(X, q)` / `HomologyGroup(X, q, A)` | Calculates and returns the `q`-th homology group of `X` with coefficients in `A` (default integers). If homology is cached in `X`, the cached result is returned. Does **not** compute the entire homology in order to return one group. | Single-degree homology of the chain complex. |
| `BettiNumber(X, q)` / `BettiNumber(X, q, A)` | Returns the `q`-th Betti number, the free rank of the `q`-dimensional homology group with coefficients in `A` (default integers). Compensates for homology being reduced: `BettiNumber(X, 0) eq Rank(HomologyGroup(X, 0)) + 1`. | Free rank of `H_q`. |
| `TorsionCoefficients(X, q)` / `TorsionCoefficients(X, q, A)` | Returns the torsion coefficients of the `q`-th homology group of `X` with coefficients in `A` (default integers). | Torsion part of `H_q`. |
| `EulerCharacteristic(X)` | Computes the Euler characteristic of the complex. If homology is cached, it is used; otherwise the characteristic is computed from the ranks of the chain groups. | Alternating sum of homology ranks (cached) or chain-group ranks. |
| `BoundaryMatrix(X, q, A)` | Returns the `q`-th boundary matrix of the chain complex corresponding to `X` with coefficients in `A`. | Simplicial boundary operator `∂_q`. |
| `ChainComplex(X, A)` | Constructs a reduced chain complex of free `A`-modules corresponding to `X`. The produced complex includes one extra rank-1 module on each end, with the zero map leading to it, simulating the maps to/from the zero module that would terminate an ordinary simplicial chain complex. | Simplicial chain complex over `A`. |
| `HomologyGenerators(X)` / `HomologyGenerators(X, A)` / `HomologyGenerators(H, M, X)` | Prints generators of the homology groups of `X` with coefficients in `A`, together with their order, in order of dimension. The three-argument form expects `H, M` to be the result of `H, M := ChainComplex(A, smp);`. Recalculates homology each time unless already cached via `Homology(A, smp)`. Each generator is printed with a prefix giving its order (so torsion elements are recognizable). Defaults to integer coefficients. | Condensed bases of the homology groups with chain representatives. |

*Worked examples: H140E13 (`Homology` of a circle over the integers, falling-dimension storage); the lens space `LensSpace(3)` showing an order-3 torsion generator in `H₁`; H140E14 (`ChainComplex` / `BoundaryMaps` over Integers and `GF(3)`); H140E15 (`HomologyGenerators` of the three-holed torus, the projective plane's order-2 `H₁` generator, and verification of the `Cone` (acyclic) and `Suspension` (dimension-shift) claims).*

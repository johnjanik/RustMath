**Handbook part:** XIII — Lie Theory
**Handbook pages:** 2977–3059 (PDF pages 3110–3193)

## Scope and overview

Chapter 100 is the central chapter on finite-dimensional Lie algebras in Magma. A Lie algebra is viewed as a free module over a base ring R with a bilinear multiplication satisfying skew-symmetry and the Jacobi identity. The chapter introduces two principal representations:

- **Structure constant algebras** — the n-dimensional Lie algebra L over R is stored as n³ structure constants a^k_{ij} ∈ R (1 ≤ i, j, k ≤ n) satisfying e_i ∗ e_j = Σ_k a^k_{ij} e_k. Three internal storage formats are supported: Dense (n² vectors of length n), Sparse (only non-zero quadruples ⟨i, j, k, a^k_{ij}⟩), and Partial (vectors with recorded non-zero positions).
- **Matrix Lie algebras** — elements are matrices with the commutator bracket.

Many structural algorithms require the base ring to be a field. The chapter also covers finitely-presented Lie algebras (free Lie algebras modulo a set of relations, §100.3), Lie algebras generated by extremal elements (§100.4), and several important families: almost reductive, Cartan-type (Witt, Special, Hamiltonian, Contact), and Melikian algebras (§§100.5.1–100.5.3).

Key structural tools provided include: Cartan and splitting toral subalgebras, root systems and root data, Chevalley bases, composition series, derived/lower/upper central series, the Killing form, and a full suite of isomorphism-testing routines. For semisimple Lie algebras the chapter documents root system recognition using **[dG00]** (characteristic 0) and the algorithm of Roozemond **[Roo10]** (characteristics 2 and 3), Chevalley basis computation using Cohen–Roozemond **[CR09]** for small characteristics, and split Cartan subalgebra computation using Cohen–Murray **[CM09]**.

Beyond structure, the chapter covers: automorphisms of classical-type reductive algebras (§100.15), restricted/restrictable Lie algebras and p-maps (§100.16), universal enveloping algebras with the Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt basis and the integral form using Kostant's basis **[Kos66]** (§100.17), databases of solvable Lie algebras (dimensions 2–4, classification from **[dG05]**) and nilpotent Lie algebras (dimensions 3–6, classification from **[dG07]**) with identification functions (§100.18), semisimple subalgebras of simple Lie algebras **[dG11]** (§100.19), and nilpotent orbits in simple Lie algebras (via the Collingwood–McGovern classification **[CM93]**, §100.20).

Related chapters: 101 (Kac–Moody Lie algebras), 102 (Quantum Groups), 104 (representations of Lie algebras and groups of Lie type, esp. §104.3.1 on constructing representations of Lie algebras).

## 100.2 Constructors for Lie Algebras

The constructors for structure-constant Lie algebras are identical to those for general structure-constant algebras. Two optional parameters apply throughout:

- `Check` (BoolElt, default `true`): verifies the Lie axioms on construction.
- `Rep` (MonStgElt, default `"Dense"`): selects the internal representation (`"Dense"`, `"Sparse"`, or `"Partial"`).

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `LieAlgebra< R, n \| Q : parameters >` | Creates the structure-constant Lie algebra over the free module R^n with basis {e_1,…,e_n}. Q is the n³ structure-constant data in one of three formats: (i) n sequences of n sequences of length n; (ii) n² sequences of length n; (iii) a flat sequence of n³ ring elements in order a^1_{11}, a^2_{11},…,a^n_{nn}. | Direct construction from structure constants; Lie axiom check if `Check := true`. |
| `LieAlgebra< R, n \| T : parameters >` | Creates the Lie algebra over R^n from sparse data: T is a sequence of quadruples ⟨i, j, k, a^k_{ij}⟩ giving non-zero structure constants; all others are 0. | Same as above, sparse input. |
| `LieAlgebra< t \| T : parameters >` | Creates a Lie algebra over the integers (a Lie ring). t is a sequence of n non-negative integers giving the moduli of the basis elements (t_i · e_i = 0; t_i = 0 means no torsion). T is as above. | Integer structure-constant algebra with torsion. |
| `LieAlgebra(A)` (associative structure-constant algebra A) | Creates the Lie algebra L with bracket (x,y) ↦ x∗y − y∗x from the associative algebra A. Also returns the identifying map L → A. | Commutator bracket from associative product. |
| `LieAlgebra(A)` (associative matrix algebra A) | Creates a structure-constant Lie algebra isomorphic to the matrix algebra A equipped with the commutator bracket. | Commutator bracket. |
| `AbelianLieAlgebra(R, n)` | Creates the abelian Lie algebra of dimension n over R (all structure constants zero). Default `Rep := "Sparse"`. | — |
| `ChangeBasis(L, B)` | Creates a new structure-constant Lie algebra L′ isomorphic to L by recomputing structure constants with respect to basis B (a set/sequence of elements, vectors, or a matrix). Returns L′ and the isomorphism L → L′. | Change-of-basis recomputation of structure constants. |
| `MatrixLieAlgebra(R, n)` | Creates the full matrix Lie algebra of n×n matrices over R with commutator bracket. | — |
| `MatrixLieAlgebra(A)` | Creates the matrix Lie algebra from an associative matrix algebra A with commutator bracket. | — |
| `Algebra(M)` / `LieAlgebra(M)` | Returns a structure-constant Lie algebra isomorphic to the matrix Lie algebra M. | — |

*Worked examples: H100E1 (Heisenberg Lie algebra via quadruples; Lie algebra from GF(27) algebra; Lie ring over integers with torsion moduli [0,4] and its lower central series); H100E2 (subalgebra of 2×2 matrix Lie algebra, upper-triangular, solvability tests).*

## 100.3 Finitely Presented Lie Algebras

A finitely presented Lie algebra is the quotient of a free Lie algebra on n generators {x_1,…,x_n} over a base ring F by the ideal generated by a set of relations. Magma works in the free non-associative anticommutative algebra (using skew-symmetry (a,b) = −(b,a) and (a,a) = 0 to reduce elements, but not the Jacobi identity), ordered by a degree-lexicographic ordering on the free magma M(X).

### 100.3.1 Construction of the Free Lie Algebra

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `FreeLieAlgebra(F, n)` | Creates the free n-generator Lie algebra over the ring F. The generators are ordered (first generator is largest). Angle-bracket notation can assign names. | Free (non-associative anticommutative) algebra; degree-lexicographic ordering on M(X). |

*Worked example: H100E3 (free Lie algebra on 3 generators over GF(2)).*

### 100.3.2 Properties of the Free Lie Algebra

|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `Rank(L)` | Number of generators of the free Lie algebra L. | — |
| `CoefficientRing(L)` / `BaseRing(L)` | The coefficient ring of L. | — |

### 100.3.3 Operations on Elements of the Free Lie Algebra

Elements of the free Lie algebra are formal bracketed expressions. Any bracket (a,b) with a > b is rewritten as −(b,a); (a,a) rewrites to 0.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `x + y` / `x - y` / `x * y` | Addition, subtraction, and Lie bracket (multiplication) of free Lie elements. | Normal form in the free anticommutative algebra. |
| `L ! 0` / `Zero(L)` | The zero element of the free Lie algebra L. | — |
| `IsLeaf(m)` | For a monomial element m: returns true if m is a generator (leaf), plus its index i such that m = L.i. If false, also returns a, b ∈ L with m a scalar multiple of (a,b) (note: m need not equal (a,b); retrieve the scalar via `LeadingCoefficient(m)`). | Structural test on the expression tree. |

*Worked examples: H100E4 (bracket computations, rewriting, Jacobi-like identities); H100E5 (`IsLeaf`, recovering bracket factors).*

### 100.3.4 Construction of a Finitely-Presented Lie Algebra

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `LieAlgebra(R)` | Given a set or sequence R of elements of a free Lie algebra L (the relations), constructs the structure-constant Lie algebra K isomorphic to L/I (where I = ideal generated by R), assuming L/I is finite-dimensional. Over a field, returns (K, G, B, f): K is the quotient, G is the grading data (list of [degree, dim] pairs if relations are homogeneous, else empty), B is a sequence of free-algebra elements mapping to a basis of K, and f : B×B → L gives products mod I. Over the integers, returns (K, G, [], f) where f : K → L is a section of the projection. | Basis computation for the quotient, described in **[dG00]** §7.4. |
| `quo< L \| R >` | Constructs the structure-constant Lie algebra K ≅ L/I (I generated by the relations R), and also returns an invertible map from L to K. | As `LieAlgebra(R)` above. |
| `NilpotentQuotient(R, d)` | Constructs the class-d nilpotent quotient of the Lie algebra L/I: adds to I all elements of degree > d (so the quotient is finite-dimensional and nilpotent of class ≤ d). Returns the same four values as `LieAlgebra`. `d` may be `Infinity()`. | Quotient computed in the free nilpotent Lie algebra of class d; same method as **[dG00]** §7.4. |

*Worked examples: H100E6 (positive-root subalgebra of E7, dimension 63, computing basis element of degree 16); H100E7 (finitely presented Lie ring over the integers, dimension 8, torsion moduli, lower central series with 11 terms); H100E8 (`quo<>` to construct A1, preimage of Chevalley basis); H100E9 (`NilpotentQuotient` of class 10, dimension 109, grading sequence).*

## 100.4 Lie Algebras Generated by Extremal Elements

A non-zero element x of a Lie algebra L over a field K is **extremal** if [x,[x,y]] ∈ Kx for all y ∈ L, equivalently if a linear map f_x : L → K exists with [x,[x,y]] = f_x(y)x. Given a connected undirected finite graph Γ with n vertices, F(K,Γ) is the quotient of the free Lie algebra over K generated by the vertices modulo [x_i, x_j] = 0 for non-adjacent vertices. For f ∈ (F*)^Π the algebra L(K,Γ,f) is the further quotient by the ideal generated by {[x,[x,y]] − f_x(y)x}. The "sandwich" case L(0) is finite-dimensional (**[ZK90]**), and dim(L(f)) ≤ dim(L(0)) for all f (**[CSUW01]**). The parameter space X = {f | dim(L(f)) = dim(L(0))} is studied. Details in **[Roo11]**. The type is `AlgLieExtr`; verbose flag `"AlgLieExtr"` may be set 1–5.

### 100.4.1 Constructing Lie Algebras Generated by Extremal Elements

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `ExtremalLieAlgebra(K, n)` | Constructs the Lie algebra over K generated by n extremal elements. `CommGens` (SeqEnum, default []): pairs (i,j) declaring [x_i, x_j] = 0. `HeisenbergPairs` (SeqEnum, default []): pairs (i,j) declaring f_{x_i}(b_j) = 0. Characteristic of K must be ≠ 2. | Construction of L(0) = L(K,Γ,0); framework of **[Roo11]**, algorithm due to W. de Graaf. |
| `ExtremalLieAlgebra(K, G)` | Constructs the Lie algebra over K with \|V(G)\| extremal generators described by the graph G (x_i, x_j commute iff not adjacent). `HeisenbergPairs` as above. | Same as above. |

*Worked example: H100E11 (two constructions of the same K_{2,2}-generated algebra of dimension 15 over Q; graph printout; CommGens vs. bipartite graph input).*

## 100.5 Families of Lie Algebras

### 100.5.1 Almost Reductive Lie Algebras

A Lie algebra is **reductive** if its radical equals its centre. An **almost reductive** Lie algebra corresponds to the Lie algebra of a reductive algebraic group. The constructors accept the Lie type T in five forms: (a) a Cartan-type string (e.g. `"A3"`, `"G2 B3"`); (b) a root datum (Chapter 97); (c) a crystallographic root system (Chapter 96); (d) a Dynkin diagram (§95.5); (e) a crystallographic Cartan matrix (§95.4). For forms (a), (d), (e), the optional parameter `Isogeny` is accepted (see §103.2 for values).

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `LieAlgebra(T, k)` | Constructs the reductive Lie algebra of type T over the ring k. `Isogeny` (default `"Ad"`): isogeny type. For type An in characteristic p\|(n+1), different isogenies produce non-isomorphic algebras (see **[Hog82]**). | Structure constants derived from the root datum/Cartan type. |
| `MatrixLieAlgebra(T, k)` | Constructs the reductive matrix Lie algebra of type T over k in the standard (smallest-degree) matrix representation. `Isogeny` (default `"Ad"`). | Standard matrix representation of the root datum. |
| `LieAlgebra(N, k, p)` / `LieAlgebra(R, k, p)` | The twisted (almost) semisimple Lie algebra over the finite field k with Cartan type N (string) or root datum R, with twist given by permutation p of simple root indices (or of all root indices). | Twisted structure constants from the Frobenius twist. |
| `TwistedLieAlgebra(R, k)` | Given a twisted root datum R and finite field k, constructs the twisted Lie algebra L = R(k). Returns 5 values: L; a homomorphism φ : L → L′ into the split Lie algebra L′ over a suitable extension of k; L′; a split toral subalgebra H of L; a split toral subalgebra H′ of L′ with φ(H) ⊆ H′. See also `TwistedBasis`. | Twisted Chevalley construction. |

*Worked examples: H100E17 (constructing D7/Q, G2/GF(5), G2⊕B3/Q, E8/GF(2)); H100E18 (Isogeny option for A4/GF(5): "Ad" vs "SC" vs intermediate isogeny; E6/GF(3) from root datum); H100E19 (twisted E6 over GF(5); twisted D4 with 3-twist over GF(7), verifying split toral subalgebras).*

### 100.5.2 Cartan-Type Lie Algebras

Over fields of characteristic p ≥ 7, the only simple non-classical Lie algebras are the Cartan-type algebras (for p = 5 Melikian algebras also occur; for p = 2, 3 classification is incomplete). They arise from infinite-dimensional algebras of differential operators over C. The notation follows Strade–Farnsteiner **[SF88]** and Strade **[Str04]**.

Let F be a finite field of characteristic p > 0, m ≥ 1. Let O(m,n) = ⟨x^(a) | 0 ≤ a_i < p^{n_i}⟩, dimension p^N where N = Σ n_i. The Witt algebra W(m,n) = Σ O(m)∂_i has dimension mp^N. S(m,n), CS(m,n), H(m,n), CH(m,n), K(m,n) are defined as subalgebras of W(m,n) via differential-form conditions.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `WittLieAlgebra(F, m, n)` | Constructs the Witt algebra W(m,n) over the finite field F (m a positive integer, n a sequence of m positive integers). `Check` (BoolElt, default false). Returns W and an invertible map φ from a polynomial ring P of degree 2m to W(m,n) (generators 1..m map to x_i, generators m+1..2m map to ∂_{i−m}). W(m,n) is simple unless p = 2 and m = 1 **[SF88, 4.2.4]**; restrictable iff n = [1,…,1] **[SF88, 4.2.4]**. | Explicit basis construction following **[Str04, 2.1]** and **[SF88]**. |
| `SpecialLieAlgebra(F, m, n)` | Constructs S(m,n) (m ≥ 2). `Check` as above. Returns: S(m,n); W(m,n); map P → S(m,n); map P → W(m,n). S(m,n)^(1) is simple for m ≥ 3 **[SF88, 4.3.5]**; dim S(m,n) = (m−1)p^N + 1. | Follows **[Str04]** and **[SF88]**. |
| `ConformalSpecialLieAlgebra(F, m, n)` | Constructs CS(m,n). Returns: CS(m,n); S(m,n); W(m,n); maps P → CS(m,n), P → S(m,n), P → W(m,n). dim CS = dim S + 1. | Follows **[Str04]** and **[SF88]**. |
| `HamiltonianLieAlgebra(F, m, n)` | Constructs H(m,n) over F of characteristic ≥ 3, m ≥ 2 even. `Check` as above. Returns: H(m,n); W(m,n); maps P → H(m,n), P → W(m,n). H(m,n)^(2) is simple **[SF88, 4.4.5]**; dim H(m,n) = p^N − 1. | Follows **[Str04]** and **[SF88]**. |
| `ConformalHamiltonianLieAlgebra(F, m, n)` | Constructs CH(m,n). Returns: CH(m,n); H(m,n); W(m,n); maps P → CH(m,n), P → H(m,n), P → W(m,n). dim CH = dim H + 1. | Follows **[Str04]** and **[SF88]**. |
| `ContactLieAlgebra(F, m, n)` | Constructs K(m,n) over F of characteristic ≥ 3, m ≥ 3 odd. `Check` as above. Returns: K(m,n); W(m,n); maps P → K(m,n), P → W(m,n). K(m,n)^(1) is simple **[SF88, 4.5.5]**; dim K(m,n) = p^N. | Follows **[Str04]** and **[SF88]**. |

*Worked examples: H100E20 (W(2,[2,1]) over GF(9), dim 54, simple, multiplication verification; W(1,[1]) over GF(2), dim 2, non-simple, restricted); H100E21 (CS(3,[1,2,1]) and S(3,[1,2,1]) over GF(9): S is not simple but S∗S is, and S∗S is not restricted); H100E22 (CH(2,[2,2]) and H(2,[2,2]) over GF(9): H simple at level 2; not restricted); H100E23 (K(3,[1,1,1]) over GF(5), dim 125, simple).*

### 100.5.3 Melikian Lie Algebras

The Melikian algebras M(n_1, n_2) are simple Lie algebras over fields of characteristic 5, parameterized by positive integers n_1, n_2, of dimension 5^{n_1+n_2+1}. The explicit construction follows Strade **[Str04, §4.3]** as M = W ⊕ O ⊕ W′ with W = W(2,[n_1,n_2]), O = O(2,[n_1,n_2]).

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `MelikianLieAlgebra(F, n1, n2)` | Constructs M(n_1, n_2) over F (characteristic 5). Returns M and an invertible map φ from a polynomial ring P of degree 6 to M (generators represent x_1, x_2, δ_1, δ_2, δ′_1, δ′_2). `Check` (BoolElt, default false). | Explicit bilinear product on W ⊕ O ⊕ W′ via **[Str04, Lemma 4.3.1, Theorem 4.3.3]**. |

*Worked example: H100E24 (M(2,1) over GF(5), dim 625, simple; constructing subspaces W, O, W′ and verifying the 3×3 multiplication table of the direct-sum decomposition).*

## 100.7 Construction of Subalgebras, Ideals and Quotients

Requires: coefficient ring R is a Euclidean domain (for submodules and ideals) or a field (for quotients). Left, right, and two-sided ideals coincide in Lie algebras.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `sub< L \| A >` | Constructs the subalgebra S of L generated by the items of A (elements, sequences of elements, subalgebras, or sequences of subalgebras). Returns S and the inclusion homomorphism S → L. | Closure under the Lie bracket. |
| `ideal< L \| A >` | Constructs the ideal I of L generated by A (same types as `sub`). Returns I and the inclusion homomorphism I → L. | Closure under the Lie bracket and under bracket with all of L. |

## 100.16 Restrictable Lie Algebras

A **restricted Lie algebra** (or p-Lie algebra) over a field of characteristic p > 0 is equipped with a restriction map x ↦ x^{[p]} satisfying the axioms of **[Jac62]**. ## 100.17 Universal Enveloping Algebras

### 100.17.1 Background

The universal enveloping algebra U(L) of a Lie algebra L over F with basis x_1,…,x_n is the unital associative F-algebra generated by x_1,…,x_n with relations x_j x_i − x_i x_j = [x_j, x_i]. The **Poincaré–Birkhoff–Witt theorem** gives a basis {x_1^{k_1}…x_n^{k_n} | k_i ≥ 0}. If L is semisimple of characteristic 0, the **integral form** uses a Chevalley basis (positive root vectors x_1,…,x_s; negative root vectors y_1,…,y_s; Cartan generators h_1,…,h_r) and divided powers y_i^{(n)} = y_i^n/n!, x_i^{(n)} = x_i^n/n!, and binomials C(h_i, k) = h_i(h_i−1)···(h_i−k+1)/k!. The integral form basis (**[Kos66]**) has integer structure constants.

| `IntegralUEA(L)` / `IntegralUEAlgebra(L)` / `IntegralUniversalEnvelopingAlgebra(L)` | For L semisimple of characteristic 0: creates the integral universal enveloping algebra U using the Kostant basis **[Kos66]** (divided powers, binomials). Databases and identification functions for small-dimensional solvable and nilpotent Lie algebras over any base field.

- **Solvable:** dimensions 2, 3, 4 — classification from **[dG05]** (valid over any base field).
- **Nilpotent:** dimensions 3, 4, 5, 6 — classification from **[dG07]** (valid except for char 2 in dimension 6).

### 100.18.1 The List of Solvable Lie Algebras

The chapter lists all solvable Lie algebra classes L^k_n (n ≤ 4) by their non-zero commutators (see §100.18.1 of the handbook for the full enumeration of L^1_2 through L^{14}_4, including parameterized classes with explicit isomorphism conditions).

### 100.18.2 Comments on the Classification over Finite Fields

Over finite fields of size q with primitive root γ, many parameterized classes reduce to finitely many distinct algebras. The chapter details exact representatives for each case (e.g., L^4_3 has 2 classes in char 2, 3 in other characteristics; L^7_4 splits into 3 sub-families with their own isomorphism conditions).

### 100.18.3 The List of Nilpotent Lie Algebras

The chapter lists all nilpotent Lie algebra classes N^k_r (r = 3,4,5,6, 26 classes in dim 6) by their non-zero commutators (see §100.18.3 for the complete enumeration N^1_3 through N^{26}_6). For parameterized classes N^k_r(a), isomorphism N^k_r(a) ≅ N^k_r(b) iff ∃ α ∈ F* with a = α²b.

### 100.18.4 Intrinsics for Working with the Classifications

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `SolvableLieAlgebra(F, n, k : parameters)` | Returns the solvable Lie algebra L^k_n over the field F. `pars` (SeqEnum, default []): parameter values for parameterized classes. | Direct structure-constant construction from the classification table **[dG05]**. |
| `NilpotentLieAlgebra(F, r, k : parameters)` | Returns the nilpotent Lie algebra N^k_r over the field F. `pars` (SeqEnum, default []): parameter values. | Direct construction from **[dG07]** classification table. |
| `AllSolvableLieAlgebras(F, d)` | For F a finite field and d ∈ {2, 3, 4}: returns a sequence of all solvable Lie algebras of dimension d over F. | Enumeration from the classification. |
| `AllNilpotentLieAlgebras(F, d)` | For F a finite field and d ∈ {3, 4, 5, 6} (char F ≠ 2 if d = 6): returns a sequence of all nilpotent Lie algebras of dimension d over F. | Enumeration from the classification. |
| `IdDataSLAC(L)` | Identifies the solvable Lie algebra L (dim 2, 3, or 4) in the classification. Returns: a string naming the algebra (with field and parameters); a sequence of parameter values; an isomorphism from L to the corresponding classification algebra. | Comparison via structure invariants against the solvable classification **[dG05]**. |
| `IdDataNLAC(L)` | Identifies the nilpotent Lie algebra L (dim 3, 4, 5, or 6) in the classification. Returns: a string naming the algebra N; a sequence of parameter values; an isomorphism L → N. | Comparison against the nilpotent classification **[dG07]**. |
| `MatrixOfIsomorphism(f)` | For f an isomorphism as returned by `IdDataSLAC` or `IdDataNLAC`: the matrix of f (row convention: i-th row = coordinates of image of i-th basis element). | Direct matrix extraction. |

*Worked examples: H100E53 (SolvableLieAlgebra over rational function field, L^3_3(a)); H100E54 (NilpotentLieAlgebra N^{19}_6(a³)); H100E55 (IdDataSLAC for a parametric 4-dim solvable algebra over Q(a): generically L^6_4; special values a=1,−1,0 yield L^3_4, L^7_4, L^4_4); H100E56 (IdDataNLAC for positive part of G2 in char 0: N^{16}_6; in char 3: N^{19}_6(0)).*

## 100.19 Semisimple Subalgebras of Simple Lie Algebras

Semisimple subalgebras of simple Lie algebras over C (ranks ≤ 8) have been classified up to linear equivalence (two subalgebras K_1, K_2 of L are linearly equivalent if all induced representations of L restrict equivalently). Background: **[dG11]**.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `SubalgebrasInclusionGraph(t)` | For t a simple Cartan type of rank ≤ 8: returns a directed graph G. Vertices 1 to N are the semisimple subalgebras (with the subalgebra as label); vertex 0 is the simple Lie algebra L of type t. An edge from vertex K_1 to vertex K_2 means K_1 has a subalgebra linearly equivalent to K_2 (not that K_2 ⊆ K_1). L (and subalgebras) may be defined over Q or a cyclotomic field extension. | Database lookup; classification from **[dG11]**. |
| `RestrictionMatrix(G, k)` | For G a subalgebras inclusion graph and k a nonzero vertex index: returns the restriction matrix for the Lie algebra at vertex k (maps weights of a representation of L to weights of the subalgebra at vertex k). Used to decompose representations of L as representations of the subalgebra. | Linear map between weight lattices. |

*Worked examples: H100E57 (SubalgebrasInclusionGraph for C3: 15 subalgebras, types of maximal subalgebras include A1, A2, A1 A1, A1 C2); H100E58 (decomposition of the adjoint representation of D4 restricted to its G2 subalgebra: weights (0,1) with mult. 1 and (1,0) with mult. 2).*

## 100.20 Nilpotent Orbits in Simple Lie Algebras

The automorphism group (identity component) of a simple Lie algebra L over C acts on L; its orbits on the nilpotent elements have been classified. Main technical tools: the **weighted Dynkin diagram** (labels 0, 1, 2 on Dynkin nodes uniquely determining the orbit) and the **sl_2-triple** [f, h, e] (satisfying [h,e] = 2e, [h,f] = −2f, [e,f] = h). By the Jacobson–Morozov theorem, every nilpotent element embeds as the nilpositive element of an sl_2-triple. Reference: **[CM93]**.

Weighted Dynkin diagrams are represented as sequences of labels; sl_2-triples as sequences [f, h, e].

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `IsGenuineWeightedDynkinDiagram(L, wd)` | For L simple and wd a sequence of integers 0, 1, 2: returns true iff wd corresponds to a nilpotent orbit, plus an sl_2-triple whose third element lies in that orbit. If wd does not correspond to any orbit, second return value is a sequence of three zeros. | Database lookup in **[CM93]** classification. |
| `NilpotentOrbit(L, wd)` | Returns the nilpotent orbit in simple L with weighted Dynkin diagram wd. Not checked. | Database lookup. |
| `NilpotentOrbit(L, e)` | Returns the nilpotent orbit in simple L having representative e (e must be nilpotent; not checked). | Identifies orbit by matching sl_2-triple structure. |
| `NilpotentOrbits(L)` | Returns the sequence of all (non-zero) nilpotent orbits in simple L. | Database lookup. |
| `Partition(o)` | For a nilpotent orbit o in a simple Lie algebra of classical type (An, Bn, Cn, or Dn): the partition corresponding to the orbit (classical-type nilpotent orbits are classified by partitions). | Classical type orbit-partition correspondence from **[CM93]**. |
| `SL2Triple(o)` | For nilpotent orbit o in simple L: an sl_2-triple [f, h, e] with e in the orbit. | Database/Jacobson–Morozov construction from **[CM93]**. |
| `SL2Triple(L, e)` | For L semisimple of characteristic 0 and nilpotent e ∈ L: an sl_2-triple [f, h, e]. May work in other settings (not guaranteed). | Jacobson–Morozov theorem (constructive). |
| `Representative(o)` | Returns an element e ∈ L lying in the nilpotent orbit o. | Database lookup. |
| `WeightedDynkinDiagram(o)` | Returns the weighted Dynkin diagram of the nilpotent orbit o. | Database lookup. |

*Worked examples: H100E59 (enumeration of all nilpotent orbits of D4 by scanning all 3⁴ weighted Dynkin diagrams; IsGenuineWeightedDynkinDiagram finds 11 genuine diagrams); H100E60 (NilpotentOrbit for A2 by diagram [2,2] and by a representative element); H100E61 (NilpotentOrbits of D4: 11 orbits; weighted Dynkin diagrams); H100E62 (Partition for D4 orbits: [4,4] and [3,3,1,1]); H100E63 (NilpotentOrbit for E8: weighted Dynkin diagram [1,0,0,0,0,0,0,1]).*

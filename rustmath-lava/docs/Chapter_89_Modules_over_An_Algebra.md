# Chapter 89 — Modules over An Algebra

**Handbook part:** XII — Representation Theory
**Handbook pages:** 2687–2719 (PDF pages 2818–2853)

---

## Scope and overview

Chapter 89 develops Magma's framework for modules over algebras — the setting that unifies
representation theory for groups, matrix algebras, and general associative algebras.

A (right) A-module M over a field K is a K-vector space equipped with a compatible right
action of the algebra A. The chapter formalises this through the correspondence between
A-modules and algebra homomorphisms A → HomK(M, M) (representations), and between
finite-dimensional A-modules and matrix representations A → Mₙ(K). The most important
special case is A = K[G], the group algebra; here A-module theory coincides exactly with
the theory of group representations.

For Magma V2.19, A-modules are supported in three cases: (i) a matrix algebra A over a
field; (ii) a matrix algebra arising from a finite-degree group representation (implicitly
computing with K[G]); and (iii) a matrix algebra over a Euclidean domain R, though
the action is then usable only for submodule construction and the coefficient ring is
effectively treated as a field.

The chapter is split into two major parts. Section 89.2 covers modules over a matrix
algebra (the richest case, with the most functions) and provides a full toolkit: construction,
submodule and quotient operations, Meataxe-based composition and socle series analysis,
decomposability and complement algorithms, the lattice of submodules, and homomorphism
spaces including the endomorphism algebra and automorphism group. Many structure
functions require the base ring to be a finite field. Section 89.3 handles modules over a
general (not necessarily matrix) algebra, with a smaller but self-contained API covering
construction, actions, related structures, and basic module-building operations. Since the
underlying n-tuple R-module underlies every A-module, all operations for R-modules are
also available.

## 89.2 Modules over a Matrix Algebra

This section describes all functions dealing with modules over a matrix algebra.

### 89.2.1 Construction of an A-Module

#### 89.2.1.1 General Constructions

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `RModule(A)` | Given a subalgebra A of Mₙ(K), creates the right A-module M with underlying vector space K^(n); the action of a ∈ A on m ∈ M is m * a. | — |
| `RModule(Q)` | Given the subalgebra A of Mₙ(K) generated by the terms of sequence Q, creates the right A-module M with underlying vector space K^(n); the action of a ∈ A on m ∈ M is m * a. | — |

### 89.2.7 Structure of a Module

Most functions in this section assume the base ring is a finite field.

#### 89.2.7.1 Reducibility

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `Meataxe(M)` | Given an A-module M with finite-field base ring, attempts to find a proper submodule N of M or prove M is irreducible. If a splitting is found, returns: (a) the A-module N for the induced action on S; (b) the A-module P = M/N; (c) an invertible matrix T conjugating the matrices of ρ(A) into reduced form with ν(a) in the top-left block and π(a) in the bottom-right block. If M is proved irreducible, returns M alone and records irreducibility in M's data structure. | Meataxe algorithm. |
| `IsIrreducible(M)` | Returns true iff the A-module M is irreducible. If reducible, also returns a proper submodule S and the quotient Q = M/S. | Meataxe. |
| `IsAbsolutelyIrreducible(M)` | Returns true iff the A-module M is absolutely irreducible. Also returns a matrix algebra generator for the endomorphism algebra E of M (a field) and the dimension of E. | Meataxe / Schur's lemma. |
| `AbsolutelyIrreducibleModule(M)` | Given an irreducible A-module M that is not absolutely irreducible over K, returns an absolutely irreducible module N that is a constituent of M considered over the splitting field for M. N is not unique. | Splitting field construction. |
| `MinimalField(M)` | Given an A-module M over a finite field K, returns the smallest subfield of K over which M can be realised. | — |
| `IsPermutationModule(M)` | Returns true iff the generators of the matrix algebra A are permutation matrices. | — |

*Worked examples: H89E5 (manual application of the Meataxe to the degree-45 permutation module for O5(3) over GF(2); successive splitting; IsIrreducible).*

#### 89.2.7.2 Composition Series

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `CompositionSeries(M)` | Given an A-module M, constructs a composition series by repeatedly applying the Meataxe. Returns: (a) the series as a sequence of A-modules; (b) the composition factors in series order; (c) a transformation matrix t such that for each a ∈ A, t * a * t⁻¹ is in reduced form. | Iterated Meataxe. |
| `CompositionFactors(M)` | Given an A-module M, constructs the composition factors by repeatedly applying the Meataxe. Returns a sequence of R-modules in series order; if M is irreducible, returns a sequence containing M alone. | Iterated Meataxe. |
| `Constituents(M)` | Given an A-module M, returns: a sequence C of representatives for the isomorphism classes of composition factors; and a sequence I of indices so that the i-th element of C is the I[i]-th composition factor of M. | Meataxe + isomorphism testing. |
| `ConstituentsWithMultiplicities(M)` | As `Constituents(M)` but also returns the multiplicity of each constituent. A sequence I of indices is also returned. | Meataxe + isomorphism testing. |

*Worked examples: H89E6 (O5(3) permutation module: Constituents, ConstituentsWithMultiplicities, CompositionSeries, IndecomposableSummands; detecting a non-absolutely-irreducible constituent of dimension 8 and lifting to GF(4)).*

### 89.2.9 Lattice of Submodules

Magma can construct the complete lattice L of all submodules of an A-module M when that
lattice is not too large. Lattice elements are numbered 1 to n = #L (in no particular order).
Once built, all lattice operations (meet, sum, subset tests) are available without further
module-theoretic computations. These functions currently apply only when A is an algebra
over a finite field.

#### 89.2.9.1 Creating Lattices

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `SubmoduleLattice(M)` | Constructs the lattice L of all submodules of M. Parameter `Limit` (RngIntElt, default 0): if non-zero, at most Limit submodules are computed; second return value indicates whether L is the full lattice. Parameter `CodimensionLimit` (RngIntElt, default −1). | Works down a composition series of M. |
| `SubmoduleLatticeAbort(M, n)` | Constructs the lattice of submodules of M provided their number does not exceed n. Returns true and the lattice if successful; false if M has more than n submodules. | — |
| `SetVerbose("SubmoduleLattice", i)` | Controls verbose printing for the submodule lattice algorithm. Level i = 1 for moderate printing, i = 2 for maximal printing; a summary is printed per level of the composition series. | — |
| `Submodules(M)` | Returns a sequence of all submodules of M sorted by dimension. Parameter `CodimensionLimit` (RngIntElt, default `Dimension(M)`). | — |

*Worked examples: H89E9 (submodule lattice of F3[Z6] with level-1 verbose printing; 16 submodules).*

### 89.2.10 Homomorphisms

Let M and N be A-modules where A is defined over a field K. Then HomA(M, N) consists
of all K-homomorphisms M → N that commute with the action of A (A-homs). The type
of such matrix homomorphisms is `ModMatGrpElt`. These functions currently apply only
when A is an algebra over a finite field.

#### 89.2.10.1 Creating Homomorphisms

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `hom< M -> N \| X >` | Given A-modules M and N, creates the (map) homomorphism from M to N given by matrix X. | — |
| `H ! f` | Given matrix space H = HomA(M, N) and a homomorphism f from M to N, creates the matrix corresponding to f. | — |
| `IsModuleHomomorphism(X)` | Given a matrix X ∈ HomK(M, N) where M and N are A-modules, returns true if X is an A-homomorphism. | — |

#### 89.2.10.2 Hom Spaces

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `Hom(M, N)` | Given A-modules M and N, constructs the vector space HomK(M, N) (K-homomorphisms, not necessarily A-equivariant). | — |
| `AHom(M, N)` | Given A-modules M and N, constructs the vector space HomA(M, N) as a submodule of HomK(M, N). | — |
| `GHomOverCentralizingField(M, N)` | Given A-modules M and N, constructs HomL[G](M, N) as a subspace of HomK(M, N), where L is the centralizing field of M. | — |

*Worked examples: H89E11 (AHom for a 12-dimensional permutation module over GF(3); kernel and image of a random element); H89E12 (GHom and Hom with right matrix action to construct the endomorphism module).*

#### 89.2.10.3 Endo- and Automorphisms

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `EndomorphismAlgebra(M)` / `EndomorphismRing(M)` | Given an A-module M with base ring K, constructs E = EndA(M) as a subring of the complete matrix ring K^(n×n). Parameter `Direct` (BoolElt, default `false`). | — |
| `CentreOfEndomorphismRing(M)` | Given an A-module M with base ring K, constructs the centre of EndA(M) as a subring of K^(n×n). Equivalent to `Centre(EndomorphismRing(M))` but typically much faster. | — |
| `AutomorphismGroup(M)` | Given an A-module M with base ring K, constructs Aut(M) as a subgroup of GL(n, K) — the group of units of End(M). | — |
| `IsIsomorphic(M, N)` | Returns true if A-modules M and N are isomorphic, false otherwise. If isomorphic, also returns a matrix T such that M^T = N (i.e. T⁻¹ * ActionGenerator(M, i) * T = ActionGenerator(N, i) for each i). Requires matching action generators. | — |

*Worked examples: H89E13 (EndomorphismAlgebra and AutomorphismGroup for the GF(3) permutation module of order-648 group; 18 automorphisms; AbelianInvariants showing Z2 × Z3 × Z3).*

## 89.3 Modules over a General Algebra

### 89.3.1 Introduction

This section covers modules over general (not necessarily matrix) algebras. A left-module
over A is a module M with a bilinear map A × M → M; a right-module has a bilinear map
M × A → M. Magma provides functionality for both.

### 89.3.2 Construction of Algebra Modules

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `Module(A, m)` | For an algebra A, creates a module over A. For a left-module, m is a map from A × M → M; for a right-module, m is a map from M × A → M (M must be an R-module where R is the coefficient field of A). | — |

*Worked examples: H89E14 (right-module over the full 3×3 matrix algebra over Q acting on its natural module).*

### 89.3.3 The Action of an Algebra Element

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `a ^ v` | Given an element v of a left-module over A and an element a ∈ A, computes the result of a acting on v. | — |
| `v ^ a` | Given an element v of a right-module over A and an element a ∈ A, computes the result of a acting on v. | — |
| `ActionMatrix(M, a)` | Given a module M over algebra A and an element a ∈ A, returns the matrix of the action of a on M. For a left-module, the i-th column contains the coordinates of a acting on the i-th basis element; for a right-module, the rows contain these coordinates. | — |

*Worked examples: H89E15 (ActionMatrix for the right-module over the 3×3 matrix algebra).*

### 89.3.4 Related Structures of an Algebra Module

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `Algebra(M)` | Returns the algebra over which the algebra module M is defined. | — |
| `CoefficientRing(M)` | Returns the ground field of the algebra module M. | — |
| `Basis(M)` | Returns a sequence containing the basis vectors of the algebra module M. | — |

### 89.3.5 Properties of an Algebra Module

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|

### 89.3.6 Creation of Algebra Modules from Other Algebra Modules

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `DirectSum(Q)` | Given a sequence Q of algebra modules (all over the same algebra, and all left or all right modules), returns the direct sum module M together with two sequences: the i-th embedding Q[i] → M and the i-th projection M → Q[i]. | — |
| `SubalgebraModule(B, M)` | Given an algebra module M over algebra A and a subalgebra B of A, returns M as a B-module. | — |
| `ModuleWithBasis(Q)` | Given a sequence Q of elements forming a basis of an algebra module M, creates an algebra module V isomorphic to M with basis in bijection with Q. | `sub< M \| S >` / `sub< M \| e1, ..., en >` | Returns the submodule of M containing the elements in sequence S or the elements e1, …, en. | — |
| `quo< M \| S >` / `quo< M \| e1, ..., en >` | Constructs the quotient module of M by the submodule S of M (or the submodule generated by S or e1, …, en). | — |

*Worked examples: H89E16 (DirectSum of two copies of M; ModuleWithBasis with a non-standard basis; ActionMatrix output in both cases).*

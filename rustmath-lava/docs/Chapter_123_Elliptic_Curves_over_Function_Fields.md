# Chapter 123 — Elliptic Curves over Function Fields

**Handbook part:** XVI — Arithmetic Geometry
**Handbook pages:** 4085–4099 (PDF pages 4218–4233)

---

## Scope and overview

This chapter covers elliptic curves with coefficients in a function field k(C), where C is a regular projective curve over a field k (typically a number field or a finite field). The commands are largely parallel to those for elliptic curves over the rationals.

An elliptic curve E over K = k(C) is regarded as an elliptic surface over k equipped with a fibration π: E → C. The Mordell–Weil group E(K) is in one-to-one correspondence with sections of π. The unique projective, regular, relatively minimal model of this surface is the Kodaira–Néron model. The Néron–Severi group NS(E_k̄) is a finitely generated group closely connected with E(K): the quotient NS(E_k̄)/N is isomorphic to E(k̄(C)) via the identification of sections with points, where N is the trivial lattice generated by fibral components and the zero section. The Shioda–Tate formula relates the rank of E(k̄(C)) to the rank of NS(E_k̄) and the number of fibral components.

The chapter proceeds in order of decreasing generality. Local information (Tate's algorithm, Kodaira symbols, minimal models) is available over univariate function fields over any exact base field. The L-function and analytic rank are available over function fields over finite fields, exploiting the connection between the characteristic polynomial of Frobenius on H²(E_k̄, Q_ℓ) and the BSD/Artin–Tate conjecture. Full Mordell–Weil group computation is available only when E can be defined by a Weierstrass equation with coefficient aᵢ of degree at most i in t (so the surface is rational, with rank(NS) = 10). Two-descent tools extend reach beyond this case.

When k is a finite field, the Tate conjecture (proved for rational and K3 elliptic surfaces) makes AnalyticRank an unconditional upper bound. The L-function equals det(1 − T · Fq | H²(E_k̄, Q_ℓ) / im(N ⊗ Q_ℓ)) and is always a polynomial. The functions FrobeniusActionOnReducibleFiber and FrobeniusActionOnTrivialLattice give the Frobenius action on the known part N.

## 123.2 Local Computations

Local information (reduction types, Tamagawa numbers, Kodaira symbols, minimal models) is computed using Tate's algorithm. These functions are available for curves over univariate function fields over any exact base field.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `BadPlaces(E)` | Returns a sequence of places where the given model of E has bad reduction. | — |
| `Conductor(E)` | The conductor of E over a function field F. Returned as a divisor of F in general; as a sequence of tuples ⟨f, e⟩ (place specified by field element f, multiplicity e) when F is a rational function field. | — |
| `LocalInformation(E, Pl)` / `LocalInformation(E, f)` | Runs Tate's algorithm at the place Pl (or the place specified by irreducible polynomial or 1/t for rational function fields). Returns a tuple ⟨Pl, v_p(d), f_p, c_p, K, split⟩ and a model Emin minimal at Pl, where v_p(d) is the valuation of the local minimal discriminant, f_p is the conductor valuation, c_p is the Tamagawa number, K is the Kodaira symbol, and split is false for nonsplit multiplicative reduction and true otherwise. The input model need not be integral. | Tate's algorithm |
| `LocalInformation(E)` | Returns a sequence of tuples as above for all places of bad reduction of E. | Tate's algorithm |
| `KodairaSymbols(E)` | A sequence of tuples ⟨K, n⟩ for each place of bad reduction of E, where K is the Kodaira symbol and n is the degree of the corresponding place. | — |
| `NumberOfComponents(K)` | The number of components of a fibre with Kodaira symbol K. | — |
| `MinimalModel(E)` | A model of E (defined over a function field of genus 0) that is minimal at all finite places, together with a map from E to this minimal model. | — |
| `MinimalDegreeModel(E)` | A model of E (over a rational function field) minimising Max([Degree(aᵢ)/i]) over the Weierstrass coefficients a₁, a₂, a₃, a₄, a₆. | — |
| `IsConstantCurve(E)` | For E over a rational function field F(t): returns true iff E is isomorphic over F(t) to an elliptic curve with coefficients in F; also returns such a curve when true. | — |
| `TraceOfFrobenius(E, p)` | The trace of Frobenius aₚ for the reduction of E at the place p, specified as an element of the base field. | — |

## 123.4 Heights

The height functions here are the Néron–Tate canonical height and local heights on elliptic curves over function fields, together with lattice-theoretic tools for working with the height pairing.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `NaiveHeight(P)` | The naive x-coordinate height of P on an elliptic curve over a function field K: the degree of the point (x(P) : 1) on the projective line. | — |
| `Height(P)` | The Néron–Tate canonical height ĥ(P) of P on an elliptic curve over a function field. | — |
| `LocalHeight(P, Pl)` | For a point P on an elliptic curve over function field F and a place Pl of F: the local height λ_Pl(P) at Pl. | — |
| `HeightPairing(P, Q)` | The height pairing ⟨P, Q⟩ = (ĥ(P+Q) − ĥ(P) − ĥ(Q))/2. | — |
| `HeightPairingMatrix(S)` | For a sequence S of points Pᵢ on an elliptic curve over a function field: the matrix (⟨Pᵢ, Pⱼ⟩) of height pairings. | — |
| `HeightPairingLattice(S)` | The height pairing lattice of a sequence of independent points on an elliptic curve over a function field. | — |
| `Basis(S)` | For a sequence S of points on an elliptic curve: a sequence of points forming a basis for the free part of the subgroup generated by S; also returns the Gram matrix for this basis under the Néron–Tate pairing. | — |
| `Basis(S, r, disc)` | For a sequence S of points: a sequence of independent points in the free part of the generated subgroup forming a lattice of rank r and discriminant disc; returns as soon as such a lattice is found. | — |
| `IsLinearlyDependent(points)` / `IsLinearlyIndependent(points)` / `IndependentGenerators(points)` | Analogues of the corresponding functions for elliptic curves over the rationals, available here for curves over function fields. | — |

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `TorsionSubgroup(E)` | For E over a function field F: returns an abelian group A isomorphic to the torsion subgroup of E(F), together with a map from A to E(F). | — |

## 123.6 The Mordell–Weil Group

The machinery in this section is for curves over a rational function field k(t) with k finite. Full Mordell–Weil group computation applies when each Weierstrass coefficient aᵢ is a polynomial in k[t] of degree at most i (giving a rational surface with rank(NS) = 10). Two-descent (§123.7) may still bound the rank and find generators outside this hypothesis.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `RankBounds(E)` / `RankBound(E)` | Returns lower and upper bounds (or just an upper bound) on the rank of E(F) for E over a function field with finite constant field. Uses all available tools including `AnalyticInformation` from the L-function. | Combination of geometric method and L-function analytic information |
| `MordellWeilGroup(E : parameters)` | Computes the Mordell–Weil group of E (satisfying the degree hypothesis). Returns an abelian group A and a map m: A → E giving an isomorphism. Parameter `Al` (MonStgElt, default `"Geometric"`): when set to `"Descent"` uses 2-descent tools; if the curve admits 2-isogenies, uses descent by 2-isogenies **[Rob07]**. Descent methods may not determine the full group (a warning is printed). | Default: geometric method (Shioda–Tate / Mordell–Weil lattice); `Al := "Descent"`: 2-descent or 2-isogeny descent **[Rob07]** |
| `MordellWeilLattice(E)` | Computes the Mordell–Weil lattice of E (free part of the Mordell–Weil group with the Néron–Tate height pairing inner product). Returns a lattice L and a map m from L to E. | Geometric method (Shioda–Tate) |
| `GeometricMordellWeilLattice(E)` | Computes the geometric Mordell–Weil lattice: free part of the group of points over the function field with (possibly extended) algebraically closed constant field, with Néron–Tate pairing. Returns a lattice L and a map m from L to a base change E′. | Geometric method |
| `Generators(E)` | For E over a rational function field F satisfying the degree hypothesis: returns a sequence of points in E(F) generating the Mordell–Weil group. | Geometric method |

*Worked example: H123E1 (curve y² = x³ + (t⁴+2t²)x + t³+2t over F₃(t), rank 2, 2-Selmer group Z/2⊕Z/2, regulator 12).*

## 123.7 Two Descent

In odd characteristic the 2-Selmer group can be computed and its nontrivial elements represented as minimised 2-coverings (hyperelliptic curves of degree 4). In characteristic 2, descent by the 2-isogenies E → E^frob → E is performed for ordinary curves, with the Artin–Schreier map playing the role of squaring.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `TwoSelmerGroup(E)` | Computes the 2-Selmer group of E over a rational function field Fq(t) of odd characteristic. Returns an abstract group and a map to the relevant algebra. | Standard 2-descent (analogous to the number field case) |
| `TwoDescent(E)` | Represents nontrivial elements of the 2-Selmer group of E (over Fq(t), odd characteristic) as hyperelliptic curves C: y² = f(x) of degree 4; returns a sequence of these curves and the covering maps C → E. Curves have polynomial coefficients and are minimised at all finite places. The conic parametrisation step uses the algorithm of Cremona and van Hoeij. | 2-descent; conic parametrisation by Cremona–van Hoeij |
| `QuarticMinimize(f)` | Minimises a 2-covering: takes a homogeneous quartic in two variables with coefficients in a rational univariate function field and returns a minimal equivalent quartic together with the transformation matrix. Used internally by `TwoDescent`. | Minimisation of quartic models over function fields |
| `Points(C : parameters)` | Finds all rational points of x-coordinate (logarithmic) height up to `Bound` on the hyperelliptic curve C over Fq(t). Finds all projective points (X : Y : Z) where X and Z are polynomials in t of degree ≤ Bound. Parameter `Bound` (RngIntElt) is not optional. | Height-bounded point search |
| `PointsQI(C, H)` | Optimised routine for finding rational points on a curve given as an intersection of two quadrics over Fq(t). Finds projective points with polynomial coordinates of degree up to H. Parameter `OnlyOne` (BoolElt, default false); `ExactBound` (BoolElt, default false; must be true to guarantee finding all points). | Lattice reduction method **[Rob07]** |
| `TwoIsogenySelmerGroups(E)` | Performs descent by 2-isogenies for a non-supersingular E over k(t) with k finite of characteristic 2, using the Frobenius isogeny E → E^frob: (x,y) ↦ (x², y²) and its dual. Returns: Selmer group S_sep of the separable isogeny (abstract group); Selmer group S_Frob of the Frobenius isogeny; and maps S_sep → k(t)/Φ(k(t)) and S_Frob → k(t)*/(k(t)*)². Here Φ is the Artin–Schreier map a ↦ a²+a. | Descent by 2-isogenies in characteristic 2 **[Kra77]** |

## 123.8 The L-function and Counting Points

The L-function equals the characteristic polynomial of Frobenius acting on the unknown part of H²(E_k̄, Q_ℓ)(1). It can be computed directly via `LFunction` (which counts points on the surface) or assembled by hand from point counts using the tools in this section and §123.9.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `LFunction(E)` / `LFunction(E, S)` | The L-function of E over a rational function field over a finite field. Parameter `Check` (BoolElt, default true). When a sequence S of points is given, those points are used to reduce the unknown part of H²; if `Check := false` then S must be a basis for a subgroup of the geometric Mordell–Weil group (mod torsion) that is Galois-stable. | Counts points on the Kodaira–Néron model over constant field extensions; uses Galois action on known points in S to reduce computation |
| `LFunction(E, e)` | The L-function for the base change of E by a constant field extension of degree e. | As above for the base change |
| `AnalyticRank(E)` | The Mordell–Weil rank of E predicted by Tate's version of BSD (equivalently, the Artin–Tate conjecture); an unconditional upper bound, proved to be the actual rank for rational elliptic surfaces and K3 elliptic surfaces. Requires E over a function field over a finite field. | Tate's conjecture / Artin–Tate conjecture |
| `AnalyticInformation(E)` / `AnalyticInformation(E, L)` / `AnalyticInformation(E, e)` | Returns the tuple ⟨rank, geometric rank, |Sha| · regulator⟩ predicted by BSD/Artin–Tate for E over a function field over a finite field. When polynomial L is given, it is assumed to be the L-function of E. When integer e is given, returns analytic data for the base change by the constant field extension of degree e (more efficient than base-changing E explicitly). | BSD / Artin–Tate conjecture; Tate's conjecture |
| `NumberOfPointsOnSurface(E, e)` | The number of points on the Kodaira–Néron model of E over the constant field extension of degree e. | Point counting |
| `NumbersOfPointsOnSurface(E, e)` | For E over a function field over Fq: returns a sequence of the number of points on the Kodaira–Néron model over extensions Fqⁱ for i = 1, …, e, together with the number of points on the base curve over these extensions. | Point counting |
| `BettiNumber(E, i)` | The i-th Betti number of the Kodaira–Néron model of the elliptic surface corresponding to E. | — |
| `CharacteristicPolynomialFromTraces(traces)` | Given a sequence [t₁, t₂, …, t_d], computes a polynomial of degree d with constant coefficient 1 such that the j-th powersum of the reciprocal zeroes equals tⱼ. Used to recover the characteristic polynomial of Frobenius on a cohomology group from traces of powers of Frobenius. | Newton power sum / symmetric function identity |
| `CharacteristicPolynomialFromTraces(traces, d, q, i)` | Given [t₁, …, t_k] and integers d, q, i: computes a degree-d polynomial with constant coefficient 1 whose reciprocal zeroes have absolute value qⁱ/² and whose j-th powersum of reciprocal zeroes equals tⱼ. Requires k ≥ Floor(d/2). If the sign of the leading coefficient is undetermined, two polynomials are returned (one for each sign). Used for the characteristic polynomial of Frobenius on the i-th cohomology group. | Newton power sum / Weil bounds; functional equation for sign determination |

*Worked examples: H123E2 (curve over F₃(t) with rank 0 and Sha ≅ Z/3⊕Z/3 detected unconditionally via `AnalyticInformation`); H123E3 (confirms rank 2 and trivial Sha for the curve of H123E1).*

## 123.9 Action of Frobenius

These functions compute the Frobenius action on various pieces of the cohomology / Néron–Severi group of the elliptic surface, enabling "by hand" computation of the L-function.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `Frobenius(P, q)` | The q-th power Frobenius map applied to the point P on an elliptic curve that can be defined over a function field with constant field Fq. | — |
| `FrobeniusActionOnPoints(S, q : parameters)` | A matrix representing the q-power Frobenius on the subgroup of the geometric Mordell–Weil group (mod torsion) with basis S (assumed Frobenius-stable). Optional parameter `gram` (AlgMatElt): the Gram matrix under the height pairing for the points in S. | — |
| `FrobeniusActionOnReducibleFiber(L)` | Given reduction data L for E (as returned by `LocalInformation`): a matrix representing the Frobenius action on the non-identity components of the corresponding fibres. | — |
| `FrobeniusActionOnTrivialLattice(E)` | For E over a rational function field over a finite field: a matrix representing the Frobenius action on the fibre components and zero section (i.e. on the trivial lattice N) of the corresponding elliptic surface. | — |

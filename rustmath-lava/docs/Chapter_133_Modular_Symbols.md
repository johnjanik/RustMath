## Scope and overview

Modular symbols provide an explicit, computable presentation for certain homology groups
attached to modular curves `X₀(N)`, and as such they can be used to compute an eigenform basis
for spaces of cusp forms `Sₖ(N, ε)`, where `k ≥ 2` is an integer and `ε` is an *arbitrary*
Dirichlet character. Their generality makes them a natural tool in applications ranging from
verification of modularity of Galois representations to elliptic-curve computations.

The modular group `SL₂(Z)` is the group of `2×2` integer matrices of determinant 1; for a
positive integer `N`, `Γ₀(N)` is the subgroup of upper-triangular matrices modulo `N`. There is
an algebraic curve `X₀(N)` over `Q` attached to `Γ₀(N)`; its Riemann surface is a compactified
quotient of the upper half plane. Modular symbols give a finite presentation for the homology of
`X₀(N)`. Concretely, for `P¹(Q) = Q ∪ {∞}` and a field `F`, one forms the `F`-vector space
generated by formal symbols `{a, b}` (`a, b ∈ P¹(Q)`) modulo the relations `{a,b}+{b,c}+{c,a}=0`,
tensored with `Symᵏ⁻²(F×F)` to build weight-`k` symbols `XⁱYᵏ⁻²⁻ⁱ{a,b}`. The group `GL₂(Q)` acts
on the left; the space `Mₖ(N, ε; F)` of modular symbols of weight `k`, level `N` and character
`ε` is the quotient by the subspace generated by `x − ε(u)g(x)` for `g ∈ Γ₀(N)`. The resulting
space is finite-dimensional and admits a finite presentation in terms of *Manin symbols*.

The implementation was deeply influenced by Cremona **[Cre92, Cre97]** and Merel **[Mer94]**;
the algorithms for computing arithmetic invariants of modular abelian varieties are based on
Stein **[Ste00]**. Spaces of modular symbols may be defined over any base ring (unlike
`ModularForms`, which only base-extends spaces defined over the integers). Note that some
functions, such as `DualVectorSpace`, may fail over a field of positive characteristic, because
the Hecke operators `Tₚ` (with `p` prime to the level) need not be semisimple there. The chapter
covers creation, bases, the associated vector space, degeneracy maps, decomposition, subspaces,
twists, Hecke operators and the Hecke algebra, the intersection pairing, `q`-expansions, special
values of `L`-functions, the associated complex torus, modular abelian varieties (modular degree,
torsion, Tamagawa numbers, component groups), elliptic curves, and dimension formulas.

## 133.2 Basics

### 133.2.1 Verbose Output

The verbosity level for modular symbols computations is set with `SetVerbose("ModularSymbols",n)`,
where `n` is 0 (silent), 1 (verbose), or 2 (very verbose). (The flag was called `ModularForms`
in Magma version 2.7.)

### 133.2.2 Categories

Spaces of modular symbols belong to the category `ModSym`; their elements lie in `ModSymElt`.
The category `SetCsp` has exactly one object `Cusps()`, the set `P¹(Q) = Q ∪ {∞}`; the element
`∞` is entered as `Cusps()!Infinity()`.

*Worked example:* H133E1 (basis of `ModularSymbols(11,2)`; coercion `M!<1,[1/5,1]>`; verbose
output of space creation; modular symbols over `GF(7)`).

## 133.3 Creation Functions

### 133.3.1 Ambient Spaces

An ambient space is created by specifying a base ring, character, weight, and optional *sign*.
The most general signature is `ModularSymbols(eps, k, sign)`, where `eps` is a Dirichlet
character (the level is the modulus of `eps`, and the base ring is the base ring of `eps`). For
Dirichlet characters, see Chapter 19.8. **Warning:** functions such as `DualVectorSpace` may fail
over a field of positive characteristic because the `Tₚ` need not be semisimple.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `ModularSymbols(N)` | Space of modular symbols of level `N`, weight 2, trivial character, over the rationals. | Manin-symbol presentation. |
| `ModularSymbols(N, k)` | Space of level `N`, weight `k`, trivial character, over the rationals. | Manin-symbol presentation. |
| `ModularSymbols(N, k, F)` | Space of level `N`, weight `k`, trivial character, over the field `F`. | Manin-symbol presentation. |
| `ModularSymbols(N, k, sign)` | Space of level `N`, weight `k`, trivial character, given `sign` (an integer), over the rationals. | As above. |
| `ModularSymbols(N, k, F, sign)` | Space of level `N`, weight `k`, trivial character, given `sign`, over the field `F`. | As above. |
| `ModularSymbols(eps, k)` | Space of weight `k` and character `ε` (an element of a Dirichlet group); `ε` determines the level and base field, so these need not be specified. | As above. |
| `ModularSymbols(eps, k, sign)` | Space of weight `k` and character `ε`, with given `sign`. Possible values of `sign` are `−1`, `0`, `+1`, corresponding to the `−1` quotient, the full space, and the `+1` quotient `M/(∗−1)M` (where `∗` is `StarInvolution(M)`). | As above. |

*Worked examples:* H133E2 (creating spaces in several ways: `ModularSymbols(37)`, decomposition,
elliptic curve from a factor, `ModularSymbols(1,12)`, the relation
`dim M₂(Γ₀(37)) = 2·(dim cusp) + 1·(dim Eisenstein)`, a level-112 space with nontrivial
character over a cyclotomic field).

### 133.3.2 Labels

Spaces of modular symbols for `Γ₀(N)` may be created from a *descriptive label*, a string of the
form `[Level]k[Weight][IsogenyClass]`, where `[IsogenyClass]` is a letter code `A, B, …, Z, AA,
…`, and `k` separates level and weight. If the label is `[Level][IsogenyClass]` the weight is
taken to be 2. If it is `[Level]k[Weight]`, the cuspidal subspace of the full ambient space of
weight `k` and level `N` is returned. Valid labels: `11A`, `37B`, `3k12A`, `11k4`. The ordering
on isogeny classes is `lt` (see `SortDecomposition`). **Note:** there is currently no intrinsic
that, given a space, returns its label. **Warning:** for 146 of the levels between 56 and 450,
the ordering of weight-2 rational newforms disagrees with that of **[Cre97]**; one can instead
create the space from a Cremona label via the associated elliptic curve.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `ModularSymbols(s)` / `ModularSymbols(s, sign)` | The space of modular symbols described by a label string `s` (and given `sign`). | Parses the label and constructs the corresponding space (cuspidal subspace for the `[Level]k[Weight]` form). |

*Worked examples:* H133E3 (label `"1k12A"` related to `Δ(q)`; label `"11k4A"` and its
`AmbientSpace`; the `+1` quotient `"37k4"`, characteristic-polynomial factorisation).

### 133.3.3 Creation of Elements

A modular symbol `P(X,Y){α, β}` is input as `M!<P(X,Y),[alpha,beta]>`, where `P(X,Y) ∈ F[X,Y]`
is homogeneous of degree `k − 2` and `α, β ∈ P¹(Q)`. When `k = 2`, simply enter
`M!<1,[alpha,beta]>`. Any space `M` is finitely generated; one proof uses that every modular
symbol is a linear combination of *Manin symbols*. A Manin symbol `⟨P(X,Y), (ū, v̄)⟩` is a pair
of a homogeneous polynomial `P(X,Y)` of degree `k − 2` and an element `(ū, v̄) ∈ P¹(Z/NZ)`.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `M ! x` | Coercion of `x` into the space `M`. Here `x` may be a modular symbol that lies in a subspace of `M`, a 2-tuple describing a modular symbol, a sequence of such 2-tuples (then `M!x` is the sum of the coercions), or anything coercible into `VectorSpace(M)`. | Coercion / linear combination of Manin symbols. |
| `ConvertFromManinSymbol(M, x)` | The modular symbol associated to the 2-tuple `x = ⟨P(X,Y),[u,v]⟩`, where `P(X,Y) ∈ F[X,Y]` is homogeneous of degree `k − 1` (here `F` is the base field and `[u,v]` defines an element of `P¹(Z/NZ)`). | Choose lifts `u, v` with `GCD(u,v)=1`, take `g ∈ SL₂(Z)` with lower row `(u, v)`; the symbol is `g(P(X,Y){0,∞})`. |
| `ManinSymbol(x)` | An expression for the modular symbol `x` in terms of Manin symbols, represented as 2-tuples `⟨P(X,Y),[u,v]⟩`. | Writes `x` as a linear combination of Manin symbols. |

*Worked examples:* H133E4 (`M = M₄(Γ₀(3); F₇)`, inputting `(X²−2XY){0,1}`; weight-2 input
`M!<1,[Cusps()|0,Infinity()]>`); H133E5 (convert between Manin and modular symbols via
`ConvertFromManinSymbol`/`ManinSymbol` for weight-4 space over `F₅`).

|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `Basis(M)` | A basis for the space of modular symbols `M`. | — |
| `IntegralBasis(M)` | If `M = AmbientSpace(M)`, returns a basis `x₁,…,xₙ` such that `Zx₁ + ⋯ + Zxₙ` is the `Z`-submodule of `M` generated by all modular symbols `XⁱYᵏ⁻²⁻ⁱ{α,β}` (`i = 0,…,k−2`, `α,β ∈ P¹(Q)`). ## 133.5 Associated Vector Space

`VectorSpace`, `DualVectorSpace`, and `Lattice` return the underlying vector space, dual vector
space, and lattice associated to a space of modular symbols. A space is represented internally
as a subspace of a vector space, together with the corresponding subspace of the linear dual;
carrying along the dual is useful for, e.g., efficient computation of Hecke operators. When the
base field is `Q`, the lattice comes from the natural integral structure on modular symbols.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `VectorSpace(M)` | The vector space `V` underlying `M`, together with the maps `V → M` and `M → V`. | — |
| `DualVectorSpace(M)` | The subspace of the linear dual of `VectorSpace(AmbientSpace(M))` isomorphic to `M` as a module over the Hecke algebra. | Linear-dual representation (may fail in positive characteristic where `Tₚ` is not semisimple). |
| `Lattice(M)` | The lattice generated by the integral modular symbols in the vector-space representation of `M`; the lattice generated by all `XⁱYᵏ⁻²⁻ⁱ{a,b}`. The base field of `M` must be `RationalField()`. | Integral structure on modular symbols. |

*Worked example:* H133E8 (level-11 weight-3 space with character; vector spaces and lattices of
cuspidal and Eisenstein subspaces; bases and basis denominators).

## 133.6 Degeneracy Maps

For an ambient space `M₁` of level `N₁` and an ambient space `M₂` of level `N₂` a multiple of
`N₁` (equal weight, induced character), each divisor `d` of `N₂/N₁` gives natural maps
`α_d : M₁ → M₂` and `β_d : M₂ → M₁` such that `β_d ∘ α_d` is multiplication by
`dᵏ⁻²·[Γ₀(N₁):Γ₀(N₂)]`. On cuspidal parts, `β_d` is dual to `f(q) ↦ f(qᵈ)` on modular forms.
Given a space `M` of level `N` and a multiple `N′`, the images of `M` under the degeneracy maps
generate a level-`N′` space, computed by `ModularSymbols(M,N′)`; the subspace `Σ_{d | N′/N}
α_d(M) ⊂ Mₖ(N′, ε)` is Hecke-stable.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `DegeneracyMap(M1, M2, d)` | The degeneracy map `M₁ → M₂` associated to `d`. With `N_i` the level of `M_i`, suppose `d` divides either the numerator or denominator of `N₁/N₂` in reduced form. If `N₁ | N₂`, returns `α_d : M₁ → M₂`; if `N₂ | N₁`, returns `β_d : M₁ → M₂`. Error if neither divisibility holds. | `α_d` / `β_d` construction. |
| `DegeneracyMatrix(M1, M2, d)` | The matrix of `DegeneracyMap(M1,M2,d)` with respect to `Basis(M1)` and `Basis(M2)`. Both `IsAmbient(M1)` and `IsAmbient(M2)` must be true. | — |
| `ModularSymbols(M, N')` | The level-`N′` space associated to `M` (level `N`). If `N | N′`, returns `Σ_{d|N′/N} α_d(M)`; if `N′ | N`, returns `Σ_{d|N/N′} β_d(M)` (and, if `Conductor(DirichletCharacter(M))` does not divide `N′`, the 0 space). | Image under degeneracy maps. |
| `M1 !! M2` | The subspace of `M₁` associated to `M₂`. With `N₁` the level of `M₁`: if `ModularSymbols(M2,N1)` is defined, let `M₃` be that space; if `M₃ ⊆ M₁`, return `M₃`, else error. | Degeneracy-image subspace. |

*Worked example:* H133E9 (degeneracy maps `α₂` and `β₂` between levels 15 and 30; image of a
level-11 space at levels 33 and 44 via `ModularSymbols(X11,33)` and the `!!` operator).

## 133.7 Decomposition

`Decomposition` and `NewformDecomposition` express a space as a direct sum of Hecke-stable
subspaces. The `Proof` parameter affects internal characteristic-polynomial computations: setting
`Proof := false` is usually safe (if it causes a characteristic-polynomial computation to fail,
the returned dimensions simply sum to less than `dim M`).

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `Decomposition(M, bound : Proof)` | Decomposition of `M` with respect to the Hecke operators `Tₚ` with `p` coprime to the level and `p ≤ bound`. If `bound` is too small, constituents are not guaranteed irreducible. Results are cached, so successive calls give possibly finer decompositions. Parameter `Proof` (default `true`). | Iterated kernel/image splitting under `Tₚ`. |
| `NewformDecomposition(M : Proof)` | Unsorted decomposition of `M` into factors corresponding to the Galois-conjugacy classes of newforms of level some divisor of the level of `M`. Requires `IsCuspidal(M)`. Often significantly faster than `Decomposition`. Parameter `Proof` (default `true`). | Newform / degeneracy-map decomposition. |
| `AssociatedNewSpace(M)` | The space corresponding to the Galois-conjugacy class of newforms associated to `M` (the newform level may be a proper divisor of the level of `M`). `M` must have been created via `NewformDecomposition`. | — |
| `SortDecomposition(D)` | Sort the sequence `D` of spaces with respect to the `lt` comparison operator. | — |
| `IsIrreducible(M)` | Returns `true` iff `Decomposition(M)` has cardinality 1. | — |
| `M1 lt M2` | Ordering on spaces: (1) if both built via `NewformDecomposition` and `Level(AssociatedNewSpace(M1))` differs from that of `M2`, the `M_i` with larger associated level is first; (2) smaller dimension first; (3) for weight 2 trivial character, order by `W_q` eigenvalues starting with the smallest `p | N` (eigenvalue `+1` less than `−1`); (4) order by `abs(trace(a_p))` for `p ∤ N`, positive trace smaller on ties. Rule (3) extends the ordering of (most of) **[Cre97]**. | — |

*Worked example:* H133E10 (`Decomposition` of `ModularSymbols(37,2)`; `IsIrreducible`;
`NewformDecomposition` of the cuspidal subspace; level-90 example showing `Decomposition` not as
fine as possible, refined further; `AssociatedNewSpace` levels dividing 90).

## 133.8 Subspaces

Functions to compute the cuspidal, Eisenstein, and new subspaces, plus the complement of a
subspace.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `CuspidalSubspace(M)` | The cuspidal subspace of `M`; the kernel of `BoundaryMap(M)`. | Kernel of the boundary map. |
| `IsCuspidal(M)` | Returns `true` iff `M` is contained in the cuspidal subspace of the ambient space. | — |
| `EisensteinSubspace(M)` | The Eisenstein subspace of `M`; the complement in `M` of the cuspidal subspace of `M`. | Complement of cuspidal subspace. |
| `IsEisenstein(M)` | Returns `true` iff `M` is contained in the Eisenstein subspace of the ambient space. | — |
| `NewSubspace(M)` | The new subspace of `M`; the intersection of `NewSubspace(M,p)` over all prime divisors `p` of the level. `M` must be cuspidal. | Intersection of `p`-new subspaces. |
| `IsNew(M)` | Returns `true` iff `M` is contained in the new cuspidal subspace of the ambient space. | — |
| `NewSubspace(M, p)` | The `p`-new subspace of `M`; the kernel of the degeneracy map from `M` to the space of level (level of `M`)/`p` with restricted character. If the character does not restrict, `NewSubspace(M,p) = M`. `M` must be cuspidal. | Kernel of a degeneracy map. |
| `Kernel(I, M)` | The kernel of `I` on `M`. With `Tₚ` the `p`th Hecke operator, and `I = [⟨p₁,f₁(x)⟩,…,⟨pₙ,fₙ(x)⟩]` a sequence of (prime, polynomial) pairs: the subspace obtained by intersecting the kernels of `fₙ(T_{pₙ})`. Only primes `pᵢ` not dividing the level are used. | Intersection of kernels of `fₙ(T_{pₙ})`. |
| `Complement(M)` | The space complementary to `M` in the ambient space, so the ambient space equals the direct sum of `M` and `Complement(M)`. | — |
| `BoundaryMap(M)` | A matrix representing the boundary map from `M` to the vector space whose basis consists of the weight-`k` cusps. (There is no intrinsic that lists these cusps.) | Boundary map on modular symbols. |

*Worked examples:* H133E11 (cuspidal/Eisenstein subspaces of `ModularSymbols(11,2)`; `E + C eq M`;
complements); H133E12 (`BoundaryMap` of `"37B"` and its ambient space; Eisenstein subspace not in
the kernel of the boundary map).

## 133.9 Twists

Twists of newforms by Dirichlet characters. A newform is specified by a space of modular symbols
containing a single Galois-orbit of newforms (over `Q` or an extension), obtained via
`NewformDecomposition`. To prove `f₂ = f₁^χ`, the program compares Hecke eigenvalues up to an
appropriate Sturm bound (e.g. Lemma 1.4 of **[BS02]**).

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `IsTwist(M1, M2, p)` | Given spaces `M1`, `M2` specifying newforms `f₁`, `f₂`, and a prime `p`: determines whether some Galois conjugate (over `Q`) of `f₂` is the twist of `f₁` by a nontrivial Dirichlet character of `p`-power conductor; if so, also returns a character `χ` with `f₂ = f₁^χ`. | Compares Hecke eigenvalues up to a Sturm bound **[BS02]**. |
| `IsMinimalTwist(M, p)` | Given a space `M` specifying a newform `f` and a prime `p`: determines whether some Galois conjugate of `f` is a twist of some newform of lower level by a Dirichlet character of `p`-power conductor. If so, returns `false` together with that lower-level newform (as a space) and the Dirichlet character. | Twist search down to lower level **[BS02]**. |

*Worked example:* H133E13 (the unique level-9 weight-4 newform is a twist of itself by a
character of conductor 3; `IsMinimalTwist` returns true).

## 133.10 Operators

Each space `M` comes with a commuting family `T₁, T₂, …` of Hecke operators. `T₁ = 1`; for `n = p`
prime, `Tₚ(x) = [(p 0; 0 1) + Σ_{r mod p} (1 r; 0 p)] x` (first matrix omitted if `p | N`); `Tₘₙ =
TₘTₙ` for coprime `m, n`; and `T_{pʳ} = Tₚ T_{pʳ⁻¹} − ε(p)pᵏ⁻¹ T_{pʳ⁻²}`. Hecke operators are
represented as `n×n` matrices acting from the right with respect to `Basis(M)`.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `HeckeOperator(M, n)` | A matrix representing the `n`th Hecke operator `Tₙ` with respect to `Basis(M)`. | Hecke recursion (above). |
| `HeckePolynomial(M, n)` | The characteristic polynomial of `Tₙ`. For prime `n`, the Deligne bound on eigenvalue sizes is used, so this is frequently much faster than `CharacteristicPolynomial(HeckeOperator(M,n))`. | Deligne-bounded characteristic polynomial. |
| `IntegralHeckeOperator(M, n)` | A matrix representing `Tₙ` with respect to `Basis(Lattice(M))`. | As `HeckeOperator`, in the integral basis. |
| `DualHeckeOperator(M, n)` | A matrix representing `Tₙ` on the dual vector-space representation of `M`. Much more efficient than `HeckeOperator(M,n)` when `dim M` is small relative to `dim AmbientSpace(M)`. Not guaranteed to be the transpose of `HeckeOperator(M,n)` (computed w.r.t. `Basis(DualVectorSpace(M))`). | Action on the dual representation. |
| `AtkinLehner(M, q)` | A matrix representing the `q`th Atkin-Lehner involution `W_q` on `M`, when defined (`M` has trivial character and even weight). For each divisor `q` of `N` with `gcd(q, N/q)=1`, choose `x,y,z,w` with `qxw − (N/q)yz = 1`, set `g = (dx y; Nz qw)`, and define `W_q(x) = g(x)/q^{(k−2)/2}`. Normalised to be an involution when possible (may be impossible if `k > 2` and the characteristic divides `q`). | Atkin-Lehner involution via the matrix `g`. |
| `DualAtkinLehner(M, q)` | The action of the Atkin-Lehner involution on the dual representation of `M`, when defined. | As above, on the dual. |
| `StarInvolution(M)` | The conjugation involution `∗` sending `XⁱYʲ{u,v}` to `(−1)ʲ XⁱYʲ{−u,−v}`. | Direct definition. |
| `DualStarInvolution(M)` | The conjugation involution `∗` on the dual representation of `M`. | As above, on the dual. |
| `ThetaOperator(M1, M2)` | Multiplication by `XᵖY − XYᵖ`, an analogue of the `θ`-operator (on mod-`p` modular forms, `θ : f ↦ q df/dq`). Both `M1`, `M2` must be over a field of positive characteristic `p`, with the same level and character, and `weight(M2) = weight(M1) + p + 1`. | `θ`-analogue via multiplication by `XᵖY − XYᵖ`. |

*Worked examples:* H133E14 (`HeckeOperator(ModularSymbols(12),2)` as a matrix); H133E15
(`HeckeOperator`, `IntegralHeckeOperator`, `DualHeckeOperator` for `"11k4"` `±1` quotients; `∗`
acting as `+1`/`−1`; `AtkinLehner`); H133E16 (`θ`-operator on level-11 modular symbols over
`GF(3)`, verifying the `n`th Hecke eigenvalue of `θ(x)` equals `n·aₙ(f)`).

## 133.11 The Hecke Algebra

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `HeckeBound(M)` | A positive integer `n` such that `T₁,…,Tₙ` generate the Hecke algebra as a `Z`-module. For trivial character the default bound is `(k/12)·[SL₂(Z):Γ₀(N)]` (follows from **[Stu87]**, as explained in **[AS02]**); for nontrivial character the default is twice this, though it is **not known** to be large enough in all such cases — one may wish to increase it via `SetHeckeBound`. | Sturm bound **[Stu87, AS02]**. |
| `SetHeckeBound(M, n)` | Sets the bound `n` used internally for which `T₁,…,Tₙ` generate the Hecke algebra as a `Z`-module. Setting it too low can produce incorrect results. | — |
| `HeckeAlgebra(M : Bound)` | The Hecke algebra `TQ` over `Q` such that `Generators(TQ)` generates `Z[T₁,T₂,…]` as a `Z`-module. If `Bound` is set, only computes the algebra generated by `Tₙ` with `n ≤ Bound`. | — |
| `DiscriminantOfHeckeAlgebra(M : Bound)` | The discriminant of the Hecke algebra associated to `M`. If `Bound` is set, computes the discriminant of the subalgebra generated by `Tₙ`, `n ≤ Bound`. | — |
| `HeckeEigenvalueRing(M : Bound)` | The order generated by the Fourier coefficients of one of the `q`-expansions of a newform corresponding to `M`, plus a map from the coefficient ring of `qExpansion(A)` to the order. If `Bound` is set (default `−1`), only `aₙ` with `n ≤ Bound` are used. | — |
| `HeckeEigenvalueField(M)` | The number field generated by the Fourier coefficients of one of the `q`-expansions of a newform corresponding to `M`, plus a map from the coefficient ring of `qExpansion(M)` to the field. `M` must be defined over `Q`. | — |

*Worked example:* H133E17 (`DiscriminantOfHeckeAlgebra` for prime level 389; 389 is the only
prime `p < 10000` dividing the discriminant of the Hecke algebra of `S₂(Γ₀(p))`).

## 133.12 The Intersection Pairing

Magma can compute the intersection pairing `H₁(X₀(N),Q) × H₁(X₀(N),Q) → Q` on the homology of
`X₀(N)`. The algorithm is essentially that of **[Mer93]**. (Warning: there is a typo in
Proposition 4 of **[Mer93]** — `W_i` should be replaced by `W_i^{ε_i}`.)

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `IntersectionPairing(x, y)` | The intersection pairing of the homology classes corresponding to the weight-2 cuspidal modular symbols `x` and `y`. The symbols must have the same parent, which must have trivial character and not be a `+1` or `−1` quotient. | Merel's algorithm **[Mer93]**. |

*Worked example:* H133E18 (intersection pairing on `H₁(X₀(37),Z)`; the pairing is perfect,
skew-symmetric, determinant `+1`, and compatible with Hecke: `(Tₙx, y) = (x, Tₙy)`).

## 133.13 q-Expansions

The following functions should only be called on cuspidal spaces. For `q`-expansions of
Eisenstein series, use the modular forms functions instead.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `Eigenform(M, prec)` / `Eigenform(M)` / `qEigenform(M, prec)` / `qEigenform(M)` / `PowerSeries(M, prec)` / `PowerSeries(M)` | The `q`-expansion of one of the Galois-conjugate newforms associated to the irreducible cuspidal space `M`, to absolute precision `prec` (defaults to the highest precision computed in previous calls, or 8 if none). Coefficients lie in a quotient of a polynomial extension of the base field of `M`. Usually `M` must have been defined using `NewformDecomposition`. | Newform `q`-expansion via Hecke eigenvalues. |
| `qExpansionBasis(M, prec : Al)` | The reduced row-echelon basis of `q`-expansions for the space of modular forms associated to `M` (over base field `K`), to absolute precision `prec`. Parameter `Al` (default `"Newform"`): `"Newform"` computes a basis by finding a newform decomposition, computing `q`-expansions, and taking images under degeneracy maps; `"Universal"` uses the algorithm of Section 4.3 of **[Mer94]** (no newform decomposition needed, but requires more Hecke operators and is usually less efficient in practice). | Newform algorithm (default) or Merel's universal Fourier expansion **[Mer94]**. |
| `qIntegralBasis(M)` / `qIntegralBasis(M, prec)` / `qIntegralBasis(seq, prec)` | The reduced integral basis of `q`-expansions for the space of modular forms associated to `M` (or to the given sequence of spaces), to absolute precision `prec`. The base field must be the rationals or a cyclotomic field. Parameter `Al` (default `"Newform"`). | Integral reduction of the newform `q`-expansions. |
| `SystemOfEigenvalues(M, prec)` | The sequence of Hecke eigenvalues `[a₂, a₃, a₅, a₇, …, a_p]`, where `p` is the largest prime `≤ prec`. With `K` the base field, the `a_ℓ` lie in `K` or an extension (constructed as a number field or a quotient of `K[x]`). `M` must correspond to a single Galois-conjugacy class of newforms. | Hecke eigenvalues. |

*Worked example:* H133E19 (`q`-basis, `qEigenform`, `PowerSeries`, `SystemOfEigenvalues` for the
`+1` quotient of `S₂(Γ₀(23))`; comparison of rational and integral `q`-expansion bases for
`S₂(Γ₀(65))`; Eisenstein `q`-expansions via `ModularForms`).

## 133.14 Special Values of L-functions

For an irreducible cuspidal space `M` over `Q` corresponding to Galois-conjugate newforms
`f^(1),…,f^(d)` with `f^(d) = Σ aₙ^(d) qⁿ`, the Dirichlet series `L(f^(i), s) = Σ aₙ^(i)/nˢ`
extends uniquely to a holomorphic function on `C`; of particular interest is `L(M, j) = L(f^(1),
j)⋯L(f^(d), j)` for `j ∈ {1,…,k−1}`. The parameter `prec` below is the number of terms of each
`q`-expansion used, not the number of correct decimals; increasing `prec` and comparing answers
gives a heuristic quality estimate.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `LSeries(M, j, prec)` | The special value `L(M, j)`, where `j` is an integer in the critical strip (`1 ≤ j ≤ k−1`). `M` must have sign 0; `prec` is the number of `q`-expansion terms used. | Approximation via `q`-expansions of the conjugate newforms. |
| `LSeriesLeadingCoefficient(M, j, prec)` | The leading coefficient `a_r` of the Taylor expansion of `L(M, s)` about the critical integer `j`, together with the order of vanishing `r` of `L(M, s)` at `s = 1`. | Taylor expansion about `s = 1`. |
| `RealVolume(M, prec)` | The volume of `A_M(R)`. With `S ⊂ C[[q]]` the cusp forms associated to `M` and a basis `f₁,…,f_d` of `S ∩ Z[[q]]`, the period map `Φ` sends `x ∈ H` to `(⟨f₁,x⟩,…,⟨f_d,x⟩) ∈ Cᵈ`; the cokernel of `Φ` is `A_M(C)`, and the standard measure on `Cᵈ` induces the measure used. See Section 3.12.16 of **[Ste00]**. | Period-map volume **[Ste00]**. |
| `MinusVolume(M, prec)` | The volume of the subgroup of `A_M(C)` on which complex conjugation acts as `−1`. | As above, minus part. |
| `LRatio(M, j : Bound)` | The rational number `L(A,j)·(j−1)! / ((2π)^{j−1}·Ω)`, where `1 ≤ j ≤ k−1` and `Ω` is `RealVolume(M)` when `j` is odd and `MinusVolume(M)` when `j` is even. If `Bound` is set (default `−1`), `LRatio` is only a divisibility upper bound. If `Sign(M)` is not 0, the result is only correct up to a power of 2. | Rational period ratio. |
| `LRatioOddPart(M, j)` | The odd part of `LRatio(M, j)` (hopefully faster to compute than the full odd part of `LRatio(M, j)`). | Odd part of the period ratio. |

*Worked example:* H133E20 (`LSeries` of the cuspidal subspace of `ModularSymbols(11,2)`; `LSeries`
of `"65B"`, a dimension-two abelian variety).

### 133.14.1 Winding Elements

For a space `Mₖ(N)` over `Q` and `i = 1,…,k`, the `i`th *winding element* is `e_i =
X^{i−1}Y^{k−2−(i−1)}{0,∞} ∈ Mₖ(N)`, of importance for special values: for a newform `f ∈ Sₖ(N)`
corresponding to `M`, and `j ∈ {0,…,k−1}`, `L(f, j) = ((2π)^{j−1}/(i^{j+1}(j−1)!)) · ⟨f,
X^{j−1}Y^{k−2−(j−1)}{0,∞}⟩`. The winding submodule appears in the formula for the canonical
rational part of `L(M, j)` (see `LRatio`).

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `WindingElement(M)` | The winding element `Y^{k−2}{0,∞}`. | Direct definition. |
| `WindingElement(M, i)` | The winding element `X^{i−1}Y^{k−2−(i−1)}{0,∞}`. | Direct definition. |
| `TwistedWindingElement(M, i, eps)` | The element `Σ_{a ∈ (Z/mZ)*} ε(a) X^{i−1}Y^{k−2−(i−1)}{0, a/m}`. | Direct definition. |
| `WindingLattice(M, j : Bound)` | The image under `RationalMapping(M)` of the lattice generated by the images of the `j`th winding element under all Hecke operators `Tₙ`. If `M` is the ambient space, the image under `RationalMapping(M)` is not taken. Parameter `Bound` (default `−1`). | Hecke-image lattice of the winding element. |
| `WindingSubmodule(M, j : Bound)` | The image under `RationalMapping(M)` of the vector space generated by all images of `WindingElement(M,j)` under all Hecke operators `Tₙ`. If `M` is the ambient space, the rational period mapping is not applied. Parameter `Bound` (default `−1`). | Hecke-image submodule of the winding element. |
| `TwistedWindingSubmodule(M, j, eps)` | The Hecke submodule of `Φ(M)` generated by the image of the `j`th `ε`-twisted modular winding element, where `Φ = RationalMapping(M)`. In characteristic 0, if `M` is new of weight 2, sign `+1` or `−1`, corresponding to `{f_i}` Galois-conjugate newforms, the dimension of the twisted winding submodule equals the number of `f_i` with `L(f_i, eps, 1) ≠ 0`. | Hecke-image submodule of the twisted winding element. |

## 133.15 The Associated Complex Torus

For a space `M` of cuspidal modular symbols (the kernel of an ideal in the Hecke algebra) there
is a complex torus `A_M(C)` attached to `M` (an abelian variety `A_M` when `M` has weight 2).
With `S` the corresponding space of modular forms, the integration pairing gives a natural map
`M → Hom(S, C)` whose cokernel is `A_M(C)`.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `SubgroupOfTorus(M, x)` | The cyclic subgroup of the complex torus attached to `M` generated by the image under the period map of the modular symbol `x`. | Image under the period map. |
| `SubgroupOfTorus(M, s)` | An abelian group isomorphic to the finite group generated by the images `π(s[i])` in the complex torus attached to `M`, where `π = PeriodMapping(M)`. | Image under the period map. |
| `ModularKernel(M)` | The kernel of the modular isogeny. With `T` the complex torus attached to `M`, the modular isogeny is the natural map from the dual of `T` into `T` induced by autoduality of `CuspidalSubspace(AmbientSpace(M))`. | Modular isogeny kernel. |
| `CongruenceGroup(M : Bound)` | The congruence group `S/(V+W)` of cusp forms corresponding to `M`, where `S = Sₖ(Γ₀(N),Z)`, `V` is the sub-`Z`-module for `M`, and `W` its orthogonal complement. Encodes congruences between forms in `V` and its complement. Parameter `Bound` (default `−1` ⇒ `HeckeBound(M)+1`, a provably correct answer); if too small, only an upper bound. | Congruence-module quotient. |
| `IntersectionGroup(M1, M2)` | An abelian group encoding the intersection of the complex tori for `M1`, `M2` (which must share an ambient space). When finite, it is isomorphic to `A_{M1}(C) ∩ A_{M2}(C)`. | Intersection of period lattices. |
| `IntersectionGroup(S)` | An abelian group encoding the intersection of the collection of complex tori for the sequence `S` of spaces. | As above. |

*Worked examples:* H133E21 (cuspidal subgroup of `J₀(N)` via `SubgroupOfTorus`, levels 20, 100,
77, 97; Ogg's `Numerator((p−1)/12)`); H133E22 (a function/procedure tabulating the cuspidal
subgroup of `J₀(N) = Jac(X₀(N))` generated by cusps `(α)−(∞)`); H133E23 (a 2-dimensional abelian
variety `B`, a quotient of `J₀(43)`; BSD plus Manin `c=1` implying first nontrivial Sha of order
2; `LRatio`, `TorsionBound`, `TamagawaNumber`, `Periods`, `IntersectionGroup`,
`MordellWeilGroup`; cf. **[PS99, CM00]**); H133E25 (congruence modulus vs modular degree, `54B`
counterexample; `IntersectionGroup`); H133E26 (component groups).

### 133.15.1 The Period Map

For a space `M` corresponding to a Galois-conjugacy class of newforms, the *period map* is a
linear map `AmbientSpace(M) → Cᵈ` (`d = dim` of the associated modular forms space); its cokernel
is `A_M(C)`. In the functions below `M` must not be a `±1` quotient and must be cuspidal.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `PeriodMapping(M, prec)` | The period mapping attached to `M`, computed using `prec` terms of the `q`-expansions of the modular forms associated to `M`. | Integration pairing via `q`-expansions. |
| `Periods(M, prec)` | The complex period lattice associated to `M`, using `prec` terms of the `q`-expansions. | As above. |
| `ClassicalPeriod(M, j, prec)` | The value `r_j(f) = ∫₀^{i∞} f(z) zʲ dz`. | Numerical period integral. |

### 133.15.2 Projection Mappings

For a space `M` over a field `K`, a surjective map `π : AmbientSpace(M) → V` with `ker(π)` equal
to the kernel of the period mapping is useful for many purposes.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `RationalMapping(M)` | A surjective linear map from the ambient space of `M` to a vector space whose kernel equals the kernel of the period mapping. | — |
| `IntegralMapping(M)` | A surjective linear map from the ambient space of `M` to a vector space, kernel equal to the kernel of the period mapping, chosen so that the image of `IntegralBasis(CuspidalSubspace(AmbientSpace(M)))` is the standard `Z`-lattice. `M` must be defined over `Q`. | Integral normalisation of the rational mapping. |

*Worked example:* H133E24 (`RationalMapping`/`IntegralMapping` at level 33; the image of the
integral basis is normalised to `Z×Z`; image of `(0)−(∞) ∈ J₀(N)(Q)` at level 97 has order
`Numerator((97−1)/12) = 8` by Abel-Jacobi).

## 133.16 Modular Abelian Varieties

For a weight-2 cuspidal space `M` with trivial character corresponding to a Galois-conjugacy
class of newforms, `A_M(C)` is the set of complex points of an abelian variety `A_M` over `Q`
(Shimura). With `J₀(N)` the Jacobian of `X₀(N)` and `I` the annihilator of `M` in the Hecke
algebra, `A_M = J₀(N)/I J₀(N)`. Magma computes the modular degree, congruence modulus, bounds on
torsion, and component-group orders at primes exactly dividing the level.

### 133.16.1 Modular Degree and Torsion

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `ModularDegree(M)` | The modular degree of `M`; the square root of `#ModularKernel(M)` (`M` weight 2, trivial character). When `M` corresponds to an elliptic curve `E = A_M`, this is the degree of the induced map `X₀(N) → E`. | `sqrt(#ModularKernel(M))`. |
| `CongruenceModulus(M : Bound)` | The congruence number `r`; the index in `Sₖ(Γ₀(N),Z)` of the sum `L+W` of the lattice `W` of cusp forms for `M` and the complementary lattice `L`. Parameter `Bound` (default `−1`). | Index of `L+W`. |
| `TorsionBound(M, maxp)` | An upper bound `gcd{#A(F_p) : 3 ≤ p ≤ maxp, p ∤ N}` on the order of the torsion subgroup of the abelian variety `A` attached to `M` (an isogeny invariant, hence also a bound for the dual `A^∨`). For a lower bound, use `#SubgroupOfTorus(M,WindingElement(M))`. | gcd of `#A(F_p)` over good primes. |

*Worked example:* H133E25 (congruence modulus vs modular degree for curves of conductor near 54;
the `54B` counterexample; cf. **[FM99]**; the dimension-24 counterexample `"431F"`; congruence-
modulus/modular-degree table for conductors 53–55).

### 133.16.2 Tamagawa Numbers and Orders of Component Groups

Functions for the orders of component groups of optimal quotients of `J₀(N)` at primes `p` that
exactly divide `N`. The algorithm involves Grothendieck's monodromy pairing on the character
group of the toric part of the closed fiber at `p` of the Néron model of `J₀(N)`; the theory is
described in **[Ste01]** (or **[Ste00]**), with a computationally-oriented introduction in
**[KS00]**. For prime `N`, the Mestre-Oesterlé method constructs the character group of the torus
as in **[Mes86]**; in general the ideal theory of quaternion algebras is used. (Note: the appendix
to **[Maz77]** gives an explicit formula for the order of the component group of `J₀(N)` at
primes `p ≥ 5` exactly dividing `N`, but this formula is not currently used by
`ComponentGroupOrder`.) `RealTamagawaNumber` computes the order of the "component group at
infinity".

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `ComponentGroupOrder(M, p)` | The order of the component group at `p`; the order of the group of `F̄_p`-points of the component group of the reduction mod `p` of the Néron model of the abelian variety attached to `M`. `p` must exactly divide the level. If `Sign(M)` is not 0, only the odd part is returned. | Monodromy pairing on the character group **[Ste01, KS00, Mes86]**. |
| `TamagawaNumber(M, p)` | The order of the group of `F_p`-rational points of the component group of `M`. `M` must be associated to a single Galois-conjugacy class of newforms. | As above (`F_p`-points). |
| `RealTamagawaNumber(M)` | The number of connected components of `A_M(R)`. | Real period-lattice analysis. |
| `MinusTamagawaNumber(M)` | The number of connected components of the subgroup `A_M(C)⁻` of `A_M(C)` on which complex conjugation acts as `−1`. | As above, minus part. |

*Worked example:* H133E26 (`ComponentGroupOrder`, `TamagawaNumber`, `RealTamagawaNumber`,
`MinusTamagawaNumber` for `X₀(11)`, level 37, and Jacobians `65A`/`65B` of **[FLS+01]**; when
`W_p` acts as `+1`, the component-group order can exceed the Tamagawa number; rectangular period
lattice of `Δ`).

## 133.17 Elliptic Curves

By the modularity theorem (Breuil, Conrad, Diamond, Taylor, Wiles), for an elliptic curve `E` of
conductor `N` there is a two-dimensional space `M` of modular symbols attached to `E`, obtained
from `ModularSymbols(N,2)` by intersecting the kernels of `Tₚ − a_p(E)` for sufficiently many
`p`. **Warning:** computing `M` can already be very resource-intensive when `Conductor(E)` is on
the order of 5000.

| Intrinsic | Description | Algorithm |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| `ModularSymbols(E)` / `ModularSymbols(E, sign)` | The space `M` of modular symbols associated to the elliptic curve `E` (with given `sign`). | Intersect kernels of `Tₚ − a_p(E)`. |
| `EllipticCurve(M : Database)` | An elliptic curve over `Q` in the isogeny class associated to `M`. Parameter `Database` (default `true`): uses the Cremona database; set `false` to compute from scratch (not optimized for large level). | Database lookup or direct construction. |
| `pAdicLSeries(E, p : CoefficientPrecision, SeriesPrecision, QuadraticTwist, ProperScaling)` | Given an elliptic curve over `Q` and a prime `p` of multiplicative or good ordinary reduction, computes the `p`-adic `L`-series. `E` must be a global minimal model. Parameters: `CoefficientPrecision` (default 3), `SeriesPrecision` (default 5), `QuadraticTwist` (default 1; computes the series for the twist by a fundamental discriminant `D` coprime to the conductor), `ProperScaling` (default `true`; ensures correct scaling of the `p`-adic `L`-series). | Computes modular symbols (`~N³` time) and evaluates `~2(p−1)pⁿ` of them. | 

*Worked example:* H133E27 (`389A`, the rank-2 curve of smallest conductor; numerical BSD-
conjectural order of Sha via `LRatio`, `LSeriesLeadingCoefficient`, `Periods`, `Regulator`,
`TorsionSubgroup`, `TamagawaNumber`, and `pAdicLSeries`).
